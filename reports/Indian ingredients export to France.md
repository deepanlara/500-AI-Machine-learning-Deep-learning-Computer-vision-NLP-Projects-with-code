# Start as agent, sell turmeric first

The quickest, lowest-risk way into France is **not** to import containers yourself on day one. Instead, register as a **commercial agent** (agent commercial, as a micro-entrepreneur) and earn commission while your brother's company invoices French and EU buyers directly. Lead with the products where India is the obvious origin and the rules are manageable: **turmeric and curcumin** (India supplied **79% of EU turmeric imports in 2023**), **cosmetic-grade castor oil** (India supplies roughly **95% of EU castor oil imports**), and the cosmetic and DIY botanicals (moringa, henna, multani mitti, amla). The French supplement market passed **€3bn in 2025** and France is the **world's largest cosmetics exporter**, so demand exists. What decides success is **documentation and compliance**, not price. Indian spices are still the EU's most-flagged origin for ethylene oxide, several Indian spices face extra border checks, and ashwagandha and high-dose curcumin are under active safety scrutiny in France. The brochure's market statistics are mostly real but **date from 2022, are partly unverifiable, and in two places are wrong**. They need fixing before any professional buyer sees them, and so does the website, because **ladolceindia.com did not resolve at all** during this research. The EU-India free trade agreement was concluded on 27 January 2026 but is **not in force**: signing is targeted for December 2026, so do not promise "duty-free" yet. Most spices already enter at 0% anyway. Begin with an agent contract, a clean document pack and a website that works, then use SIAL Paris (17-21 October 2026) and Fi Europe (17-19 November 2026) to start building a pipeline. Move to importing in your own name (a SASU) only once repeat orders justify stock, working capital and the legal liability of being the "food business operator."

A note on method: the research team worked from web search extracts because several official databases (Eurostat Comext, Access2Markets/TARIC, EUR-Lex full texts) could not be opened directly. Every tariff rate and every regulatory annex entry below should be re-checked on the official site before you put it in a contract or brochure. Where something is uncertain, this report says so.

## The brochure's numbers are real but stale, and two claims are wrong

Professional buyers in France check numbers. An old or unlabelled statistic can cost you credibility in the first meeting, so this is the first thing to fix.

The turmeric and ginger figures come from Eurostat's December 2023 release, which covers **2022** data. In 2022 the EU imported **15,800 t of turmeric, 77% from India**, and **122,900 t of ginger, 54% from China**. Between 2012 and 2022, turmeric imports rose 142% and ginger 149% ([Eurostat, Dec 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1)). So the brochure's "fastest growing spice" claim for turmeric is **wrong on Eurostat's own numbers**: ginger grew faster. The 2023 data has been published since. Turmeric rose to **16,000 t with India at 79%**, while ginger fell to about **114,000 t with China down to 43%** ([Eurostat, Dec 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1); [Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/12/02/ginger-most-imported-spice-in-eu-for-2023/)). A trade source puts 2024 EU turmeric imports at **17,197 t, worth roughly €42 million**, with India at about 73% by value in 2025 ([Mundus Agri](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/spices-eu-spends.n36669.html)). The brochure's "€39.9M" and "18,136 MT" figures could not be traced to any source. They may refer to a different year or to Europe including the UK. Either way, the brochure quotes two different volumes for the same product without saying which year or geography each covers.

The ginger headline also misleads in a subtler way. Eurostat's 114,000-123,000 t is mostly **fresh** ginger, where China dominates. An Indian exporter of dried ginger or ginger powder competes in a much smaller pool. CBI estimates European imports of dried ginger from developing countries at about **18,000 t in 2024**, of which India supplied only about **1,810 t (around 10%)**. CBI also says dried-ginger imports peaked in 2022 and then fell ([CBI dried ginger](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/dried-ginger/market-potential)).

The castor oil numbers are harder to verify. The brochure says EU imports are **$308.7M / 151,559 t**. WITS/Comtrade shows **$260.5M / 152,300 t in 2023** ([WITS 2023](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/ALL/year/2023/tradeflow/Imports/partner/WLD/product/151530)), and another source gives **$264.4M / 162,870 t for 2024** ([Tradologie](https://www.tradologie.com/blogs/why-castor-oil-export-from-india-holds-global-importance/)). The tonnage is about right but the value looks high, perhaps from the 2022 price spike. EU imports from India were **$251.8M in 2024** ([WITS 2024](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/All/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/IND/product/151530)). Combining the two 2024 sources gives India a share of about 95%, so the brochure's 97.3% is the right order of magnitude. The claim that India produces "more than 90% of world castor" is slightly overstated: official Indian data gives **89.8% in 2020** ([DES Agri/AERC](https://desagri.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2021-22-Castor-Crop-Cultivation-in-Gujarat.pdf)), so "about 85-90%" is defensible. The ranking of top EU buyers is also wrong. In 2022 the **Netherlands ($173M) led, ahead of France ($136M) and Germany ($86.4M)** ([OEC](https://oec.world/en/profile/hs/castor-oil-or-fractions-not-chemically-modified)). France's imports then fell to about $100M in 2023 ([TrendEconomy](https://trendeconomy.com/data/h2/France/151530)), so "$90.3M" may be a 2024 figure, but that is unconfirmed. Finally, the claim that 30-35% of Indian castor exports go to the EU looks overstated. EU purchases of about $252M against India's roughly $1bn of castor oil exports gives closer to **25%**, and China is consistently named as the largest single buyer ([Seair](https://www.seair.co.in/blog/castor-oil-export-from-india.aspx)).

| Brochure claim | Verdict | What to print instead |
|---|---|---|
| Turmeric 15,800 t, India 77% | Accurate for 2022, outdated | "16,000 t in 2023, 79% from India (Eurostat)" |
| Turmeric €39.9M / 18,136 t | Unverifiable | Drop, or re-pull from Comext with year and geography stated |
| Turmeric "+142%, fastest growing" | Misleading: ginger grew faster (+149%) | "+142% 2012-2022" without "fastest" |
| Ginger 122,900 t, 54% China | Outdated | "~114,000 t in 2023, 43% China"; for India, cite the dried-ginger segment |
| Castor $308.7M / 151,559 t | Volume plausible, value high | "~$260M / ~152,000 t (2023, Comtrade)" |
| India 97.3% of EU castor | Plausible (~95%) | "~95% (2024)" |
| India >90% of world castor | Slightly overstated | "~85-90%" |
| France $90.3M | Unconfirmed year | Label the year, or use "France: 2nd-largest EU importer" |
| Top buyers France, Germany, Netherlands | Wrong order | "Netherlands, France, Germany" |
| 30-35% of castor exports to EU | Likely overstated | "~25% of castor oil exports" |

Practical fix: ask your brother to re-pull every figure from Eurostat Comext or ITC Trade Map and add a year and a source line under each number. French buyers forgive modest numbers far more readily than unlabelled ones.

## Turmeric and castor are India's natural wins; ginger, cardamom and chilli need a reason to switch

For most of the range, India is a minor supplier to Europe. Market share decides which products are easy to sell and which need a special angle.

**India is the default origin** for turmeric (79% of EU volume) and castor oil (about 95%). **India is a challenger** in ginger, where China dominates. In cardamom, Guatemala dominates: India's shipments to the EU rose 63% in 2024 but were still only about **6% of EU volume** ([Mundus Agri](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/cardamom-slightly-bumpy-ride-europe.n34678.html); [CBI cardamom](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/cardamom-0/market-potential)). In cinnamon, **Vietnam leads with 35%**. In chilli and paprika, **China has 73%** ([Eurostat 2023 data](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1)). Overall EU spice imports from outside the EU were **339,800 t in 2023**, with China the largest supplier at 39% ([Efanews](https://www.efanews.eu/item/46039-eu-eurostat-spice-imports-at-339-800-tons-in-2023.html)). CBI lists France among Europe's most attractive turmeric markets, alongside the UK, the Netherlands, Germany, Spain and Poland, and says most turmeric goes to the food industry ([CBI turmeric](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-health-products/turmeric/market-potential)). To sell ginger, cardamom or chilli, you need a reason for the buyer to switch. Typical reasons are organic certification, a named variety (Kerala ginger, Kashmiri or Byadgi chilli), or clean residue results.

The French demand picture favours health and beauty over ethnic food. The **food supplement market topped €3bn in 2025** (+2.6%), with **pharmacies taking about 55% of sales**, organic stores about 8% and distance selling more than 10%. About 70% of the market is vitality/immunity, stress/sleep and respiratory products, and Deloitte forecasts €4bn by 2030 ([Le Moniteur des Pharmacies](https://www.lemoniteurdespharmacies.fr/business/retail/officine/complements-alimentaires-un-marche-a-3-milliards-deuros); [Synadiet](https://www.synadiet.org/les-complements-alimentaires/leur-consommation/)). That suits turmeric, moringa and ashwagandha, subject to the safety caveats below. On the cosmetics side, France has about **6,300 cosmetics establishments**, exported **€22.4bn in 2025**, and is the world's number-one exporter with a 14% share. The Cosmetic Valley cluster has 660 members, 80% of them SMEs ([Cosmetic Valley](https://www.cosmetic-valley.com/en/chiffres-cles)). The Indian diaspora, by contrast, is small: India's foreign ministry counts about **119,000 overseas Indians in metropolitan France** ([Wikipedia citing MEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_France)). One commercial dataset counts about **1,123 Indian restaurants in France, 506 of them in Île-de-France** ([Rentech Digital](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-indian-restaurants-in-france)). The ethnic channel is real and close to you, but it is a niche, not the main prize.

Putting market share, demand and regulatory difficulty together gives a clear priority order:

| Priority | Products | Why | Best buyers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 – lead with these** | Turmeric powder/fingers, cosmetic castor oil (cold-pressed, ideally organic and hexane-free), henna (pure Lawsonia leaf), multani mitti, moringa leaf powder, amla powder | India is the natural origin; rules manageable with good paperwork; strong French DIY and natural-cosmetics demand | Spice packers, DIY cosmetics retailers, indie natural brands, ingredient distributors |
| **2 – sell with a specific angle** | Ginger (dried/powder), cardamom, cinnamon, chilli, curry leaf, garam masala, tamarind, moringa seed oil, black cumin seed oil, triphala | India is a minor supplier, or extra border checks apply, or EtO history (garam masala, chilli) | Organic packers, ethnic wholesalers, restaurants via a stock-holding importer |
| **3 – handle with care, verify first** | Curcumin 95%, ashwagandha, shilajit, noni, moringa extract | Novel Food questions, ANSES safety opinions, national bans, heavy-metal risk | Only established supplement brands with their own regulatory teams, after the checks below |

## Compliance decides every sale: border checks, ethylene oxide and contaminants

Under EU law, whoever imports into France and places the goods on the market is the **food business operator (FBO)** and is legally responsible for compliance. That means you if you import, or your buyer if you act only as an agent. In France, food safety controls have been consolidated under the Ministry of Agriculture's DGAL, which works through the departmental DDPP offices. Some DDPPs have dedicated port-import units ([DDPP Hérault](https://www.herault.gouv.fr/contenu/telechargement/49421/368950/file/Bilan%20activit%C3%A9%20DDPP%202023.pdf)). The research did not find a separate "importer registration" step for non-animal foods such as spices. Call your local DDPP (Hauts-de-Seine, Val-de-Marne, Seine-Saint-Denis or wherever you are based) before your first import to confirm what registration they expect.

### Several Indian spices face extra checks at the border

EU Regulation 2019/1793 lists foods from specific countries that get increased checks at the border, and it is amended roughly every six months. The latest confirmed amendment, **Reg (EU) 2026/194 of 28 January 2026**, made three changes ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R0194)):

- It **ended ethylene oxide (EtO) checks** on Indian okra, vanilla and cloves, because compliance had become "satisfactory".
- It moved Indian **pepper, chilli/Capsicum and Pimenta (heading 0904)** out of the stricter Annex II, which requires an official certificate, into **Annex I at a 20% check rate** ([EUR-Lex HTML](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202600194)).
- **Indian cumin** was already raised to **30%** checks for pesticide residues under Reg 2024/3153 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202403153)).

AGRINFO describes a **June 2026 update** that moved Indian cinnamon into Annex I for EtO and removed Indian Capsicum from that list ([AGRINFO](https://agrinfo.eu/book-of-reports/temporary-increased-official-controls-on-foods-from-certain-countries-june-2026-update/)). A trade blog also says turmeric remains in Annex II at 20% ([Spicemodo](https://spicemodo.com/blog/eu-spice-import-rules-2026.html)), but that regulation could not be found on EUR-Lex, and a 2022 report said Indian turmeric had been excluded from EtO controls ([Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2022/07/eu-import-control-changes-mention-ethylene-oxide-and-aflatoxins/)). **Turmeric's current status is therefore unresolved.** Before every first shipment of a product, search EUR-Lex for the consolidated text "02019R1793" and check the India entries. Curry leaves were listed for pesticides at 10% in the original 2019 annex ([Reg 2019/1793 copy](https://www.shefexil.org/upload/EU-Commission-Regulation-1793%20dtd.pdf)). The 50% curry leaf rate you may see quoted online is a **UK** rule, not an EU one ([Senedd](https://senedd.wales/media/cakk013r/sub-ld16820-e.pdf)).

For a listed product, the shipment is pre-notified in the EU's TRACES system with a CHED-D document. It must enter through a designated border control post, and it may be held until lab results come back. The importer pays for the controls. In practice your customs broker handles the filing, but you have to budget for the delay and the testing cost.

### Ethylene oxide is the reputational landmine

EtO is a fumigant that is not approved in the EU, and EU authorities treat it with zero tolerance. EtO alerts in the EU's RASFF system fell from **345 in 2021 to about 25 in the most recent 12 months**, but **India is still the largest origin: 85 of 144 notifications since 2023**. Recent alerts include Indian garam masala, biryani masala, chilli powder, black pepper and cumin ([third-party RASFF analysis](https://rasff.fruit-risk.com/findings/ethylene-oxide-five-years-on.html)). The 2024 MDH/Everest affair, in which Singapore and Hong Kong withdrew curry powders over EtO, hit the reputation of all Indian spices, even though no EU notification named those brands ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDH_and_Everest_global_safety_concerns)). Across all hazards, RASFF recorded **279 spice and herb notifications in 2025 versus 275 in 2024**, with pesticide residues the most common problem ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/buyer-requirements)). The practical rule is that **every lot is steam-sterilised, never EtO-fumigated, and tested for EtO/2-chloroethanol by an EU-accredited lab**. That test report goes in every sales pack. As the CBI-based research put it, independent EU lab testing is a **precondition, not a differentiator**.

### Contaminant limits to test against

EU Regulation 2023/915 sets the maximum contaminant levels. Ochratoxin A is capped at **15 µg/kg in most dried spices** ([consolidated Reg 2023/915](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02023R0915-20250101); [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/eu-regulations-on-food-contaminants/)). There are also aflatoxin limits for spices, with exceptions for cardamom and smoked Capsicum ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/915/oj/eng)). Pesticide residues are measured against the EU MRL regulation, and where no specific limit exists the default is very low. Your buyer will expect a test panel per lot:

- multi-residue pesticides
- EtO/2-chloroethanol
- aflatoxins and ochratoxin A
- heavy metals (lead especially, critical for turmeric, shilajit and triphala)
- Salmonella and general microbiology
- Sudan dyes, for chilli and turmeric (an old EU concern with Indian origin)

## Ashwagandha, curcumin 95% and shilajit carry the most regulatory risk

The Ayurvedic part of the range is where French rules bite hardest, and they bite at the **supplement** stage, which is your buyer's responsibility but your sales problem.

**Ashwagandha.** France allows ashwagandha in supplements. ANSES (opinion 2021-SA-0077, 2024) advised against use by **pregnant or breastfeeding women, under-18s, people with thyroid, liver or heart conditions, and people on sedatives**, after eight adverse-effect reports. It did not ban the plant ([Nutriradio](https://nutriradio.fr/news/prudence-recommandee-par-l-anses-sur-l-utilisation-de-l-ashwagandha-538); [La Compagnie des Sens](https://www.compagnie-des-sens.fr/ashwagandha-interdit-france/)). Other countries are stricter. **Denmark banned ashwagandha from April 2023** ([Food Law Consult](https://foodlawconsult.com/2023/07/18/ashwaganda-prohibited/)). **Belgium treats it as medicinal**, and the EU logged **26 border rejections of ashwagandha supplements in 2021-2024** ([Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/ashwagandha-safety-questioned-in-europe-and-india/)). Poland allows only the root, with dose limits ([Sagentia](https://sagentia.com/blog/navigating-ashwagandha-balancing-safety-consumer-demand-and-regulatory-concerns/)). As of a **14 July 2026** meeting, EU regulators were still considering a Europe-wide safety review, which could end in restrictions ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/16/ashwagandha-armed-with-critical-safety-data-as-industry-prepares-for-europe-wide-assessment/)). Sources disagree on the leaf. One says all plant parts are "not novel" in supplements ([Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/ashwagandha-safety-questioned-in-europe-and-india/)), while another says the leaf cannot be sold as food ([Manubolu](https://manubolu.de/blog/novel-food-regulation-eu.html)). The safe position is to offer **root-only** material with full identity documentation (botanical name, plant part, extraction solvent, withanolide profile), never sell into Denmark, and expect French brands to put warnings on their labels. In cosmetics, ashwagandha root extract appears in the EU ingredient inventory with no restriction found ([cosingchecker](https://cosingchecker.com/ingredients/60191-withania-somnifera-root-extract)).

**Turmeric and curcumin 95%.** France's ANSES recorded **15 liver adverse-effect cases** linked to turmeric/curcumin supplements, and Italy recorded about 20 hepatitis cases in 2018-2019 ([Vigil'Anses n°17](https://vigilanses.anses.fr/sites/default/files/VigilAnsesN17_Juin2022_Curcuma_0.pdf)). ANSES recommends keeping curcumin from supplements **below 153 mg/day** for a 60 kg adult and flags "enhanced bioavailability" formulations ([Hépatites Info Service](https://www.hepatites-info-service.org/curcuma-et-hepatites-vigilance/)). Italy's health ministry is reported to treat turmeric extracts with up to **95% curcumin as Novel Food** ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/13/European-food-safety-report-taking-pot-shots-at-popular-botanicals/)). Curcumin 95% will still sell, but only to supplement makers who do their own regulatory work. Plain turmeric powder for the food industry is the far easier product.

**Shilajit, moringa and noni.** The European Commission concluded that shilajit (mumijo) is **not novel in food supplements but may be novel in other foods** ([EC consultation](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/f551e7aa-8128-49bc-be3e-36e35b82eae0_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_mumijo.pdf)). So it can be sold as a supplement ingredient but not, for example, as a food powder, and it needs strict heavy-metal testing. A German importer guide reports moringa **leaf** as not novel ([Manubolu](https://manubolu.de/blog/novel-food-regulation-eu.html)). Moringa seed products, noni (only certain forms are authorised) and black cumin seed oil in food use should each be checked against the [EU Novel Food Catalogue](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/novel-food-status-catalogue_en) before you quote them to a food or supplement buyer. Remember that the catalogue is non-binding: the seller must be able to prove the product was significantly consumed in the EU before 15 May 1997.

**How French supplement rules work.** France keeps a positive list of plants allowed in supplements, the Arrêté of 24 June 2014 ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000029254516)). Each finished supplement must be declared to DGAL on the **Compl'Alim** platform, which took over from DGCCRF's Téléicare in September 2025 ([data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/declarations-de-complements-alimentaires)). DGAL has two months to examine a declaration. A product containing a plant not on the French list can still be declared through "mutual recognition" if it is legal elsewhere in the EU ([Synadiet](https://www.synadiet.org/les-complements-alimentaires/reglementation/)). All of this is the **brand's** job, but buyers will ask whether each plant and plant part is on the French list, so have the answer ready.

## Organic, labelling, cosmetics and REACH: the paperwork that wins buyers

### Organic certification needs checking shipment by shipment

The EU has repeatedly delisted Indian organic certifiers over EtO-contaminated consignments:

- **Reg 2021/2325** removed CU Inspections India, Ecocert India, Indocert, Lacon and OneCert ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/2325/oj)).
- **Reg 2022/2049** removed TQ Cert Services ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2022/2049/oj/eng)).
- **Reg 2025/883 (May 2025)** recorded the suspension of the Sikkim agency and Reliable Organic, and added new bodies including ISCOP, CGOCERT and CERT ID India ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/883/oj/eng)).

So "organic" from India is only worth what the certifier's EU recognition is worth **on the date of shipment**. Each organic consignment also needs an electronic Certificate of Inspection in TRACES. The transition rules for third-country recognitions under the new EU organic regulation (2018/848) are moving, and the researchers could not confirm India's status after end-2026. Ask your brother's certifier, in writing, whether their EU recognition covers shipments arriving in 2027. If you import organic goods yourself, you also need your own certification with a French organic control body.

### Food labelling basics

French law requires product information in **French** for anything sold in France. Supplements must carry the term "complément alimentaire", the daily dose, a warning not to exceed it, a keep-out-of-reach-of-children warning, and the statement that supplements do not replace a varied diet ([entreprises.gouv.fr](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/espace-entreprises/s-informer-sur-la-reglementation/complements-alimentaires)). Three traps catch spice sellers repeatedly:

- **Undeclared mustard or celery** in garam masala or curry blends. Both are EU allergens.
- **Missing lot numbers.**
- **Health claims.** Only claims on the EU's authorised list are allowed. Botanical claims such as "reduces stress" or "anti-inflammatory" are legally grey, and anything that sounds like treating a disease can get the product reclassified as an unauthorised medicine.

Bulk B2B deliveries can carry the required particulars on the commercial documents instead of the bag. Retail packs cannot.

### Cosmetics: you supply the data, the brand carries the liability

The EU Cosmetics Regulation puts legal duties on the brand's "Responsible Person", not on the raw-material supplier. But that brand needs a full ingredient dossier to write its safety report, so **documentation quality is what wins cosmetic business** ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1382.pdf)). CBI's standard list is a technical data sheet, safety data sheet, certificate of analysis per batch, allergen declaration, traceability and certifications ([CBI essential oils](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/essential-oils/market-entry)). For oils, buyers also expect the fatty-acid profile and peroxide and acid values on the COA ([CBI vegetable oils](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/market-information/buyer-requirements-europe-vegetable-oils-2016.pdf)). Aroma-Zone, for example, makes suppliers sign a charter and be assessed by EcoVadis ([Aroma-Zone](https://www.aroma-zone.com/content/notre-demarche-achat-responsable)).

**Henna** needs special care. The EU scientific committee (SCCS, 2013) found henna powder safe **as a hair dye** at a defined preparation (100 g powder to 300 ml boiling water), but its opinion **does not cover skin/body-art use** ([EC SCCS](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/opinion-lawsonia-inermis-henna-c169_en)). "Black henna" containing PPD is illegal on skin. An Indian black-henna hair dye with PPD above the 2% limit has already triggered an EU Safety Gate alert ([CosmeticOBS](https://cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/recalls-of-products-27/recall-of-products-january-3-2020-5095); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/health/How-safe-are-e28098black-hennae28099-tattoos.aspx)). Supply **only pure Lawsonia inermis leaf powder**, with lawsone % on the COA and a PPD-free statement for every lot. Do not offer "black", "neutral" or blended henna.

**Multani mitti (fuller's earth clay)** naturally contains trace heavy metals, which the EU tolerates only as "technically unavoidable". Expect buyers to demand a heavy-metal COA, plus microbiology and a crystalline-silica statement ([Argile du Velay TDS](https://www.knowde.com/stores/argile-du-velay/products/coswhite)). Laboratory-grade fuller's earth is explicitly "not for food, drug use", so the grade on the paperwork matters ([LabAlley SDS](https://media.laballey.com/docbuilder/fuller-safety-data-sheet-671bb87634b24.pdf)).

**Nagoya/ABS.** If an EU buyer uses an Indian botanical in R&D, they must show it was legally accessed under India's Biological Diversity Act, which was amended with new rules effective December 2024 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/life-sciences-biotechnology-nanotechnology/1350780/the-biological-diversity-amendment-bill-2023-an-overview)), to satisfy EU Reg 511/2014 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/511/article/4/data.htm?view=plain)). Your brother should keep his National Biodiversity Authority position on file, either an approval or a reasoned exemption.

### REACH: oils and clay are mostly exempt, extracts may not be

Under the EU chemicals law REACH, cold-pressed or pressed-and-refined vegetable oils that are not chemically modified and not hazard-classified are **exempt from registration (Annex V point 9)**. Solvent-extracted oils fall outside that exemption ([legislation.gov.uk Reg 987/2008](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2008/987/data.pdf); [Cosmetics & Toiletries](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/regulatory/reach/17385739.html)). Naturally occurring clays are exempt under Annex V point 7 ([Flints SDS](https://www.flints.co.uk/pdf/datasheets/PAT870%20Fuller's%20Earth%20F.pdf)). Registration only applies at **1 tonne or more per year per substance per importer** ([Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/chemicals/registering-chemicals-reach/faq/index_en.htm)). If a registration is ever needed, the Indian company can appoint an EU "Only Representative", which removes the duty from EU importers ([CIRS](https://cirs-reach.com/REACH/REACH_Roles_Obligations.html)). In practice:

- Ask your brother whether the castor oil is **cold-pressed or hexane-extracted**. That determines REACH status, and it is the selling point for cosmetics anyway.
- Keep a one-page "REACH Annex V exemption" letter for each oil and for the clay.
- Supply a French-language safety data sheet.

Pharmaceutical-grade castor oil, which must meet the European Pharmacopoeia monographs ([EMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ricini-oleum)), and industrial castor are not realistic starting points. **Arkema**, the big French buyer that makes Rilsan PA11 plastic entirely from castor oil, sources most of its supply from Gujarat through long-term programmes such as Pragati with Jayant Agro-Organics, BASF and Solidaridad. Pragati has now certified more than 10,000 farmers ([Arkema HPP](https://hpp.arkema.com/en/media/newslist/news/global/csr/2026/20260210-pragati-year-9-sustainable-castor-bean-program/)). A small trader cannot compete there. The castor opportunity for you is **organic, cold-pressed, hexane-free cosmetic oil in 20-200 kg lots** for hair, lash and nail brands and DIY retailers.

## Agent first, SASU later: how to set up in France

There are two business models, and the right legal structure depends on which one you choose.

**Model B: commercial agent.** Your brother invoices the buyer and you earn commission. Register as an **agent commercial**, which you can do as a micro-entrepreneur ([Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/agent-commercial-auto-entrepreneur)). Registration on the special register of commercial agents (RSAC) at the commercial court costs about **€25** and is mandatory ([LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/inscription-registre-special-agents-commerciaux/)). Your commission counts as commercial services, so the micro-entreprise **turnover ceiling is €83,600** and **social charges are 21.2% of turnover**. ACRE halves those charges for your first four quarters ([CCI Paris IDF](https://www.entreprises.cci-paris-idf.fr/actualites/micro-entrepreneur-revalorisation-des-seuils-pour-2026-2028); [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales)). You can optionally pay income tax as a flat 1.7% of turnover. The VAT-exemption threshold for services stays at **€37,500**: the planned cut to €25,000 was abandoned at the end of 2025 ([Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/actualites/nouveaux-plafonds-chiffre-affaires-2026-micro-entreprise)). Commission you invoice to an Indian company is generally outside the scope of French VAT, but confirm that with an accountant.

The agent statute (Code de commerce L134-1 onwards) gives you real protection. If the principal ends the contract, French courts customarily award **two years of gross commission** as an end-of-contract indemnity (confirmed again by the Aix-en-Provence appeal court in May 2026). You must claim it by registered letter within one year of termination ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/publication/indemnite-de-rupture-du-contrat-d-agence-commerciale); [Doctrine](https://www.doctrine.fr/d/CA/Aix-en-Provence/2026/CAPD96F3C66FA6731FA72CB)). Statutory notice is one month in year one, two in year two and three from year three ([Justifit](https://www.justifit.fr/b/guides/droit-contrats/contrat-agent-commercial/)). Because this is a family arrangement, write the contract anyway. Cover territory, exclusivity, products, the commission base (invoiced or paid), the commission rate, payment timing and governing law. Keep the commission rate at a level you can defend as a market rate, because tax authorities in both countries can question payments between related parties. The researchers could not verify the commonly quoted 3-10% range for ingredient agents.

A looser option is to act as an **apporteur d'affaires** (business introducer), paid per introduction with no special statute. It suits occasional deals, but it does not give you the indemnity protection.

**Model A: importer and distributor.** You buy, import, hold stock and resell. A micro-entreprise is a bad fit here. Social charges are **12.3% of gross sales** whatever your margin ([economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales)), so on €100,000 of sales at a 20% gross margin, about **€12,400 of your €20,000 margin goes to URSSAF**. In the VAT-exempt regime you also cannot recover import VAT. A **SASU or EURL** taxed under corporate tax is the right vehicle. Corporate tax is **15% on the first €42,500 of profit and 25% above that** ([Hayot Expertise](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/is-2026-taux-normal-taux-reduit-15-seuils)). Mandatory setup fees run about **€200-226 for a SASU** through the INPI single portal, or €400-850 through an online formalities platform ([Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/cout-creation-sasu); [LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/cout-creation-sasu-inpi/)).

| | Model B: agent (start here) | Model A: import and resell (later) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Micro-entrepreneur + RSAC (~€25) | SASU/EURL, corporate tax 15%/25% |
| Social cost | 21.2% of commission | Salary/dividends from company profit |
| EORI / customs | Not needed | Needed (free) + customs broker mandate |
| Import VAT | Buyer's problem | Self-assessed on your VAT return |
| Legal liability | Low; take professional liability insurance | You are the food business operator: recalls, traceability, product liability |
| Working capital | Almost none | Stock + 1-2 months transit + up to 60 days customer credit |
| Upside | Commission only | Full trading margin, own brand possible |

A sensible path is to start in Model B. When one or two buyers want small lots delivered from Paris stock, such as restaurants, La Chapelle shops or indie cosmetics brands, set up a SASU and import for those customers while still acting as agent on the large direct deals.

## Duties are mostly zero; VAT is reverse-charged; the FTA is not in force yet

**EORI.** Model A needs an EORI number. It is **free**, comes only from douane.gouv.fr, and is active within about 24 hours. Customs has warned about **scam sites that charge for it** ([douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/demarche/enregistrer-votre-entreprise-aupres-de-la-douane-numero-eori); [douane alert](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/actualites/arnaques-la-demande-de-numero-eori)). Make sure the customs declaration uses **your** EORI, not the carrier's, or the VAT reverse charge fails ([Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/autoliquidation-tva-importation-france/)). Most small importers clear goods through a registered customs representative, usually the freight forwarder.

**Duties.** Most of the range enters at 0%. The rates below come from tariff mirror sites, so **re-check each on TARIC/Access2Markets with origin "IN"**:

| Product | Code | Duty found | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ginger | 0910 11 | 0% | [thetradehub](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/customs/origin/0910) |
| Turmeric (whole or ground) | 0910 30 | 0% | [thetradehub](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/customs/origin/0910) |
| Curry | 0910 91 | 0% | same |
| "Other" ground spices/mixtures (possibly garam masala) | 0910 99 / 0910 91 90 | **up to 12.5%** | [zolltarifnummern](https://www.zolltarifnummern.de/2026/09109999) |
| Cardamom | 0908 | from 0% | [celna24](https://www.celna24.pl/customs-tariff/0908) |
| Pepper/chilli | 0904 | 0-4% depending on subheading | [celna24](https://celna24.pl/customs-tariff/0904) |
| Castor oil | 1515 30 90 | **5.1% MFN, 1.6% with GSP** | [hsciq](https://hsciq.com/en/HSEU/Code/1515309000) |

The garam masala line matters: classify it carefully with your broker before quoting a landed price.

**GSP.** India keeps the EU's lower GSP rates on agricultural and vegetable-oil products for 2026-2028. Reg 2025/1909 suspended GSP for India only in industrial sections: minerals, chemicals, plastics, rubber, textiles, stone, precious metals and base metals ([EUR-Lex 2025/1909](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1909/oj); [Access2Markets notice](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/gsp-system-updates-period-2026-2028)). Castor oil therefore still gets 1.6% instead of 5.1%, but **only if your brother is registered in the EU's REX system and puts a statement on origin on the invoice**. On €10,000 of castor oil that saves about €350. Two edge cases are worth knowing. Multani mitti falls in the graduated minerals section, though the duty on clay is probably already zero. Any chemically defined extract classified in the chemicals chapters loses GSP, which is why curcumin's tariff classification needs checking.

**The India-EU FTA.** Negotiations were **concluded on 27 January 2026** ([EC press release](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_184/IP_26_184_EN.pdf)). India's factsheet says **spices** are among the lines that get immediate duty elimination on entry into force ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219146&reg=3&lang=2)). Legal scrubbing was reported complete in August 2026, with **signing expected in Brussels in December 2026** ([ANI](https://aninews.in/news/business/india-eu-fta-legal-scrubbing-completed-signing-expected-by-year-end-govt-sources20260820171517/)). After signing, the deal still needs European Parliament consent and Council approval, plus national ratifications if it is treated as a mixed agreement ([Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/media/dhqd4p2s/eu-india-free-trade-agreement-announced-conclusion-of-negotiations-and-practical-compliance-considerations.pdf)). In October 2026 the FTA gives **zero tariff benefit**. Say "FTA concluded, signing expected December 2026", never "duty-free under the FTA". Because single spices are already at 0%, the FTA will matter mainly for castor and other oils, spice mixtures and processed items. No product-level schedule had been published at the time of research.

**VAT.** Since January 2022, import VAT in France is **reverse-charged** on your monthly CA3 VAT return, so you pay nothing at the border and there is no cash-flow hit, provided you are VAT-registered ([douane FAQ](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/uploads/files/FAQ%20op%C3%A9rateurs.pdf); [Dougs](https://www.dougs.fr/blog/tva-importation/)). Food for human consumption takes the **5.5%** reduced rate ([Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/tva/alimentaire)). Cosmetic products (henna, clay, castor oil) take 20%. Supplement ingredients are probably 5.5%, but check with an accountant. If you resell to a buyer in Belgium or Germany, the sale is VAT-exempt as an intra-EU supply once you have verified the buyer's VAT number on VIES.

## Freight swung threefold in 2026; insure, price in euros and get paid early

**Freight.** Container rates from India's west coast to North Europe have been volatile. MSC's rate from Nhava Sheva to Antwerp was **$1,550 per container in February 2026** and **$4,650 in August 2026** ([MSC Feb](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/january/price-announcement-india-pakistan-to-europe); [MSC Aug](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/june/price-announcement-trade-from-india-and-pakistan-to-europe)). Hapag-Lloyd announced North Europe base rates of about $5,253-6,753 per 20-foot container from August-September ([Container News](https://container-news.com/hapag-lloyd-raises-india-and-pakistan-europe-freight-rates/); [Portcast](https://www.portcast.io/carrier-advisories/hapag-lloyd-ocean-tariff-increase-india-to-europe-1aug2026)). In mid-September carriers began moving services back through Suez, and analysts expect overcapacity and **falling rates in Q4 2026** ([Hellenic Shipping News](https://hellenicshippingnews.com/suez-transits-are-up-59-and-rates-are-about-to-fall); [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/premier-alliance-prepares-for-asia-n-europe-services-return-to-suez/)). For part loads, LCL from India to North Europe runs about **$45-95 per cubic metre** plus origin and destination charges ([Suaid Global](https://suaidglobal.com/de/insights/lcl-shipping-rates/)). Many shipments reach Paris through Antwerp or Rotterdam and then by truck, rather than calling at Le Havre directly. Ask two or three forwarders for door-to-Paris quotes before you quote buyers. Freight barely matters for cardamom or extracts, but for low-value bulk such as turmeric and chilli it can be several percent of the price. Other practical points:

- Wooden pallets must be ISPM 15 heat-treated.
- Send samples by express courier.
- Never accept EtO fumigation of food cargo.

**Incoterms.** The following is general guidance, not sourced in this research. For Model B, your brother should quote **FCA, FOB or CIF** to EU buyers. Experienced importers usually prefer to control freight themselves (FCA/FOB). Smaller buyers like CIF to their port. Avoid **DDP**: it would make the Indian company the importer of record, responsible for French VAT and compliance. For Model A, buy **CIF or CFR** from your brother so he books the freight, and buy your own "all-risks" (ICC A) cargo insurance, because the CIF minimum is thinner. Sell onward to French customers **DAP** from your warehouse.

**An illustrative landed cost.** This combines the sourced freight and duty data above with **assumed** local costs; replace them with real quotes. Take one 20-foot container of 12,000 kg of turmeric powder at an assumed €2.50/kg FOB (€30,000). Add ocean freight of about €2,150 (mid-2026 range), insurance of about €100, **0% duty**, and assumed figures of €600-900 for port and clearance, €300-600 for lab testing and €700-1,000 for trucking to Paris. That gives a landed cost of about **€34,000-35,000, or €2.85-2.90/kg, about 14-16% over FOB**. Import VAT is reverse-charged and cash-neutral. A 15-30% distributor markup would put the resale price at about €3.30-3.75/kg. That markup range is a working assumption, not verified market data. CBI notes that consumer-packed spices are very competitive in Europe and recommends starting with bulk ground spices rather than retail packs ([CBI value-added spices](https://www.srilankabusiness.com/pdf/read_more_products/annex_7_8_cbi_reports_on_value_added_spices_herbs.pdf)).

**Payment terms.** French law caps B2B payment terms at **60 days from invoice, or 45 days end-of-month** if agreed; the default is 30 days. Late payers owe a **flat €40** recovery fee plus penalties, and abusive terms can bring fines of **up to €2m** ([Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/delai-paiement-facture-entreprises-regles-lme-sanctions); [RubyPayeur](https://rubypayeur.com/blog/indemnite-forfaitaire-frais-de-recouvrement/)). In Model A, you finance one to two months of ocean transit plus up to 60 days of customer credit, and that working capital is the real constraint. The following is practical guidance, not sourced. In Model B, have your brother ask new EU buyers for a **30% advance, with the balance against documents** (copy bill of lading or cash-against-documents). Move to 30-60 days open account only once there is history, ideally backed by trade credit insurance from Allianz Trade, Coface or Atradius. **Invoice in euros**, so currency risk sits with the Indian side, which can hedge it with Indian banks. Get your own commission paid **monthly on invoiced sales**, not "when the buyer pays", or at least set that rule in writing.

**Insurance.** In Model A you are legally the "producer" for EU product liability and food recalls, so take **professional and product liability insurance** (RC pro / RC produit) and consider contaminated-product recall cover, given the EtO history. In Model B, a basic professional liability policy is enough.

These are the verified targets.

**French spice houses and packers.**

### Outreach scripts

French buyers expect formal "vous", short emails, a ready offer and real documents. CBI describes European buyers as "professional, pragmatic and to the point" ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-find-buyers)). Attach a one-page French spec sheet (fiche technique). Phone three to five working days later. Avoid August. Tailor the product line in each email to what the buyer actually sells.

**French – food/spice buyer**

> **Objet :** Curcuma, gingembre et cardamome d'Inde – échantillons et COA disponibles à Paris
>
> Madame, Monsieur [Nom],
>
> Je me permets de vous contacter en tant qu'agent en France (basé en Île-de-France) de La Dolce India, exportateur indien d'épices et d'ingrédients botaniques.
>

## Conclusion

This opportunity is more about **trust infrastructure** than about finding demand. France's supplement and cosmetics industries are large and growing, and India really is the natural origin for turmeric and castor. But French buyers are not short of Indian suppliers. They are short of Indian suppliers who show up with EU lab results, correct statistics, a working website and someone local who answers the phone in French. That last point is your real asset. Living near Paris lets you turn a family export business into a supplier that French quality managers can approve, and the agent model lets you test that value for almost no money before taking on stock, credit risk and food-law liability.

Watch three moving parts over the next year, because each could change the plan: the **EU review of ashwagandha** (possible EU-wide restrictions), the **six-monthly changes to Indian border-check lists** (turmeric's status is currently unclear), and the **FTA's signature and ratification**, which will help castor oil and spice mixes more than single spices. On a two-year view, the strongest direction is probably not commodity spices at all. It is **documented, organic, cosmetic-grade botanicals** (cold-pressed castor and moringa oils, pure henna, clay, amla), sold to France's dense network of natural-cosmetics SMEs. There, regulatory hurdles are lower than for supplements, competition from China is weaker than in ginger or chilli, and a Paris-based intermediary adds the most value.
