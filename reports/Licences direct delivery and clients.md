# Register now, earn commission, import later

France has **no general "import licence"** for spices, plant extracts, oils, henna or clay. Anyone can import them once they have a registered business and a few free identification numbers; beyond that, only product-specific health and safety rules apply ([service-public.gouv.fr](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F21919)). Your route is a **micro-entreprise registration** (free, plus about **€25–27** if you register as a commercial agent; SIRET in roughly **1–4 weeks**), then a **SIREN-based EORI customs number** (free, about 1–2 days), an **intra-EU VAT number** (free, about 2 days), a free check with your local food-safety office (DDPP) and a **written mandate to a customs broker**. That makes you legally able to import in about **3–6 weeks, for less than €30 in official fees**. Your brother's company needs its own Indian paperwork: an IEC, a Spices Board CRES and possibly an FSSAI licence, which alone can take **2–4 months**. Some spices also need test certificates on every shipment. Being *able* to import is not the same as being *ready* to. The importer becomes the legally responsible "food business operator", carries recall risk on the EU's most-flagged spice origin, and, on the micro-entreprise regime, cannot recover import VAT. Delivering straight to a client works in four ways. The client can import (the normal and simplest route). Your brother's company can ship "DDP" (legally heavy since 1 January 2026). An EU importer can sit in the middle (best for small clients). Or you can earn commission as an agent on top of either of the first and third routes. **Start this month as La Dolce India's registered commercial agent (agent commercial).** Let professional buyers import, and send small buyers through an EU importer. For clients, this report lists about 120 named French and EU prospects. Start with small spice packers, Ayurvedic supplement brands and natural-cosmetics makers around Paris, and expect roughly **3–5% of cold emails to get a reply**. Use **SIAL Paris (17–21 October 2026)** and **Fi Europe (17–19 November 2026)** to fill the pipeline. The better overall idea is to lead with **cosmetic-grade botanicals (cold-pressed castor oil, pure henna, multani mitti, amla, moringa) plus turmeric**, where India is the natural origin and the rules are lightest. Import in your own name, through a company, only once two or three small clients want repeat deliveries from French stock.

**How this research was done.** The research tools could read only search-result snippets, not full web pages. Every fee, deadline and regulation below should be checked on the official page before you sign or pay anything. Items that could not be confirmed are flagged as unverified. In the source notes the person in France is sometimes called "the brother"; in this report **"you" means the person near Paris, and "your brother" means the exporter in India.** This report builds on the earlier report, *Indian ingredients export to France*, and corrects it where newer findings differ (see the section "Seven things changed since the first report").

## No licence exists in France, but five free registrations make you an importer

The idea of an "import licence" comes from India, where every exporter needs an Importer-Exporter Code (IEC). France has nothing to buy or apply for under that name for these goods. The official government guide for micro-entrepreneurs who import from outside the EU says what you actually need. You must be registered with customs (an EORI number, free through the SOPRANO service). Customs declarations must be filed, either by you or by a registered customs representative such as a freight forwarder or express carrier. Import VAT, and sometimes duty, is due at clearance ([service-public.gouv.fr](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F21919)). On top of that come the product rules: food safety for spices and supplement ingredients, cosmetics rules for finished beauty products, chemicals rules (REACH) for large volumes, and organic rules if you sell organic.

### Short answer: about 3–6 weeks and almost no money on your side

The order matters. **Since 1 January 2026, France accepts only EORI numbers based on the SIREN** (FR plus your 9-digit company number); SIRET-based EORIs are no longer valid ([douane.gouv.fr](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/towards-single-eori-siren-and-european-portal-customs-authorisations)). So nothing can start until your business exists and you have a SIREN. Every new business registration has gone through one online portal, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (run by INPI), since 2023. Registering as a micro-entrepreneur for buying and reselling is **free**. A commercial agent must also register on the special register of commercial agents (RSAC), for about **€25–27**. Sources disagree on timing: about **8–15 working days** for the SIRET is typical, but plan for **1–4 weeks**, and the URSSAF social-security account can take longer ([Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/encyclopedie/micro-entreprise-regime-auto-entrepreneur/lessentiel-ce-regime/ou-comment-sinscrire)).

The import-VAT step hides a trap for beginners. Since 1 January 2022, import VAT is self-assessed on the monthly VAT return (form CA3) rather than paid at the border. Even a micro-entrepreneur under the VAT exemption ("franchise en base") **must** have an intra-EU VAT number, give it to customs and declare the import VAT on form 3310-CA3. Customs warns that using the self-assessment code G0008 without a VAT number leads to no refund and possible penalties ([customs FAQ](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/uploads/files/FAQ%20op%C3%A9rateurs.pdf)). A micro-entrepreneur under the VAT exemption **cannot recover** that import VAT, so it becomes a pure cost ([service-public.gouv.fr](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F21919)). The micro regime also charges social contributions of **12.3% on gross sales of goods**, whatever your margin ([economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales)). That is why the earlier report recommended a SASU, taxed at **15% on the first €42,500 of profit and 25% above** ([Hayot Expertise](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/is-2026-taux-normal-taux-reduit-15-seuils)), once you trade real volumes. If you later create a SASU, it gets a new SIREN, so it needs its own EORI and VAT number. That repeats a few free steps but costs nothing.

### Product rules decide what being "the importer" really means

The registrations above are cheap. The responsibility that comes with them is not. **Since 1 June 2023, French customs (DGDDI), not the DGCCRF or DDPP, carries out import health checks on food of non-animal origin, which includes spices.** The DGCCRF checks products after customs clearance ([douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/professionnels/commerce-international/controles-sanitaires-et-de-qualite-des-aliments)). Foods listed in EU Regulation 2019/1793 must enter through a border control post, with a CHED-D pre-notification filed in the EU's TRACES system **at least one working day before arrival** ([douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/professionnels/commerce-international/controles-sanitaires-et-de-qualite-des-aliments/vous-importez)). That list changes about every six months. The June 2026 update, **Regulation (EU) 2026/1206**, raised checks on Indian cumin to **50%** of consignments for pesticide residues ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1206)). According to AGRINFO's summary, it also moved Indian cinnamon into the lighter Annex I (20% checks, no exporter certificate) and removed Indian Capsicum (chilli) from the ethylene-oxide list ([AGRINFO](https://agrinfo.eu/book-of-reports/temporary-increased-official-controls-on-foods-from-certain-countries-june-2026-update/)). Sources disagree on exactly which Indian entries January's Regulation 2026/194 removed: okra, vanilla and cloves according to AGRINFO, or okra, rice and nutmeg according to the Spices Board ([EUR-Lex 2026/194](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/194/oj/eng); [Spices Board advisory](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Trade%20advisory_compressed.pdf)). Turmeric's status is still unresolved. **Read the consolidated annex on EUR-Lex before every first shipment of a product.** The research tools could not open it.

The real exposure is recalls, not border checks. Between September 2020 and April 2024, the EU alert system logged **527 ethylene-oxide notifications, 332 of them for India alone**. **Only 87 were stopped at the border.** Most of the rest were recalled from the market later, which means the risk falls on the EU importer of record, not the Indian exporter ([University of Veterinary Medicine Budapest](https://dfi.univet.hu/en/?p=10695)). Spice and herb notifications reached **279 in 2025**, mostly pesticide-residue excesses ([KVK](https://kvk.nl/en/international/importing-herbs-and-spices)).

The other product rules only apply if you sell *finished* goods yourself. Whoever first places an imported food supplement on the French market carries the same responsibility as a manufacturer and must declare it on DGAL's **Compl'Alim** platform (which replaced TeleIcare in September 2025). Bulk extracts sold to a supplement brand are normally declared by that brand, not by the trader ([DAAF note](https://daaf.reunion.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/communication_complements_alimentaires.pdf); [Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/declaration-mise-sur-le-marche-complement-alimentaire-france)). Retail-packed henna, multani mitti or castor oil sold as a cosmetic needs an **EU "Responsible Person"**, a CPNP notification and a Product Information File with a safety assessment kept for 10 years. Bulk raw materials sold business-to-business to a cosmetics maker appear not to need CPNP filing; that is the consultancy's inference ([Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/importateur-cosmetique-info)). Henna must be free of PPD, and clay must be tested for heavy metals. Under REACH, an EU importer of a substance at **1 tonne a year or more** must register it. Cold-pressed vegetable oils that are not chemically modified are exempt under Annex V entry 9, which likely covers castor, moringa seed and black cumin oil ([ECHA](https://echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/2324906/annex_v_en.pdf)). Organic goods need an **electronic certificate of inspection (e-COI)** issued in TRACES by your brother's certifier before departure ([Soil Association](https://www.soilassociation.org/for-business/soil-association-certification/business-support/exporting-for-organic-businesses/traces-nt/)). Organic import checks in France have been done at Nice since 1 December 2025 ([douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/professionnels/commerce-international/controles-sanitaires-et-de-qualite-des-aliments)).

## Four ways to get goods straight from India to a French client

"Direct delivery" raises two separate questions. The first is **who imports**: who is named on the customs declaration, pays import VAT and becomes the EU "food business operator" responsible for labels and recalls. The second is **how you get paid**: a margin if you buy and resell, or a commission if you only connect the parties. EU food-labelling law answers the first question for food. The responsible operator is the business under whose name the food is sold. **If that business is not established in the EU, the responsibility falls on the importer into the EU** ([Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/article/8/data.htm)). Your brother's Indian company therefore cannot simply be the responsible party for food sold in France. Someone established in the EU always has to be.

| | **Route 1: client imports** | **Route 2: brother ships DDP** | **Route 3: EU importer in the middle** | **Route 4: you as agent or introducer** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who imports | The French client | La Dolce India itself | A French or Dutch importer, distributor or 3PL | Not you; combined with route 1 or 3 |
| What the client needs | EORI, intra-EU VAT number, customs broker, food-business registration | Nothing beyond receiving goods | Nothing beyond a normal domestic purchase | Same as route 1 or 3 |
| What your brother needs | IEC, CRES, certificates per shipment, export documents | All of that **plus** an EU EORI, a French VAT number, a fiscal representative (possibly), a jointly liable customs representative and an EU-based food operator | Export documents only | A written agency contract and a bank able to remit commission |
| What you need | A registration only if you earn commission | Same | Same | Micro-entreprise (+ RSAC if agent commercial) |
| Paperwork burden | Low | **Very high** | Lowest | Low |
| Best for | Professional buyers that already import | Rarely worth it; only if a large client insists | Small buyers and volume spices | Earning from routes 1 and 3 |

### Route 1: the client imports, the normal way professionals buy

This is how most professional ingredient buyers work. Your brother's company invoices the French client and ships on FCA, FOB, CFR, CIF or DAP terms. The client clears the goods with its own EORI and VAT number, self-assesses import VAT and is the EU food business operator. Under delivered-at-place terms the French buyer is still the official importer ([Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/en/importing-into-france/)). Buyers like this route because they recover import VAT through self-assessment, use their own broker and freight contracts, and are legally responsible for the product anyway.

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client and your brother | Purchase order and pro-forma invoice in euros. First deals often require a 30–50% advance; this comes from low-authority marketplace guides ([Giriraj guide](https://girirajpsyllium.in/buy-whole-spices-bulk-india-procurement-guide/)) |
| 2 | Your brother | Pre-shipment test through the Spices Board ESS portal if the spice is notified, plus the official certificate for listed items, certificate of analysis, REX origin statement on the invoice and organic e-COI if relevant |
| 3 | Indian customs agent | Files the shipping bill under the LUT; goods are loaded |
| 4 | Your brother, then the client's broker | Your brother sends the bill of lading, invoice, packing list and certificates. For listed spices, the broker files the CHED-D at least one working day before arrival |
| 5 | French customs | Document, identity and sometimes physical checks at the border post (Le Havre, Marseille, Roissy). Samples taken for testing can hold goods for days |
| 6 | Client | Customs release under the client's EORI; import VAT self-assessed on its CA3 return |
| 7 | Client | Responsible for traceability, labelling and any recall |
| 8 | You | Invoice your commission to La Dolce India (route 4) |

The advantage is that **you need almost no money and carry almost no legal risk**. The drawbacks are real, though. Only buyers that already import from outside the EU will accept this route. Small shops, small cosmetic brands and start-up supplement brands often will not. Orders tend to be part-container or full-container sizes. And because the client carries border holds and recall risk, it will only say yes to a complete document pack.

### Route 2: shipping DDP got harder on 1 January 2026

Under DDP ("delivered duty paid"), your brother's company would be the importer of record in France, paying VAT and duty itself. Since **1 January 2026 the one-off fiscal representation used for these imports ("représentation fiscale ponctuelle", régime 42) is no longer available to non-EU sellers**, and forwarders can no longer clear such imports under their own number ([KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html); [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/en/selling-ddp-france/)). To ship DDP, the Indian company would need its own EU EORI and a French VAT number. It would also need a customs representative acting in "indirect representation", who is jointly liable for the import VAT and will therefore ask for a guarantee ([douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/fiche/identification-la-tva-et-representation-fiscale)). Normally it would need an accredited fiscal representative in France too. Sources conflict on that last point: one compliance firm lists **India among the countries exempt** from appointing a fiscal representative, while others say every non-EU importer now needs one ([Mathez Compliance](https://www.mathez-compliance.com/liste-pays-societes-redevables-tva-pas-de-rf-en-france/); [Customs Support](https://www.customssupport.com/customs-procedure-42-changes-ahead-in-2026-what-non-eu-businesses-need-to-know/)). A lighter "mandataire à l'international" has existed since 21 February 2025 for importers without a French VAT number ([KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html)). No public French price list was found. One provider quoted roughly **€800–1,000 per quarter** for small companies without naming the country ([Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/france/fiscal-representation); [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/cost-of-vat-compliance-eu/)).

For food there is an extra problem. A customs importer-of-record service does not automatically become the EU food business operator named on the label. That role has to be accepted by contract, and the party taking it needs to be registered with a DDPP ([Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/importer-of-record-france/)). **Verdict: do not start here.** DDP adds a fixed cost base and puts French border rejections and recalls on your brother's company. Two developments could change this later: the Netherlands still allows fiscal representation for non-EU companies, and an EU directive aims to move import VAT onto non-established sellers from 1 July 2028 ([KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html)).

### Route 3: an EU importer takes title, clears and resells

This is the most scalable route for an exporter with no EU company. An established importer or distributor buys from your brother's company (FOB or CIF), clears customs, holds stock and resells, including to your French clients. The **Netherlands imported about 116,400 tonnes of spices and herbs in 2025**. Dutch firms grind, blend, pack and re-export to Belgium, Germany and Spain ([CBI Netherlands](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/netherlands)). CBI advises newcomers to approach importers first, because many serve several European countries, and notes that one Dutch importer can open France, Germany, Belgium and Spain ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-find-buyers)). For botanical extracts, importers and distributors are also the main entry channel, and they expect a safety data sheet, technical data sheet and certificate of analysis before talking ([CBI health ingredients](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-health-products/finding-buyers)).

There are four variants. In the first, the distributor buys outright and resells to the French client; this is the cleanest. In the second, the French client places the order but a Dutch or French importer imports and invoices "back-to-back" for a handling margin. In the third, a 3PL or importer-of-record service imports for your brother's account and stores goods in a French or Dutch warehouse; this only works if the 3PL accepts the food-operator role and a VAT solution exists. In the fourth, a large Dutch or German importer buys full containers and the French client buys from it. The cost is the importer's margin, which no source quantified. The benefit is that **your brother's company only ever ships FOB or CIF**, and small French clients can receive goods with no import formalities at all. You can still be the agent who brings each French client to the importer.

### Route 4: you earn commission as agent commercial or business introducer

French law offers two ways to earn commission without buying the goods. An **agent commercial** is a permanent representative who negotiates or concludes sales in the principal's name (Code de commerce L.134-1 to L.134-17) and must register on the RSAC. An **apporteur d'affaires** (business introducer) makes one-off introductions, has no special legal status and can work as a micro-entrepreneur ([Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/encyclopedie/structures-juridiques/statuts-particuliers/intermediaires-du-commerce-agent-commercial)).

| | Agent commercial | Apporteur d'affaires |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Micro-entreprise + RSAC (about €25–27) | Micro-entreprise only |
| Contract | Written contract; quarterly commission statements | Simple written agreement recommended |
| Typical commission (indicative, all industries) | About 10–25% | About 1–5% |
| On termination | Indemnity, by custom about **2 years of commissions** | Nothing |
| Risk | The principal owes the indemnity | An introducer who negotiates regularly can be reclassified as an agent |

Sources: [Captain Contrat](https://www.captaincontrat.com/articles-droit-commercial/agent-commercial-apporteur-daffaires); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/publication/indemnite-de-rupture-du-contrat-d-agence-commerciale). The commission ranges are general, across all industries. For thin-margin bulk spices, expect the low end. The earlier research could not verify a typical rate for ingredient agents.

The tax mechanics are simple once you know them. Your commission invoice to an Indian company is a business-to-business service supplied abroad, so it is **outside French VAT**. Write **"TVA non applicable – article 259-1 du CGI"** on the invoice ([Copeps](https://copeps.fr/actualites/auto-entrepreneur-tva-client-etranger/)). Sources disagree on the income category. Most place introducers under **BNC** (micro-BNC, with a 34% flat allowance for income tax), while one places them under micro-BIC ([Le Blog du Dirigeant](https://www.leblogdudirigeant.com/apporteur-daffaire-et-fiscalite/); [Portail auto-entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/actualites/apporteur-affaires-auto-entrepreneur/)). That decides whether your social contributions are about **21.2%** (BIC services) or about **25.6%** (other BNC) of what you invoice, halved for your first four quarters under ACRE ([economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales)). URSSAF decides the category from the activity you declare, so ask at registration. Foreign-source income and any foreign accounts such as Wise or PayPal must be declared ([impots.gouv.fr brochure](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/www2/fichiers/documentation/brochure/ir_2026/pdf_som/20-revenus_etranger_365a368.pdf)).

On the Indian side, new RBI export and import rules (FEMA Regulations and Directions 2026) took effect on **1 October 2026**, replacing the old master direction. Among other changes, they cut the export proceeds realisation period from 15 to 9 months ([AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/rbi-issues-foreign-exchange-management-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services-regulations-2026-and-directions-on-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services/)). Their text on agency commission was not found. Under the old rules, banks applied a **12.5% benchmark** and allowed more on request ([FIEO/HSBC FAQ](https://hsbcindia.globallinker.com/bizforum/article/banking-rbi-regulations-for-exporters/87823)). Indian courts mostly hold that **no Indian tax needs to be withheld** on commission to a non-resident for services rendered abroad, though one tribunal disagreed ([BCAJ](https://bcajonline.org/journal/section-40ai-r-w-s-195-whether-no-tax-is-deductible-u-s-195-on-the-commission-payable-to-a-non-resident-for-services-rendered-outside-india-held-yes/)). Forms 15CA/15CB became Forms 145/146 from 1 April 2026 ([SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/form-145-form-146-foreign-remittances)). Your brother should ask his bank and chartered accountant before the first payment.

| Step | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You | Register the micro-entreprise as agent commercial plus RSAC (1–4 weeks) |
| 2 | You and your brother | Sign a written contract: territory (France, perhaps Benelux), products, commission rate and base (invoiced or paid sales), payment timing, exclusivity, governing law |
| 3 | You | Find the client, send samples, negotiate. The client buys from La Dolce India through route 1 or 3 |
| 4 | Your brother | Declares the commission to his bank, or pays it after the client pays |
| 5 | You | Invoice in euros with "TVA non applicable – art. 259-1 du CGI" |
| 6 | Your brother's bank | Sends the money by SWIFT with the required Indian forms |
| 7 | You | Declare turnover to URSSAF (monthly or quarterly) and on your income-tax return (2042-C-PRO) |

Because this is a family arrangement, keep the rate one you can defend as a market rate, and keep invoices transparent. Tax authorities in both countries can question payments between related parties.

### A fifth option for later: you import and the truck goes straight to the client

You can also buy from your brother's company, import in your own name and have the forwarder deliver from the port straight to the client's door, with no warehouse. Legally this is the full importer setup from the first section: you hold the EORI and VAT number, carry food-operator liability and pay import VAT. It suits the moment when small clients want delivered lots and no EU importer will serve them. Do it through a SASU rather than a micro-entreprise.

### Which route to start with

With no registration, no clients and no capital at risk, **start with route 4 layered on routes 1 and 3**. Register as agent commercial now. Let professional importers buy directly from La Dolce India (route 1). Steer small clients to an EU importer or distributor (route 3), ideally one or two you have recruited yourself. Skip DDP. Choose agent commercial over introducer because you will be negotiating continuously for one principal. That is exactly the activity the agent statute covers, and it costs only about €25 more. Keep the fifth option for when demand proves itself.

## Samples: courier them as "no commercial value" and stay under 10 kg

Small food samples are legal and routine. Most problems come from missing paperwork, not from the rules.

On the Indian side, any trade-related courier shipment needs the exporter's **IEC**, including free samples; only genuine personal parcels are exempt ([DHL India KYC](https://www.dhl.com/content/dam/dhl/local/in/dhl-express/documents/pdf/KYC%20Norms.pdf)). India's courier rules define samples as free commercial samples worth up to **₹50,000 per consignment**, with no foreign-exchange payment ([Seair](https://www.seair.co.in/custom-regulations/courier-imports-and-exports-clearance-regulations-1998-62622.aspx)). It is unconfirmed whether that threshold survived the March 2026 amendments, which removed the ₹10 lakh cap on commercial courier exports ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/mar/doc2026331837101.pdf)). Even a free sample needs a declared value on the invoice "for customs purposes only" ([Bhatt & Joshi](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/chapter-10-exporting-product-samples/)).

On the French side, non-invoiced samples of negligible value, used only to win orders, qualify for **duty relief under Article 86 of Regulation 1186/2009** ([legislation.gov.uk](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2009/1186/article/86?view=plain)). French customs clears them with additional procedure code **C30** (and CANA 0066) ([douane FAQ](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2026-06/26/FAQ-Droit-de-douane-forfaitaire-de-3-euros.pdf)). Samples follow that relief route rather than the new **€3 flat duty** that has applied to low-value parcels since 1 July 2026 ([Lexgo](https://www.lexgo.be/en/news-and-articles/14980-the-end-of-the-eur-150-customs-duty-exemption-the-european-union-introduces-a-flat-rate-eur-3-duty-on-small-parcels)). Crucially, **the EU border-check regulation 2019/1793 does not apply to trade samples not placed on the market unless they weigh over 10 kg net** (50 kg for fresh produce) ([EUR-Lex consolidated 2019/1793](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2019/1793/2026-02-18/eng)). So a 100 g–1 kg sample needs no CHED-D and no official certificate. One catch: since 1 January 2026, a SIREN-based EORI is required on import declarations, and a missing EORI or VAT number can block goods ([Parcel Broker](https://parcelbroker.co.uk/articles/french-customs-EORI-changes/)). **Have samples sent either to the prospect's business address or to you once you hold an EORI.**

Costs are modest. India Post Speed Post to France costs about **₹1,040 for the first 250 g plus ₹70 per extra 250 g**, roughly ₹1,250 per kilo ([Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/international-speed-post/amp/)). Express couriers charge about **₹1,890–4,250 for 0.5–2 kg**, plus 18% GST and a 15–25% fuel surcharge ([ClickPost](https://clickpost.ai/blog/international-courier-charges)). DHL treats foodstuffs as a restricted category that needs its prior written agreement ([DHL](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-ca/ship-with-dhl/start-shipping/restricted-commodities)). Pack 50–250 g of each powder or 50–100 ml of each oil in sealed food-grade pouches. Label each with: product, botanical name, lot number, "origin India", net weight and **"Échantillon gratuit – sans valeur commerciale – ne pas vendre"** (free sample, no commercial value, not for resale). The pro-forma invoice should give a low but realistic value per item (for example €5–20), the HS codes, the consignee's name, address and SIREN-EORI, and the purpose "free trade sample – relief under Art. 86 Reg. 1186/2009". Put the matching certificate of analysis, the specification and, for oils and cosmetic powders, the safety data sheet in the parcel. That checklist is a synthesis of the sources above. Cold-pressed castor, moringa and black cumin oils are normally not dangerous goods, but essential oils often are. That point is background knowledge and should be checked with the carrier before shipping.

A **Paris sample library** works best. Your brother sends one consolidated kit under 10 kg to you once your EORI is active. You then hand-deliver or post samples within France in a day, which no Indian competitor can match.

### Start where the order cycle is shortest

French buyers fall into segments with very different buying speeds. Ranked from quickest to slowest first order, the order is as follows. Small blenders and fine-food packers come first. Next come Ayurvedic and botanical supplement brands that buy small lots of powder, then DIY and independent cosmetics brands that buy oils, henna, amla and clay. Paris South Asian cash-and-carries take retail packs. Organic wholesalers have long listing cycles and need organic certification. Industrial buyers (McCormick, Arkopharma, PiLeJe, Nutergia, Havea, Naturactive) need audited supply and full dossiers, so treat them as **12–24-month targets**. This ranking is the researchers' synthesis, not a published statistic. Two segment facts support it. Supplement manufacturing in France is mostly outsourced to contract manufacturers ("façonniers"), so **pitch both the brand, which specifies the raw material, and the façonnier, which buys it** ([Nutri&Co](https://nutriandco.com/fr/pages/laboratoire-complement-alimentaire)). And Biocoop decides listings centrally against a specification it tightens every year, with about 80% of its range of French origin ([LSA](https://www.lsa-conso.fr/biocoop-le-militantisme-reste-au-c-ur-du-modele-assument-les-patrons-de-l-enseigne-bio-interview,465313)). Demand is real: France, the Netherlands and the UK each import over **€300 million of spices a year**, behind Germany's €592 million ([Statista](https://WWW.STATISTA.COM/statistics/1289767/leading-spice-and-herb-importers-europe/)). The two-week recipe to a list of 100 or more companies is the researchers' synthesis. On days 1–3, pull the NAF codes above nationwide and then for départements 75, 92, 93, 94, 95, 77, 13, 84 and 69. Keep active firms with at least one employee, and cut 300+ raw names to the 60–80 whose websites mention spices, plants, Ayurveda, henna or clay. Contact the quality manager as well as the buyer, because quality managers strongly influence deals ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-do-business)). On days 10–12, find emails with Hunter, or use generic addresses such as achats@ or contact@. Track SIREN, NAF, segment, product fit, contact, source, dates, sample sent and status in one sheet.

### What buyers will ask for before they reply

CBI's guidance is blunt. Have a **technical data sheet, a safety data sheet, a certificate of analysis per lot and a product sheet**, prepared by a lab or chemist rather than copied from internet templates. Send **"consistent quality samples"**, and prove traceability ([CBI cosmetic ingredients](https://cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/doing-business)). State the curcumin assay basis (95% curcuminoids versus 95% curcumin), because specifications differ ([Atida example](https://www.atida.fr/vit-all-curcuma-extraits-standardises-avec-poivre-noir-120-gelules-vegetales.html)). Henna buyers want proof of no PPD and no metallic salts ([Soin et Nature](https://www.soin-et-nature.com/fr/henne-128004)). Buyers increasingly test for adulteration, ethylene oxide and pesticides ([CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/buyer-requirements)). On price, quote **euros per kilo**. Small French buyers prefer **DAP / "franco de port"** (delivered to their site, excluding import duties and taxes) ([Ship4wd](https://ship4wd.com/incoterms/dap-vs-fob)). Importers prefer **FCA**, which suits containers better than FOB ([BBVA](https://www.bbva.es/en/empresas/productos/negocio-internacional/noticias-comex/incoterms-2020-may26.html)). Under CIF the seller only has to buy minimum insurance cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C), so state the cover ([doesspice](https://doesspice.com/en/news/incoterms-2020?rCH=2)). Give prices a validity of 15–30 days.

### Outreach: legal, short, French, six touches in four weeks

Cold email to professionals is legal in France on an **opt-out** basis, while email to consumers needs opt-in ([CNIL/BPI](https://WWW.CNIL.FR/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/bpi-cnil-rgpd_fiche_2_ameliorez-maitrisez-votre-relation-client_0.pdf)). The message must relate to the recipient's job, or go to a generic address such as contact@ ([Bercy](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/obligations-entreprises-envoi-courriel-newsletters)). Every email needs a simple, free way to object ([Leto](https://www.leto.legal/news/cnil-communications-electroniques-prospection-regles-2026)). Keep batches under 50 per segment. Small campaigns average a **5.8% reply rate against 2.1% for large ones** ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)).

| Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | LinkedIn | Connection request with a one-line note |
| 1 | Email 1 | Subject naming their product; 3 lines; 2–3 matching products; free sample offer; opt-out line |
| 4 | LinkedIn | Short message, or a comment on their post |
| 7 | Email 2 | Specification sheet and an indicative price (€/kg DAP, minimum order) |
| 10–12 | Phone | Short call in French |
| 18 | Email 3 | Proof: lab results or certificate (only if you really hold them) |
| 28 | Email 4 | Polite "closing the file" email |

These scripts adapt the researchers' suggested sequence. The earlier report has longer French templates for food, cosmetic and importer buyers. Use them only once your SIRET and the website exist, and never claim EU lab results you do not hold.

> **LinkedIn (day 0):** « Bonjour [Prénom], je représente en France La Dolce India, exportateur indien d'épices et d'actifs végétaux (curcuma, gingembre, cardamome, moringa, amla, huile de ricin). Lots tracés, certificat d'analyse par lot. Je serais ravi d'échanger. »
> *(English: I represent La Dolce India in France; traceable lots, certificate of analysis per lot; happy to connect.)*

> **Email 1 (day 1). Objet : Curcuma d'Inde pour vos mélanges ? Échantillon offert**
> Bonjour Madame/Monsieur [Nom],

> **Email 2 (day 7):** « Comme promis, voici la fiche technique de notre [produit]. Prix indicatif : à partir de [X] €/kg rendu chez vous (DAP), minimum [25] kg, validité 15 jours. Je peux vous remettre l'échantillon en main propre à [ville]. »
> *(English: here is the spec sheet; indicative price delivered to you, minimum order, valid 15 days; I can hand-deliver the sample.)*

> **Phone (day 10–12):** « Bonjour, [Prénom] de La Dolce India, je vous ai écrit au sujet de [produit]. Qui s'occupe des achats de matières premières chez vous ? … Quels critères comptent le plus pour vous : origine, analyses, prix, petits volumes ? »

> **Before a fair, to exhibitors who buy:** « Je visite [SIAL Paris] du [17] au [21] octobre. Auriez-vous 15 minutes sur votre stand pour découvrir nos épices et actifs indiens ? »

### What to expect: roughly 1 order per 100–300 well-chosen contacts

Generic B2B cold-email benchmarks set expectations. Average reply rates fell from **8.5% in 2019 to about 5% in 2025** ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). Instantly measured **3.43%** across its platform in its 2026 benchmark ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)). In a February 2026 sample of 2 million emails, only about **14% of replies were positive**, giving roughly 0.64% interested replies ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). Apollo calls anything above 6% strong ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach)). Applied to your situation, which is an inference since no spice-specific data exists, **100 well-targeted contacts give about 3–6 replies and 1–2 sample requests**. **300–500 contacts give 3–10 sample trials and 1–3 first orders over 3–6 months.** Phone calls, in-person visits around Paris and fair meetings should beat these email-only rates, which is why the local presence matters.

One warning protects you from a common trap. Alibaba's own seller guide says a real buyer **never** asks the seller to pay registration, verification or insurance fees before an order ([Alibaba seller blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/vn/blogs/prevent-intl-trade-fraud-8-schemes)). Indian traders have lost money paying "sample dispatch" fees, and hacked email accounts have redirected buyer payments ([SeaIR](https://www.seair.co.in/exim-news/india-cautions-traders-about-fraud-in-import-export-from-china.aspx); [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/indian-smes-face-heat-cyber-crimes)). Confirm bank details by phone, every time.

## The better overall idea: become the trusted local half of an Indian supplier

French buyers can find Indian suppliers easily. What they lack is one they can trust: a supplier who brings EU-standard documents, answers in French, hands over a sample the same week, and takes responsibility when something goes wrong. **You are the missing local half of La Dolce India.** The plan below uses that advantage without exposing you to stock, credit and recall risk before you have proof of demand.

| Phase | When | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Agent and sample library | Oct–Dec 2026 | Register as agent commercial. Get the EORI anyway (free) so a consolidated sample kit can reach you. Sign the agency contract. Work SIAL, Cosmetic 360 and Fi Europe. Run 100–300 targeted contacts | Costs about €25; tests demand; builds the pipeline |
| 2. Lead with the easy wins | From November | Push **cold-pressed castor oil, pure henna, multani mitti, amla and moringa leaf powder** to natural-cosmetics and DIY brands, and **turmeric** to small spice packers. Hold ashwagandha, shilajit and curcumin 95% for supplement brands with their own regulatory teams | India is the natural origin. Raw cosmetic materials sold in bulk need no CPNP filing, and unmodified oils are REACH-exempt. Ashwagandha faces a possible Dutch ban and an EU review |
| 3. Recruit one EU importer partner | Dec 2026–Mar 2027 | Sign up one French or Dutch importer or distributor (for example Jacarandas, NEVER DY, Unipex, Nedspice or Catz) to stock La Dolce India lines, so small clients buy domestically | Solves the problem that small buyers will not import from India |
| 4. Own stock, only on proof | When 2–3 small clients want repeat lots | Create a SASU (about €200–226), get its EORI and VAT number, sign a customs mandate, take liability insurance, and import a "hero" range for delivery from Paris | You become the food or cosmetic operator only when margins justify it |

The cosmetic-first logic is partly inference and deserves an honest note. EU food border checks (Regulation 2019/1793) cover food and feed. Henna, clay and castor oil imported and sold as cosmetic raw materials should therefore avoid CHED-D formalities and the spice recall history. They also escape CPNP filing when sold in bulk to a brand ([Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/importateur-cosmetique-info)), and unmodified oils are exempt from REACH ([ECHA](https://echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/2324906/annex_v_en.pdf)). That makes them the lightest products for a first-time importer to handle later. The buyers still demand heavy-metal results for clay and PPD-free statements for henna. Turmeric stays in the plan because it is India's strongest food card, but **let established importers carry the border and recall risk on food spices**.

The arithmetic also argues for higher-value lines and for volume through importers. On a container of turmeric worth an assumed €30,000 FOB, a 3% commission (an assumption, at the introducer end of the cited ranges) earns about €900 before social contributions of 21–26%. A commission business built only on commodity spices needs many containers. A few hundred kilos of cosmetic-grade oils or standardised extracts can pay as much as a container of turmeric. That is why the cosmetic and supplement buyers in the prospect table matter more than the ethnic food channel. **Get one thing right before any outreach: your brother's website must work and show his IEC, CRES and FSSAI numbers**, because the earlier research found the La Dolce India domain did not load, and every French buyer will check it.

## Conclusion

The question "what licence do I need?" turns out to have a reassuring answer and an important hidden one. The paperwork to become an importer in France is free, online and takes about a month. The hard part is the responsibility that comes with it. Since January 2026, every French customs identity starts from a registered business. Since June 2023, Indian spices meet customs inspectors rather than food inspectors at the border. And most ethylene-oxide problems surface as recalls after the goods are sold, landing on whoever imported them. For someone starting from zero, the smartest licence is therefore the cheapest one: a €25 agent registration that lets you sell, earn and learn while professional importers carry the liability.

The deeper insight is that "direct delivery" is a question of who carries the legal weight in the EU, not of logistics. Every workable route puts an EU-established business in that role: the client, an EU importer, or eventually your own company. DDP by an Indian company has become the hardest option just as small French buyers would most like it. That gap is where you fit. A Paris-based agent who recruits one EU importer partner, keeps a sample library and leads with cosmetic-grade botanicals offers French buyers something an Indian exporter alone cannot. Over the next three months, watch for the FTA signature in December, the decision on India's organic recognition before 31 December, and the Dutch ashwagandha consultation closing on 7 December. Each could change which products you lead with in 2027.
