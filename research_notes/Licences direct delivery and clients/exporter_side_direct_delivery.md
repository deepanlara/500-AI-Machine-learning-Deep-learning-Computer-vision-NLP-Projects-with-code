## 1. Indian exporter-side registrations/certificates: what is needed, how long, how much

### Takeaway
The minimum legal stack for an Indian company to export these goods is: **IEC (₹500, ~1–3 working days, lifetime with free annual update)** → **GST registration + LUT (free, ARN on filing, per financial year)** → for spices, **Spices Board CRES (mandatory; ₹11,800 merchant / ₹17,700 manufacturer incl. GST, 3-year block, ~1–3 weeks)**; **RCMC from CHEMEXCIL / Pharmexcil / SHEFEXIL is only needed to claim incentives**; **FSSAI central licence** is widely described as required for food exporters (₹7,500/yr; statutory 60 days, in practice 1–4 months), though the regulation text is narrower. Per shipment to the EU, spices listed in Annex II of Reg. 2019/1793 need a **Spices Board official certificate backed by EtO/pesticide lab results (EtO test ~₹2,750, ~4 days)**; a **phytosanitary certificate does not appear legally required for dried/ground spices for consumption** (unconfirmed — no official text found either way); origin is self-certified via **REX** (free); organic needs **NPOP certification + per-consignment e-COI in TRACES**, with India's EU equivalence legally expiring **31 Dec 2026** unless the pending extension is adopted.

### Cited Findings

**IEC (DGFT)**
- Government fee ₹500, paid online on DGFT portal — [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/iec-code-charges); [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/iec-code-charges). Outlier: Qwikfilings lists ₹500 for individuals/proprietors/HUFs but ₹1,000 for partnerships, companies, LLPs — [Qwikfilings](https://qwikfilings.com/?p=278950) (conflicts with most sources; verify on dgft.gov.in).
- Issuance: "1 to 3 working days" ([Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/import-export-code-iec-registration)); "1–2 working days" ([StartupGrants India](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/services/iec-code-registration)); "3–5 working days" ([DisyTax](https://disytax.com/iec-registration/)). Certificate generated "instantly" only after ~1 day processing ([SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/iec-renewal)).
- Lifetime validity, no renewal fee, but mandatory free annual update between 1 April and 30 June; IEC deactivated on 1 July if missed, reactivable on portal — [Taxbuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/why-iec-has-lifetime-validity-and-no-renewal-requirement); [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/iec-renewal); [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/iec-renewal).

**GST + LUT (export without IGST)**
- LUT filed on GST portal in Form GST RFD-11; ARN generated on submission with DSC/EVC; no physical filing; no government fee; LUT must be furnished *prior to export*; FY 2026-27 module enabled — [Taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=717); [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/gstn-update-lut-filing-for-fy-2026-27-now-live-on-gst-portal-check-details-and-step-by-step-guide-409580.html); [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/gst-lut-filing-fy-2026-27).
- LUT "deemed accepted" on ARN generation (citing Circular 40/14/2018-GST) — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=113578) (not verified against official text).
- Valid for the whole FY in which tendered ([CHEMEXCIL circular relaying Circular 8/8/2017-GST](https://chemexcil.in/circulars/lut---renewal-of-letter-of-undertaking--lut--for-export--for-fy-2018-19----facility-of--online-furnishing-of---letter-of-undertaking-on-gst-portal-for-export-of-goods-or-services-for-fy-2018-19/3352/fc1d808a01ecb5bd6fb964824cbd687d.html)); contradicted in part by commentary saying a mid-year LUT is valid only from filing date, not retrospectively ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/lut-letter-of-undertaking-gst)).
- Rule 96A: goods must leave India within 3 months of export invoice, else IGST + 18% interest — [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/gst-lut-filing-fy-2026-27).

**Spices Board CRES (Certificate of Registration as Exporter of Spices)**
- Board's online CRES form lists: manufacturer exporter new ₹17,700 (₹15,000 + 18% GST); merchant exporter new ₹11,800 (₹10,000 + GST); renewal ₹11,800 (manufacturer) / ₹8,850 (merchant); amendment ₹5,900; application via DGFT portal — [Spices Board CRES form](https://indianspices.org.in/CRES_new/e-r-o/exporters-registration/form); regulation amendment (25 Oct 2021) kept ₹15,000/₹10,000 — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/11/04/spices-board-registration-of-exporters-amendment-regulations-2021/). Lower figures (₹5,000) on some consultancy sites appear to come from an old demand-draft process — [Spices Board FAQ](https://indianspices.com/hin/sites/default/files/faq.pdf).
- Validity: 3-year block; renewal can be filed up to 2 months before expiry; late renewal → fresh registration; no exports in the block → renewal refused — [2021-24 CRES circular](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Circular%20on%20Registration%20and%20Guidelines%20for%20CRES%202021-24%20dt%2027%20Feb%202021-24%201%20Mar%202021%20updated%20on%2022%20Mar.pdf); [Spices Board FAQ](https://indianspices.com/hin/sites/default/files/faq.pdf).
- Processing: Board FAQ says about a week after a complete application; consultancy says 10–15 working days or more if manufacturer inspection is needed — [Spices Board FAQ](https://indianspices.com/hin/sites/default/files/faq.pdf); [Agile Regulatory](https://www.agileregulatory.com/blogs/spice-board-registration-in-india).
- CRES is required under s.11 of the Spices Board Act for the 52 scheduled spices and needs a valid IEC first [workflow snippet] — [Spices Board Circular 03/2025-26](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Circular%20No%2003%202025%2026.pdf).
- No 2024-27 block circular was found; fees may have changed — (gap noted by search; see [Spices Board ESS](https://www.indianspices.org.in/ESS)).

**RCMC (CHEMEXCIL / Pharmexcil / SHEFEXIL / APEDA / FIEO)**
- CHEMEXCIL covers dyes, cosmetics, essential oils, specialty chemicals and castor oil; India is world's largest castor oil producer/exporter — [Hubco](https://www.hubco.in/answers/basic-chemicals-pharmaceuticals-cosmetics-epc-chemexcil-export-promotional-council-epc); [CHEMEXCIL castor page](https://chemexcil.in/castor-oil-and-its-derivatives-industry).
- Pharmexcil purview includes Herbal/Ayurveda and Nutraceuticals — [Agile Regulatory](https://www.agileregulatory.com/service/pharmexcil-registration); SHEFEXIL (Shellac & Forest Products EPC) is described as nodal agency for nutraceuticals, extracts/botanicals, herbs; ~860 products — [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/shellac-export-promotion-council); [Fi Europe exhibitor listing](https://visitor.figlobal.com/event/fi-europe-2024/exhibitor/RXhoaWJpdG9yXzE4NDMyMjI=). A botanical extract maker has won export awards from both Pharmexcil and SHEFEXIL — [SupplySide](https://www.supplysidesj.com/specialty-nutrients/olive-lifesciences-wins-shefexil-export-award).
- Henna powder is HS 1404 10 19 (vegetable materials used in dyeing); henna-based hair dye is HS 3305 90 40 — [Ximpex](https://ximpex.in/hs-codes/14041019/china); [Seair](https://www.seair.co.in/henna-powder-export-data/hs-code-33059040.aspx). No official source found allocating henna to one council.
- RCMC comes from the council covering the declared main line of business; products not covered by any council → FIEO; drugs/pharma RCMC must be from Pharmexcil (DGFT PN 61/2004-09) — [howtoexportimport](https://howtoexportimport.com/How-to-get-RCMC-from-Export-Promotion-Councils-197.aspx); [RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/rcmc-from-pharmexcil).
- RCMC is mandatory with CHEMEXCIL *only if the exporter wants government incentives*; RCMC not required for Drawback/RoDTEP/RoSCTL but needed for Advance Authorisation, EPCG — [CHEMEXCIL FAQ](https://chemexcil.in/content/faqs); [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4943).
- RCMC applications go through DGFT's common e-RCMC portal; DGFT says issued for 5 financial years, while CHEMEXCIL membership lapses each 31 March (annual subscription) — [DGFT e-RCMC](https://dgft.gov.in/CP/?opt=e-rcmc); [CHEMEXCIL fee circular](https://chemexcil.in/circulars/revision-in-the-chemexcil-membership-subscription-fees-from----1st-april-2022-based-on-export-turnover-of-the-company/5606/906428acd4fb6f5f62986418d4a498c0.html).
- CHEMEXCIL fees are turnover-based; lowest band ₹7,500 + 18% GST — [CHEMEXCIL fee structure](https://chemexcil.in/uploads/files/FEES_STRUCTURE.pdf). APEDA registration cited as ₹10,000 one-time (blog figure) — [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4943).

**FSSAI licence for export**
- Central licence fee ₹7,500/yr (up to 5 years, ₹37,500 upfront) — [Corpseed](https://www.corpseed.com/knowledge-centre/fssai-central-license); [StartupGrants India](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/services/fssai-central-license/fees).

**Spices Board EtO / pesticide testing & EU official certificate (Reg. 2019/1793)**
- Under Art. 11 of Reg. 2019/1793 each consignment of listed spices must carry an official certificate (Annex IV model) signed/stamped by an authorised Spices Board officer; signatory list revised by circular dated 26 Feb 2026 — [Spices Board Circular 07/2024-25](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Circular%20No.7%202024%20-%2025.pdf); [Circular 18 Authorised Signatory](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Circular%2018%20Authorised%20Signatory%20HC_22_11_2024.pdf).
- Spices Board EtO official certificate is "a mandatory requirement for clearance of the spice consignments at the destination ports in the EU" (2022 circular) — [FSSAI-hosted Spices Board circular](https://cdd.fssai.gov.in/viewFile.php?id=128). Circular 05/2022-23 made a cleared EtO report a precondition for exporting notified spices to the EU (following Reg. 2021/2246); limit 0.1 mg/kg for spice mixes — [Spices Board mandatory sampling notice](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Mandatory%20Sampling%20&%20Testing%20of%20export%20consignments%20of%20Spices%20&%20Spice%20products%20under%20the%20Quality%20Evaluation%20System%20of%20Spices%20Board.pdf); [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/news/spices-board-mandatory-sampling-and-testing-of-export-consignments-of-spices-and-spice-products/).
- Exporters file sampling intimation through the Board's online Export Support System (ESS); analytical and health-certificate fees payable online (Circular 15/2023-24) — [Spices Board ESS](https://www.indianspices.org.in/ESS); [Tender notice](https://indianspices.com/indianspices/hin/sites/default/files/Tender%20Notice-SRD_0.pdf).
- Mumbai QEL Form 75 (Oct 2023): EtO (sum of EtO + 2-chloroethanol) ₹2,750, 250 g sample, 4 days; turnaround lengthens with sample inflow — [Mumbai QEL Form 75](https://www.Indianspices.Com/sites/default/files/MumbaiQELFORMNo75_2020.pdf); [Guntur form](https://www.Indianspices.Com/sites/default/files/GunturQELFORMNo75_2020.pdf).
- Cumin: Circular 03/2025-26 adds mandatory pesticide-residue testing for EU cumin; EtO report issued only if both EtO and pesticide limits met — [Circular 03/2025-26](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/Circular%20No%2003%202025%2026.pdf).
- Board's Oct 2024 EtO SOP asks exporters to test raw materials, processing aids, packaging and finished goods — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/News/spices-board-issues-guidelines-for-exporters-to-mitigate-ethylene-oxide-contamination/); [EtO SoP](https://indianspices.com/indianspices/sites/default/files/EtO%20SoP%20v.pdf).

### Inferences
- Realistic exporter-side lead time to be "shipment-ready" from scratch: IEC (≤1 week) + GST/LUT (days, assuming GST already exists) + CRES (1–3 weeks) + FSSAI central licence (2–4 months, the critical path) + optional RCMC (weeks). If organic is required, NPOP certification of a trader adds months. (Inference from cited timings.)
- Annex I vs Annex II matters for the French buyer: Annex II (official certificate + lab results per consignment) vs Annex I (no exporter certificate, but CHED-D pre-notification and a higher % of identity/physical checks at the French border control post). After 2026/194 and 2026/1206, several Indian spice lines moved from Annex II to Annex I, lowering Indian paperwork but not border-check risk. **The exact current Annex I/II list for India must be read in the consolidated 2019/1793 on EUR-Lex** before quoting. (Inference.)
- [BK – verify] Spices covered by Ch. 09 of the EU Common Customs Tariff mostly carry 0% MFN duty, so GSP loss or FTA staging matters less for raw spices than for oils/extracts. Essential oils (HS 3301) sit in GSP Section VI (chemicals), which is likely among the suspended sections → MFN rates apply. Clay (Ch. 25, mineral products) likely also suspended. Not verified line by line.
- [BK – verify] For henna/clay/castor oil sold as finished **cosmetics**, the EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009 requires an EU "Responsible Person" + CPNP notification; bulk raw materials sold B2B to a cosmetics maker generally do not need CPNP (consistent with [workflow snippet, Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/importateur-cosmetique-info)). REACH applies to the EU importer at ≥1 t/yr, with Annex V entry 9 exempting unmodified vegetable oils ([workflow snippet, ECHA](https://echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/2324906/annex_v_en.pdf)).
- Phytosanitary: on the evidence found, dried/ground spices for consumption are probably not in Annex XI Part A (the spice entry found is "for planting"), so no EU-mandated PC; many buyers/forwarders still ask for one "for safety", and PQIS can issue on request. Whole seeds (cumin, coriander, fennel) are the grey zone — check TARIC document codes for the exact CN code. (Inference; no official EU text confirming either way.)

## 2. Model 1 — French client is importer of record (FCA/FOB/CFR/CIF/DAP)

### Takeaway
This is the normal, simplest model: the Indian company invoices the French client, ships under FCA/FOB/CFR/CIF (or DAP), and the **French client (with its EORI, intra-EU VAT number and customs broker) clears the goods, self-assesses import VAT, handles CHED-D/border checks, and is the EU food business operator legally responsible for the product and its labelling**. Professional buyers prefer it because they recover import VAT via autoliquidation, control their own broker/forwarder, and are legally the responsible EU operator anyway.

### Cited Findings
- EORI: required before first import; free; only via SOPRANO EORI on douane.gouv.fr; active 24 h after issue; paid "EORI services" are scams; since 1 Jan 2026 France accepts only SIREN-based EORI (SIRET-based no longer valid) — [douane.gouv.fr EORI](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/service-en-ligne/numero-eori-fr); [douane.gouv.fr EORI SIREN](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/towards-single-eori-siren-and-european-portal-customs-authorisations); [douane scam warning](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/actualites/tentatives-darnaques-la-demande-de-numero-eori).
- Import VAT autoliquidation (self-assessment on CA3 return instead of paying at customs) mandatory for VAT-identified operators since 1 Jan 2022; requires a valid intra-EU VAT number — [douane.gouv.fr TVA à l'importation](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/fiche/tva-limportation-les-essentiels); [BOFiP](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/node/374792). Micro-entrepreneurs under VAT franchise cannot recover import VAT; can hand declarations to a registered customs representative (freight forwarder/express carrier) [workflow snippet] — [service-public F21919](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F21919).
- Under delivered-at-place terms the French buyer is the official importer using its own EORI and VAT number — [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/en/importing-into-france/) (via snippet).
- Since 1 June 2023, French customs (DGDDI) — not DGCCRF — carries out import sanitary controls on food of non-animal origin; DGCCRF handles post-clearance market controls — [douane.gouv.fr news](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/actualites/transfert-la-douane-des-controles-limportation-realises-par-la-dgccrf-au-havre-et); [douane cartographie](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/controles-sanitaires-et-de-qualite-des-aliments-cartographie-des-services-competents).
- Products listed in 2019/1793 must enter via a border control post with CHED-D in TRACES NT, pre-notified ≥1 working day before arrival (Reg. 2019/1013); sanitary checks precede any customs procedure — [douane.gouv.fr "Vous importez"](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/professionnels/commerce-international/controles-sanitaires-et-de-qualite-des-aliments/vous-importez); [FLI CHED-D guide](https://www.traces.fli.de/Dokus/Anleitung%20f%C3%BCr%20Beh%C3%B6rden%20von%20Kontrollstellen%20(CHED-D).pdf); [Strong & Herd](https://strongandherd.co.uk/updates-to-imports-of-high-risk-food-and-feed-of-non-animal-origin-eu-2019-1793).
- Food information responsibility: Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 8(1) — the FBO under whose name the food is marketed, **or, if not established in the Union, the importer into the Union market**; Art. 9(1)(h) name/address of that FBO on label; PO boxes not acceptable — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/article/8/data.htm); [IFSQN](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/34836-question-about-the-responsibilities-11692011/?ampmode=1); [GS1 Sweden](https://gs1.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/01/contact-information-guideline-1.pdf).
- Most EtO notifications on Indian spices were recalled from the market after border clearance, meaning the French importer of record carries the recall risk [workflow snippet] — [DFI Budapest](https://dfi.univet.hu/en/?p=10695).
- Food supplements: the importer that first places supplements on the French market has manufacturer-equivalent responsibility and must declare on DGAL's Compl'Alim (replaced TeleIcare, Sept 2025) under décret 2006-352 arts. 15–18 [workflow snippet] — [DAAF Réunion note](https://daaf.reunion.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/communication_complements_alimentaires.pdf); [Ecomundo](https://ecomundo.eu/blog/declaration-mise-sur-le-marche-complement-alimentaire-france).
- Food business registration (Reg. 852/2004) for plant-only traders: Cerfa 13984 covers food of animal origin; plant-only traders should ask local DDPP which form applies [workflow snippet] — [service-public R17520](https://entreprendre.service-public.fr/vosdroits/R17520).
- Dutch analogue: importers of herbs and spices must register with NVWA as a food company — [KVK](https://kvk.nl/en/international/importing-herbs-and-spices).

### Inferences
- Step-by-step (Model 1, sea FCL/LCL, CIF/CFR example): (1) PO/proforma + payment terms (often 30–50% advance on first deals, LC or CAD for larger); (2) Indian exporter: pre-shipment sampling/testing via Spices Board ESS (if listed), official certificate (Annex II items), CoA, REX statement on invoice, NPOP TC/e-COI if organic; (3) Indian CHA files shipping bill (LUT, no IGST), goods loaded; (4) Exporter sends B/L, invoice, packing list, certificates; for Annex I/II items the French broker pre-notifies CHED-D in TRACES ≥1 working day before arrival at the BCP (Le Havre/Marseille/Roissy etc.); (5) Documentary/identity/physical checks at BCP (sampling holds can take days–weeks); (6) customs release with the client's EORI, import VAT autoliquidated on CA3; (7) client is EU FBO for traceability, labelling, recalls. (Synthesis of cited items + [BK].)
- [BK – verify] Incoterms 2020: FCA is technically more correct than FOB for containerised cargo; with FOB/FCA the buyer controls freight; CIF/CFR leaves freight with the exporter but risk passes at loading in India; DAP = exporter pays freight to destination but buyer still clears and pays import VAT/duties. Professional buyers like FCA/FOB because they use negotiated freight contracts and their own broker.
- The brother can be involved as agent/introducer without being importer (see Model 4) — the French client must itself be a French-established FBO with EORI + VAT number.

- No source on typical BCP inspection hold times at Le Havre/Marseille for Indian spices.

## 3. Model 2 — DDP by the Indian exporter (exporter is importer of record in France)

### Takeaway
Legally possible but heavy, and especially awkward for food. Since **1 Jan 2026 the "représentation fiscale ponctuelle" (one-off fiscal representation, used for régime 42) is gone**, so a non-EU seller importing in its own name needs **its own EORI + French VAT number**, a **customs representative acting in indirect representation (jointly liable, express written mandate required per Cour de cassation 11 Feb 2026)**, and normally an **accredited fiscal representative** — although **India appears on the French list of countries exempt from the mandatory fiscal-representative rule** (conflicting sources). A lighter "mandataire à l'international" (art. 289 A bis CGI, since 21 Feb 2025) can handle VAT formalities for a non-VAT-identified importer. For food, the DDP seller would also be the importer/FBO under Reg. 1169/2011 and responsible for CHED-D, rejections and recalls, without an EU establishment — which is why it is rarely done for food ingredients. No public price lists for fiscal representation/IOR were found.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 289 A CGI: non-EU businesses owing French VAT must appoint an accredited fiscal representative established in France; accreditation conditions tightened by Finance Law 2022 (solvency) — [douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/fiche/identification-la-tva-et-representation-fiscale); [CCI Paris IdF](https://www.entreprises.cci-paris-idf.fr/fiches-pratiques/quand-et-comment-designer-un-representant-fiscal); [Fiscalonline](https://fiscalonline.com/Entreprise/nouvelles-modalites-de-delivrance-et-de-retrait-de-l-accreditation-des-representants-fiscaux-).
- Exemption: no fiscal representative needed for entities in non-EU states with a mutual-assistance instrument comparable to Dir. 2010/24 / Reg. 904/2010; list fixed by arrêté of 15 May 2013 (updated Feb 2021, adding UK, Turkey; reportedly 2025); a consultancy list includes **India** (without the "new addition" asterisk) — [Mathez Compliance list](https://www.mathez-compliance.com/liste-pays-societes-redevables-tva-pas-de-rf-en-france/); [Deloitte Avocats](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/mise-a-jour-de-la-liste-des-pays-non-ue-dont-les-assujettis-sont-dispenses-de-designer-un-representant-fiscal-en-france/); [Hayot Expertise](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/representant-fiscal-france-societe-etrangere). **Contradicted by** Eurofiscalis/Customs Support who say every non-EU importer now needs own French VAT number *and* an accredited fiscal representative — [Customs Support](https://www.customssupport.com/customs-procedure-42-changes-ahead-in-2026-what-non-eu-businesses-need-to-know/); [workflow snippet, Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/en/selling-ddp-france/).
- Représentation fiscale ponctuelle abolished by Finance Law 2024 (art. 112) effective 1 Jan 2025, tolerated until 31 Dec 2025; ponctual reps' VAT numbers invalid from 1 Jan 2026; forwarders can no longer clear régime 42 under their own number; administration (3 Dec 2025 doctrine) says the ponctual mandate is not open to non-EU entities even from mutual-assistance countries — [KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html); [BOFiP BOI-RES-TVA-000211](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/14724-PGP.html/identifiant=BOI-RES-TVA-000211-20251203); [VATcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/france/france-ends-vat-simplification-for-non-eu-importers/).
- Mandataire à l'international (art. 289 A bis CGI; décret 2025-153, in force 21 Feb 2025): an importer not VAT-identified in France may appoint the person physically holding its goods to do VAT formalities without accreditation, keeping import VAT deduction; owner stays responsible; not usable once a French VAT number is assigned; not usable for régime 42; BOFiP draft doctrine open for comment to 31 Mar 2026 — [KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html); [BOFiP BOI-TVA-DECLA-20-30-40-30](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/14755-PGP.html/identifiant=BOI-TVA-DECLA-20-30-40-30-20260211); [Weblex](https://www.weblex.fr/weblex-actualite/entreprises-a-l-etranger-et-tva-le-point-sur-les-mecanismes-de-representation-en-france).
- Without a valid intra-EU VAT number, autoliquidation is unavailable (VAT paid at clearance); non-EU importer must ask the SIE for a French VAT number — [BOFiP](https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/node/374792); [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/numero-tva-france/).
- Indirect customs representation: representative acts in own name for the client and is jointly liable for duties and import VAT; direct representation reserved to registered customs representatives — [douane BOD DA 04-060](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/reglementation/france/doctrine/decisions-administratives/da-04-060); [douane BOD](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/bod/src/dana/da/F1_04-060.doc). Non-EU operator clearing in France must use indirect representation (CGI art. 293 A) [workflow snippet] — [douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/fiche/identification-la-tva-et-representation-fiscale).
- Cour de cassation, 11 Feb 2026: customs representation must be an express written mandate; otherwise the forwarder can become personally liable for import VAT — [Mathez Compliance](https://www.mathez-compliance.com/france-rde-mandat/).
- Workaround cited: enter via another member state, e.g., Netherlands still allows fiscal representation for non-EU companies — [KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html). Dutch fiscal reps must provide financial security; Article 23 import-VAT deferment licence needs a representative — [Commenda NL](https://www.commenda.io/netherlands/fiscal-representation); [Hellotax](https://hellotax.com/blog/fiscal-representation-in-the-netherlands/).
- Costs: no public tariff found for French accredited fiscal reps or indirect representation; IOR fees quoted per shipment flat or as % of value; fiscal representation usually annual fee + deposit/guarantee; one provider cites €800–1,000/quarter for small companies (country not stated) — [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/?p=7087); [Commenda](https://www.commenda.io/france/fiscal-representation); [Gerlach](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/cost-of-vat-compliance-eu/).
- Food FBO problem: customs IOR is not automatically the EU food business operator named on the label; an IOR/3PL must contractually accept FBO duties [workflow snippet] — [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/importer-of-record-france/); Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 8(1) makes the EU importer responsible when the marketer is non-EU — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/1169/article/8/data.htm).
- Future: Directive (EU) 2025/1539 aims to shift import VAT to non-established suppliers/platforms from 1 July 2028 — [KPMG Avocats](https://kpmg.com/av/fr/avocats/eclairages/2025/12/importateurs-non-etablis-dans-l-ue-ce-qui-change-au-1er-janvier-2026.html).

### Inferences
- Step-by-step DDP (if attempted): Indian company obtains EU EORI (from French customs as first place of import) → French VAT number via SIE (and an accredited fiscal rep unless the India exemption is confirmed) → signs written indirect-representation mandate with a French broker (who will demand a guarantee/deposit because of joint VAT liability) → appoints an EU-established party willing to be FBO/label address → files CHED-D, clears, autoliquidates import VAT on monthly CA3 → resells to French client. [BK – verify]: the onward domestic B2B supply by a non-established seller to a French VAT-registered buyer is normally reverse-charged to the buyer (art. 283-1 CGI), but the import leg still creates French VAT filing obligations for the Indian seller.
- Practical verdict: for a small first trade, DDP adds a fiscal/customs cost base (likely a few thousand euros a year plus per-shipment fees — inference from the cited €800–1,000/quarter anecdote) and exposes the Indian exporter to French border rejection/destruction and recall liabilities; most food buyers do not ask for DDP. Use only if a large client insists, and then preferably via an IOR/3PL that is also a registered FBO, or via a Dutch fiscal representative.
- The India exemption from the fiscal-rep obligation (if confirmed on Légifrance) would lower cost, but a French VAT number and indirect customs representation would still be needed.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm on Légifrance that India is on the arrêté list (and whether 2025 update changed it).
- No published French prices for accredited fiscal representation, indirect representation guarantees or IOR services for food.
- No source on whether DDPP/DGAL accept a non-EU company as the registered FBO.

## 4. Model 3 — French/EU importer, distributor, 3PL/IOR partner, or Dutch/German importer buying containers

### Takeaway
The most scalable route for an Indian exporter without an EU entity is to **sell (FOB/CIF) to an established EU importer/distributor that takes title, clears, holds stock and resells** — including in France. The Netherlands is the EU's spice import/processing/re-export gateway (≈116,400 t imported in 2025) and Germany the largest spice importer by value; CBI recommends multi-country importers as the first target. A pure customs IOR/3PL service solves customs but not the EU food-operator role unless contractually accepted.

### Cited Findings
- Netherlands imported ~116,400 t of spices/herbs in 2025, 57% from low/middle-income countries; Dutch companies import from India, China, Vietnam, Indonesia, then grind, blend, pack and re-export (Belgium, Germany, Spain key destinations) — [CBI Netherlands](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/netherlands); [CBI market potential](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/netherlands/market-potential).
- Germany largest European spice importer (~€592m in 2021) — [Statista](https://WWW.STATISTA.COM/statistics/1289767/leading-spice-and-herb-importers-europe/).
- "Several hundred" potential importers in Europe; importers often serve several European countries or all of Europe; newcomers should start with them; hubs by product (Spain chillies, NL/UK pepper) — [CBI 8 tips finding buyers](https://www.cbi.eu/node/1017); [CBI 10 tips](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-find-buyers). A Dutch importer can open France, Germany, Belgium and Spain [workflow snippet] — same CBI page.
- Buyer types differ: raw-material importers, packers/processors, distributors of pre-packed goods — different demands on processing, packaging, order size, lead time — [CBI 7 tips doing business](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-do-business).
- Direct trade between medium/large origin exporters and European grinders has grown, historically bulk went via a few brokers/trader-importers; many single spices imported in full container loads; spice-mix makers import FCLs but sell in lots of a few hundred kg to 1 t — [CBI research PDFs](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1092.pdf); [CBI 2700](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/2700.pdf).
- Leads from Nov 2025 Indonesian trade mission to NL: Amboina, Nesia Food BV, Bina BV, INA Trading — [RRI](https://rri.co.id/en/business/1952606/indonesia-s-spice-trade-mission-to-netherlands-generates-usd-14-6-million) (leads, not verified buyers).
- Botanical extracts for supplements: importers/distributors are the main entry channel; buyers expect SDS, TDS, CoA up front [workflow snippet] — [CBI natural ingredients for health products](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-health-products/finding-buyers).
- IOR basics: foreign seller without EU presence often cannot practically be IOR; options are a third-party IOR or making the EU client the IOR; indirect representative jointly liable — [Euridex](https://euridex.com/?p=4315); [24hour-AR](https://www.24hour-ar.com/importer-of-record); [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/help/eu-imports-faq/).
- NL importers of food must register with NVWA; organic importers with Skal — [KVK standard steps](https://kvk.nl/en/international/standard-steps-of-importing-into-the-netherlands); [KVK organic](https://www.kvk.nl/en/international/importing-organic-products/).
- European Spice Association member lists and national associations are CBI-recommended contact sources; fairs: Fi Europe (17–19 Nov 2026, Frankfurt), SIAL Paris (17–21 Oct 2026), BIOFACH (16–19 Feb 2027), Vitafoods Europe (18–20 May 2027, Barcelona) [workflow snippet] — [CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-find-buyers); [SIAL](https://www.sialparis.com/en/pratical-info/dates-venue-opening-times); [Import Promotion Desk](https://www.importpromotiondesk.de/en/news-detail/food-ingredients-europe-fie-2026/); [Vitafoods](https://www.vitafoods.eu.com/en/visit.html).

### Inferences
- Model 3 variants: (a) **distributor/importer buys outright** (FOB/CIF) and resells to the French client — cleanest; importer margin typically sits between exporter and end client; (b) **French client orders, but a Dutch/French importer imports and invoices** (back-to-back) — the importer earns a handling margin; (c) **3PL/IOR service** imports in its name for the Indian exporter's account and stores stock in a French/Dutch bonded or duty-paid warehouse, then delivers to French clients — needs the 3PL to accept FBO role and a VAT solution (fiscal rep or NL Art. 23 licence); (d) **Dutch/German importer buys full containers** and the French client buys from them — exporter only needs FOB/CIF capability.
- [BK – verify] Well-known European spice importers/processors include Catz International (Rotterdam), Verstegen and Euroma (NL), Fuchs/Ostmann/Kotányi (DE/AT), and in France McCormick/Ducros, Sabarot, Thiercelin, Cook/Arcadie; these are background names only — not verified in this research.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying importer/distributor margins or 3PL storage/IOR fees for food in France or NL.
- No current (2025–26) CBI Germany spice page found.

## 5. Model 4 — Brother as commercial agent / apporteur d'affaires earning commission

### Takeaway
The brother can legally earn commission from the Indian exporter without importing: he registers a French business (micro-entreprise; **agent commercial** additionally registers on the RSAC), invoices the Indian company (**French VAT out of scope for a B2B service to an Indian taxable company — mention "TVA non applicable, art. 259-1 du CGI"**), receives payment by bank transfer (SWIFT), and declares it in France (most sources: **BNC**; micro-BNC 34% allowance; 2042-C-PRO). In India, commission to an overseas agent can be remitted through the AD bank (old RBI framework: declared on shipping bill, paid by remittance or deduction from invoice; benchmark 12.5% with higher on application) — but **new FEMA Export & Import Regulations/Directions 2026 took effect 1 Oct 2026** and their agency-commission text was not found. Indian income-tax: case law mostly says no TDS on commission for services rendered abroad, but contested.

### Cited Findings
- Agent commercial: permanent mandatary negotiating/concluding sales in principal's name (Code de commerce L.134-1 ff.), must register on RSAC (~€25–27); apporteur d'affaires: unregulated, one-off introductions, can be micro-entrepreneur [workflow snippet] — [Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/encyclopedie/structures-juridiques/statuts-particuliers/intermediaires-du-commerce-agent-commercial); [Bpifrance micro-entreprise](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/encyclopedie/micro-entreprise-regime-auto-entrepreneur/lessentiel-ce-regime/ou-comment-sinscrire).
- Agent commercial entitled to end-of-contract indemnity (custom ≈ 2 years' commissions); indicative commission ranges ~1–5% apporteur, 10–25% agent; regular negotiation by an apporteur risks requalification as agent [workflow snippet] — [Captain Contrat](https://www.captaincontrat.com/articles-droit-commercial/agent-commercial-apporteur-daffaires).
- VAT place of supply: service to a taxable customer located where the customer is established (art. 259 CGI) → commission to an Indian business is outside French VAT scope; intermediaries acting in the name and for account of another for operations outside the EU exempt (art. 263 CGI) — [Advizexperts art. 259](https://advizexperts.fr/code-general-impots/article-259-cgi-lieu-prestations-services-tva/); [BOFiP BOI-TVA-CHAMP-20-50](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/reglementation/france/doctrine/bofip/bofip-boi-tva-champ-20-50); [TheTradeHub BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-30-40](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/reglementation/france/doctrine/bofip/bofip-boi-tva-champ-30-30-40). Invoice mention "TVA non applicable – article 259-1 du CGI"; VAT franchise thresholds €37,500 / €41,250 (services) — [Copeps](https://copeps.fr/actualites/auto-entrepreneur-tva-client-etranger/); [Portail auto-entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/academie/fiches-metiers/commerce-vente/apporteur-affaires).
- Income tax: most apporteurs fall under BNC (micro-BNC allowance 34%); one site classifies micro-BIC (ceiling €83,600 in 2026, social contributions 21.2%); declare turnover on 2042-C-PRO; foreign-source BNC on form 2047; declare foreign accounts (e.g., Wise/PayPal) — [Le Blog du Dirigeant](https://www.leblogdudirigeant.com/apporteur-daffaire-et-fiscalite/); [Portail auto-entrepreneur](https://www.portail-autoentrepreneur.fr/actualites/apporteur-affaires-auto-entrepreneur/); [impots.gouv brochure](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/www2/fichiers/documentation/brochure/ir_2026/pdf_som/20-revenus_etranger_365a368.pdf); [Abby](https://abby.fr/blog/remplir-declaration-impot-micro-entreprise/).
- Commission invoice must identify the intermediary (name, address, business name) — [Copeps](https://copeps.fr/actualites/ajouter-la-tva-sur-des-commissions/); [L-Expert-Comptable](https://l-expert-comptable.com/a/facturation-apporteur-d-affaires).
- India FEMA: RBI's FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 + Directions (dated 13/16 Jan 2026) effective **1 Oct 2026**, superseding the 2016 Master Direction on exports and 167 circulars; export realisation period cut from 15 to 9 months (12 months for INR invoicing) — [AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/rbi-issues-foreign-exchange-management-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services-regulations-2026-and-directions-on-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services/); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2026/03/rbi-issues-exim-guidelines); [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/international-trade-investment/1780898/rbi-issues-foreign-exchange-management-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services-regulations-2026-and-directions-on-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services).

## 6. Samples — sending 100 g–1 kg samples to French prospects legally

### Takeaway
Small food samples are legal and routine. On the Indian side, ship by courier/Speed Post with IEC, a commercial/pro-forma invoice showing a **customs value** and "free sample – no commercial value", within the **₹50,000-per-consignment free-sample definition** (the ₹10 lakh commercial courier cap was removed 1 Apr 2026). On the French side, non-invoiced trade samples qualify for **duty and VAT relief under Art. 86 Reg. 1186/2009** (French customs: DELTA IE, code **C30**, CANA 0066), and **Reg. 2019/1793 does not apply to trade samples not placed on the market below 10 kg net (non-fresh)** — so no CHED-D/official certificate for 100 g–1 kg. The new **€3 flat duty (from 1 July 2026)** applies to low-value postal consignments but samples follow the C30 relief route. Costs: Speed Post ≈ ₹1,250 for 1 kg; express couriers ≈ ₹1,900–4,250 for 0.5–2 kg + 18% GST + fuel surcharge.

### Cited Findings
**India export side**
- IEC mandatory for trade-related shipments including commercial samples; only bona fide personal shipments exempt (DHL India KYC, Dec 2025) — [DHL India KYC norms](https://www.dhl.com/content/dam/dhl/local/in/dhl-express/documents/pdf/KYC%20Norms.pdf).
- Courier export cap of ₹10 lakh per commercial consignment removed (Notifications 33/2026-Cus (NT) and 34/2026-Cus (NT), 31 Mar 2026) — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/india-cbic-removes-rs1m-cap-101524718.html); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/mar/doc2026331837101.pdf); [TEXPROCIL circular](https://texprocil.org/images/circulars/1775198743-Eserve-130_of_2026.pdf).
- Courier regulations define samples as bona fide commercial samples/prototypes supplied free of charge of value ≤ ₹50,000 per consignment, no forex; Blue Dart routes samples > ₹50,000 via freight mode — [Seair (Courier Regs)](https://www.seair.co.in/custom-regulations/courier-imports-and-exports-clearance-regulations-1998-62622.aspx); [Blue Dart](https://api.bluedart.com/international-export) (not verified post-2026 amendment).

- Even free samples need a declared value on the invoice "for customs purposes only" — [Bhatt & Joshi](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/chapter-10-exporting-product-samples/).

**EU / France import side**
- Art. 86 Reg. 1186/2009: samples of negligible value usable only to solicit orders admitted free of import duty; authorities may require them to be rendered unusable/marked; "sample" defined by presentation and quantity — [legislation.gov.uk Art. 86](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2009/1186/article/86?view=plain); [Tulli Finland](https://tulli.fi/en/businesses/import/relief-from-customs-duty/relief-from-customs-duty-on-the-basis-of-the-duty-relief-regulation/samples-of-goods) (declaration still required; cannot be sold/transferred).
- French customs FAQ (2026): non-invoiced samples exempt under Art. 86; cleared via DELTA IE with additional procedure code C30 and CANA 0066 until DELTA H7 integration — [douane FAQ €3 duty](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2026-06/26/FAQ-Droit-de-douane-forfaitaire-de-3-euros.pdf); [douane Q&A](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/fiche/principales-questions-et-reponses-autour-du-droit-de-douane-forfaitaire-de-3-euros).

(EU) 2026/382); €3 flat duty per item/category on postal consignments ≤ €150 from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2028; French national €2 small-parcel tax (from 1 Mar 2026) suspended from 1 July 2026 — [Lexgo](https://www.lexgo.be/en/news-and-articles/14980-the-end-of-the-eur-150-customs-duty-exemption-the-european-union-introduces-a-flat-rate-eur-3-duty-on-small-parcels); [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/particuliers/tout-savoir-sur-la-taxe-sur-les-petits-colis); [Weblex](https://www.weblex.fr/weblex-actualite/petits-colis-importes-la-france-suspend-sa-taxe-nationale).
- Reg. 2019/1793 does not apply to consignments sent as trade samples, laboratory samples or exhibition items not intended to be placed on the market, unless net weight exceeds 50 kg fresh or 10 kg other products (earlier version: 30 kg) — [EUR-Lex consolidated 2019/1793 (2026-02-18)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2019/1793/2026-02-18/eng).
- FedEx: samples often duty/tax-free if low value, for buyer examination, clearly marked "sample" on commercial invoice — [FedEx FR](https://www.fedex.com/fr-fr/customer-support/faq/duties-taxes-imported-goods/when-do-duties-and-taxes-apply/import-duty-on-samples.html). UK guidance for food samples to France: commercial invoice still required with value; reason "sample"; marking e.g. "sample, not for resale"; MS-specific marking rules; a sample may need the same health certificate as the product (dated 2023) — [UK DIT/Issuu](https://issuu.com/ukti/docs/export_food_samples_to_france/s/23148652).
- DHL: restricted items carried only with prior written agreement; foodstuffs are a declared restricted category; some flavourings/chemicals can be dangerous goods — [DHL restricted commodities](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-ca/ship-with-dhl/start-shipping/restricted-commodities); [DHL food shipping](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-vn/ship-with-dhl/services/mastering-international-food-shipping); [Goods Across Borders](https://goodsacrossborders.com/carriers/dhl-express).

**Costs**

- Express couriers 0.5–2 kg: ₹1,890–4,250 depending on carrier/country (2026, multi-country); add 18% GST + 15–25% fuel surcharge — [ClickPost 2026](https://clickpost.ai/blog/international-courier-charges); [ClickPost Canada](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/courier-charges-for-canada).

### Inferences
- Practical tips: (1) keep each sample 100–500 g in sealed, labelled food-grade pouches (product name, botanical name, lot no., origin India, net weight, "Sample – not for resale – no commercial value"); (2) include CoA (incl. EtO/pesticide results if available), spec sheet, SDS for oils/extracts; (3) pro-forma invoice with realistic low value (e.g., €5–20 per item), HS codes, consignee SIREN-EORI, purpose "free trade sample", request clearance under Art. 86/C30; (4) send to a business address, notify the prospect of tracking; (5) avoid shipping liquid essential oils by Speed Post/express without DG check — [BK – verify] many essential oils are flammable liquids (UN 1169/1197) requiring dangerous-goods handling or limited-quantity packaging; cold-pressed fixed oils (castor) are normally non-DG; (6) split many SKUs into one consignment ≤ €150 and keep total weight far below 10 kg so 2019/1793 does not bite. (Synthesis + BK.)
- [BK – verify] If relief is refused, French import VAT on foodstuffs (spices) is generally 5.5%, on cosmetics/essential oils 20%, plus courier disbursement fees; for EU MFN duty most raw spices are 0%.
- Indian spice samples are not systematically "stopped" — the common failure modes are missing EORI/consignee data, zero-value invoices, undeclared food, or DG liquids (inference from DHL/UK guidance).

## 7. Realistic timelines: first contact → trial order → first container

### Takeaway
No CBI or industry source gives a hard number. Sourced evidence describes a multi-stage qualification (documents → samples with CoA → buyer lab tests → possible audit → trial/LCL → FCL), item-by-item supplier approval, quality-manager veto power, and 20–35 days sea transit. A reasonable planning assumption (inference) is **1–3 months to a first trial/LCL order with a responsive buyer and an already-registered exporter, and 6–12+ months to a first full container**, longer if registrations (FSSAI 2–4 months), organic certification or a buyer audit are needed.

### Cited Findings
- CBI: biggest mistake is overpromising; honour specs, delivery times, packaging; be on time; quality managers strongly influence deals; in larger companies purchasing managers consult superiors; French/Dutch meetings structured — [CBI 7 tips doing business (updated 15 Oct 2024)](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/tips-do-business).
- CBI recommends representative samples (section "Provide representative product samples") and early notice if a shipment may deviate from the approved sample — [CBI doing business](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/doing-business/).
- Supplier qualification: sample + CoA + spec; quantitative testing; from questionnaire to full on-site audit; qualification item-by-item (each ingredient separately); ongoing requalification — [Natural Products Insider](https://www.naturalproductsinsider.com/manufacturing/best-practices-ingredient-and-supplier-qualification); [SupplySide SJ](https://www.supplysidesj.com/manufacturing/key-hallmarks-of-a-good-supplier-qualification-program).
- Trial-order practices: some suppliers accept 50 kg or even 1 kg orders for formulation testing; LCL suitable for trial/smaller orders; first-deal deposits 30–50%; sea freight 20–35 days — [Alibaba cumin guide](https://www.alibaba.com/price-comparison/cumin-is-what); [Giriraj Psyllium guide](https://girirajpsyllium.in/buy-whole-spices-bulk-india-procurement-guide/) (marketplace/supplier sources, low authority).
- Buyers increasingly test for fraud/adulteration (e.g., cardamom origin), EtO, pesticides — [CBI requirements](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/buyer-requirements); [CBI fraud news](https://www.cbi.eu/news/fraud-and-adulteration-european-spice-and-herb-sector).

### Inferences
- Indicative sequence (inference): Week 0–4 intro + spec/price + document pack (IEC, CRES, FSSAI, CoA, SDS/TDS, organic cert); Week 2–8 samples by courier + buyer's lab test (1–3 weeks) + counter-samples; Month 2–4 supplier approval (questionnaire; audit for larger buyers) and trial order (LCL or air, 50 kg–1 t) with advance payment; Month 3–6 trial shipment arrives (sea 20–35 days + BCP checks) and is used in production; Month 6–12+ first FCL / annual contract (often tied to crop season and buyer's tender calendar). Spice-mix makers and grinders buy FCLs; small French clients may never buy FCLs and are better served via an EU importer (Model 3).
- Attending SIAL Paris (17–21 Oct 2026) or Fi Europe (17–19 Nov 2026) can compress the first-contact stage (workflow snippet dates).

## 8. Verify or refute: "France banned food supplements containing ashwagandha in 2025/2026"

### Takeaway
**Not supported / likely false as of October 2026.** No French arrêté, décret or DGAL measure banning ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) in food supplements was found. Ashwagandha remains legally usable in French food supplements (via EU mutual recognition / art. 16 décret 2006-352), subject to the **ANSES 2024 opinion (saisine 2021-SA-0077)** recommending that pregnant/breastfeeding women, under-18s, people with thyroid/endocrine, liver or heart conditions, or on CNS depressants avoid it — with label warnings expected/reportedly required via DGAL. The "ban" claim appears in one trade article without a legal reference. Real restrictions exist elsewhere: **Denmark ban (2023)**, **Poland dose cap**, **Netherlands draft national ban under consultation until 7 Dec 2026**; EU Article 8 procedure recommended (June 2024) but **not formally initiated** as of mid-2026.

### Cited Findings
- ANSES opinion on Withania somnifera supplements (saisine 2021-SA-0077), published April/June 2024, after DGCCRF referral in April 2021 following 8 adverse-effect reports; advises against use by pregnant/breastfeeding women, under-18s, people with endocrine disorders (dysthyroidism, hyperandrogenism), liver or heart problems, or on CNS depressants; does not withdraw authorisation — [NutraIngredients (17 Jun 2024)](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/17/French-agency-warns-ashwagandha-unsafe-in-specific-populations); [Compagnie des Sens](https://www.compagnie-des-sens.fr/ashwagandha-interdit-france/).
- Ashwagandha was suspended in France 2014–2015, re-authorised since 2015 via EU mutual recognition (art. 16 décret 2006-352), not via the 2014 arrêté plantes positive list; included without restriction in DGCCRF consolidated plant list Jan 2019; root on French Pharmacopoeia List B (relevant only to pharmacy preparations) — [Compagnie des Sens](https://www.compagnie-des-sens.fr/ashwagandha-interdit-france/) (secondary, retailer source).
- PHINN (June 2024): highly likely DGAL will ask operators to add warnings; since competence moved from DGCCRF to DGAL, ANSES opinions are effectively the basis for regulatory action — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/17/French-agency-warns-ashwagandha-unsafe-in-specific-populations).
- A 2026 article says France, Sweden and Poland require warning statements (legal text not located) — [Nutrition Insight](https://nutritioninsight.com/news/regulatory-update-spotlight-on-international-authorities-response-to-ashwagandha-safety-concerns.html).
- **Contradicting claim:** "More recently, food supplements containing ashwagandha have also been banned in France" (no date/instrument) — [Nutraceuticals World (May 2026)](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/ashwagandha-safety-questioned-in-europe-and-india/). The July 2026 NutraIngredients timeline mentions France only as having "initiated investigations" after the 2020 Danish report — [NutraIngredients timeline (6 Jul 2026)](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/06/timeline-will-authorities-de-throne-the-king-of-herbs/).
- EU: Heads of Food Safety Agencies WG (June 2024) recommended Article 8 (Reg. 1925/2006) prioritisation, root proposed for Annex III Part C — [BVL HoA report](https://www.bvl.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/01_Lebensmittel/Internationales/report_HoA_WG_FS-en.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6); Article 8 "not yet initiated" (May 2026) and stakeholders met in Brussels 14 Jul 2026 as regulators consider review — [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/ashwagandha-safety-questioned-in-europe-and-india/); [NutraIngredients 16 Jul 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/16/ashwagandha-armed-with-critical-safety-data-as-industry-prepares-for-europe-wide-assessment/); Food Supplements Europe (Mar 2025) asked for no national bans before EU assessment — [Nutrition Insight](https://nutritioninsight.com/news/regulatory-update-spotlight-on-international-authorities-response-to-ashwagandha-safety-concerns.html).
