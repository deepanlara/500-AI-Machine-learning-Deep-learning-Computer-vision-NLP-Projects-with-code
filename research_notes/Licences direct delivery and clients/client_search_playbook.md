# Client-search playbook: finding French and EU B2B buyers for Indian spices, botanical extracts, oils, henna and multani mitti (Paris, October 2026)

Research date: 8 October 2026. Method: web-search result snippets only, because page fetching was unavailable. Every row and claim below has a source link. Verification levels in the lead table:
- **V**: the company exists and its activity matches the source.
- **P**: partly verified. It is a directory, aggregator or retailer listing, or the source is old or only indirectly about the activity.
- **U**: the name surfaced but its fit or activity is unconfirmed.

Names the brief said were already known were excluded: Arcadie/Cook, Terre Exotique, Thiercelin, Solina, Nactis, Nexira, Aroma-Zone, VS.CO Cash & Carry, Verstegen, Catz International, Daarnhouwer, Fuchs/Ostmann/Ubena. **Note:** "L'Herbier de France", which the brief suggested as a candidate, is an Arcadie brand, so it is a duplicate ([Tourisme Gard](https://www.tourismegard.com/fr/fiche/commerce-et-service/arcadie-mejannes-les-ales_TFO6551233/), [Greenweez](https://www.greenweez.com/marque/lherbier-de-france)). "Epices Fuchs SARL" (Monteux, NAF 46.37Z) is the excluded Fuchs group, so it was left out.

---

## Q1. Which additional French buyers exist in each segment, and are they verified?

### Takeaway
I identified about 115 additional named prospects across 9 segments. Most are in France, plus EU alternatives. Roughly 70 are verified (V) and the rest are partly verified (P) or unverified (U). The strongest fits for an Indian-origin portfolio are:
- Spice importer-packers: Comptoir Colonial, Cepasco, Albert Ménès, Épices Roellinger, Integral Foods.
- Organic wholesalers: Relais Vert group, Biocoop central purchasing, Markal, Ekibio.
- Herbal wholesale: Cailleau Herboristerie.
- Ayurvedic and botanical supplement brands: Ayur-Vana, Dynveo, Nutrimea, Natura Force, Nutri Expert, Vit'All+, Amoseeds.
- Botanical ingredient distributors and contract manufacturers: Unipex/Barentz, Plantex, Activ'Inside, Floralpina, Netlab Pharma.
- Oil and cosmetic-ingredient players: Olvea, Emile Noël, Comptoir des Huiles, MyCosmetik, Propos'Nature, Compagnie des Sens.
- Henna and colour brands: Les Couleurs de Jeanne (Comptoir des Lys), Henné Color (NJD Cosmetics), Cattier for clays.

#### B. Segment facts that matter for targeting
- **Supplement manufacturing is mostly outsourced.** One industry blog estimates about 350 French supplement firms, 95% of them SMEs ([Dubocalalassiette](https://dubocalalassiette.fr/blog/fabricant-complement-alimentaire-france/)). Capsules are usually made by façonniers, and few brands run their own lines ([Nutri&Co](https://nutriandco.com/fr/pages/laboratoire-complement-alimentaire)). Pitch both the brand, which specifies the raw material, and the façonnier, which buys it.
- **Biocoop decides listings centrally.** A listing commission and a supplier file exist (2014 study), and the specification has been tightened year after year. Biocoop said it would avoid large industrial groups but would work with suppliers that also sell to mass retail if they meet its quality bar ([Erudit study](https://www.erudit.org/en/revue/lsp/2014/v/n72/1027213ar.pdf); [LSA interview](https://www.lsa-conso.fr/biocoop-le-militantisme-reste-au-c-ur-du-modele-assument-les-patrons-de-l-enseigne-bio-interview,465313); [LSA Orion Porta](https://www.lsa-conso.fr/orion-porta-biocoop-ouvrir-davantage-biocoop-sur-le-monde-qui-nous-entoure,270007)).
- **Relais Vert prefers local producers** (Carpentras, Gard, Bouches-du-Rhône) and four French supply chains. Imported spices fit as a complementary range, not a priority ([Best Food Importers](https://bestfoodimporters.com/company/relais-vert/); [Best of Bio PDF](https://www.best-of-bio.fr/upload/media/post_pdf/0001/47/0f4c67ae4b3170dc266242800ff4cd0ca35a3df3.pdf)).
- **France as a spice market.** Germany leads European spice imports (about €592M in 2021), and France, the Netherlands and the UK each import over €300M ([Statista](https://WWW.STATISTA.COM/statistics/1289767/leading-spice-and-herb-importers-europe/)). CBI flags France as attractive for **ground spices and spice mixtures** ([CBI finding buyers](https://cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/finding-buyers)). India–France spice trade was 4,314 t worth US$43.48M in FY2020-21. Turmeric, chillies, ginger, small cardamom, curry powder, oleoresins and mint oils were the main lines, shipped by 60+ Indian exporters ([Spices Board France BSM brochure](https://indianspices.com/sites/default/files/BSM%20FRANCE-BROCHURE-SMALL.pdf)).
- **Regulatory red flags change which buyers are realistic.**
  - *Ashwagandha:* In 2024 ANSES advised against it for pregnant or breastfeeding women, minors and people with thyroid, liver or heart conditions, and found no safe level could be set. Denmark banned it in 2023, Belgium treats it as medicinal, Poland caps the daily dose, and the Netherlands announced a ban in early 2026 (status unclear). An EU "Article 8" review was still being weighed in July 2026 ([NutraIngredients 2024](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/17/French-agency-warns-ashwagandha-unsafe-in-specific-populations); [Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/regulation/eu-regulatory-update-ashwagandha); [NutraIngredients July 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/16/ashwagandha-armed-with-critical-safety-data-as-industry-prepares-for-europe-wide-assessment/); [Regask](https://regask.com/herbal-supplement-bans-europe-ashwagandha-kratom-regulations-2025)).
  - *Shilajit:* Reportedly listed in the EU Novel Food Catalogue as "Mumijo", novel food but "not novel only in food supplements". That means supplements only, with no food or drink use ([PTA heute](https://www.ptaheute.de/serien/superfoods/shilajit-ein-superfood-aus-dem-himalaya); [Ruokavirasto on the catalogue](https://ruokavirasto.fi/en/foodstuffs/food-sector/food-ingredients-and-contents/novel-foods/establishing-novel-food-status-of-a-food/novel-food-catalogue-of-european-commission)).
  - *Curcumin:* Finished-product specs vary (95% curcuminoids vs 95% curcumin). Buyers will ask for the exact assay and method ([search synthesis of retail listings, e.g. Atida](https://www.atida.fr/vit-all-curcuma-extraits-standardises-avec-poivre-noir-120-gelules-vegetales.html)).

### Inferences
- **Priority order for a beginner in Paris**, from fastest to first order:
  1. Small blenders and fine-food packers (Comptoir Colonial, Integral Foods, Roellinger, Albert Ménès, the small 10.84Z firms).
  2. Ayurvedic and botanical supplement brands, which buy small lots of powders (Ayur-Vana, Amoseeds, Natura Force, Nutri Expert, Vit'All+).
  3. DIY and indie cosmetics brands for castor, black cumin and moringa oils, henna, amla and multani mitti (MyCosmetik, Les Couleurs de Jeanne, Cattier, Lamazuna, Propos'Nature).
  4. Paris South Asian cash-and-carries for retail packs (VT Cash & Carry, plus walking the Faubourg Saint-Denis and La Chapelle streets).
  5. Organic wholesalers (Relais Vert, Biocoop, Markal, Ekibio), which have long listing cycles and need organic certification.
  6. Industrial buyers (McCormick, Arkopharma, PiLeJe, Nutergia, Havea, Naturactive), which need audited supply, full dossiers and often existing approved suppliers. Treat these as 12 to 24 month targets.
- **Ingredient distributors are also partners.** Unipex/Barentz, Plantex/Nactarome, Activ'Inside and NEVER DY can be customers for bulk or channel partners. Vidya Europe is an Indian-owned competitor; avoid pitching it as a customer.
- **Ashwagandha and shilajit:** Expect hesitation from large, pharmacy-channel brands. Smaller online and Ayurvedic brands remain the realistic buyers. Lead with turmeric, curcumin, moringa, amla and black cumin for supplements.
- **Henna and multani mitti:** The French "coloration végétale" niche (Les Couleurs de Jeanne, Cattier colour kits, Henné Color) and clay-based brands are the natural buyers. Buyers will want COSMOS/Cosmébio-compatible raw materials and proof of no PPD or metallic salts.

### Gaps
- These names were **not verified** in this research: "Plantes & Industrie", "Khadi France", "Les Huilettes", "Biocyte", "Nutrinov", "Unither" (a pharma CDMO, probably irrelevant), "Gemini/Ganesha" cash-and-carries, Transgourmet/Promocash and Sysco France. ## Q2. Which free or cheap prospecting tools work, and how should each be used?

### Takeaway
The cheapest reliable lead engine is:
1. Pull free company lists from the French registry by NAF code and département, using annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr or its free API, plus Pappers' first 10 free export rows.
2. Enrich the list with association directories (Cosmébio, Cosmetic Valley, Synadiet via Natexpo/Vitafoods), trade-fair exhibitor lists and retailer brand pages (Greenweez, Atida, Redcare list brand names by ingredient).
3. Find the decision-maker on LinkedIn (free search, or a 30-day Sales Navigator trial) and the email with Hunter's free tier.

Indian institutions (the Spices Board, the Embassy's economic wing, IFCCI France at CCI Paris) are mostly useful for supply and credibility, not French buyer lists. Business France/Team France Export events target French exporters.

### Cited Findings

**NAF/APE codes to filter on (NAF rév. 2, valid until 31 December 2026)**
- **46.37Z**: wholesale (B2B) of coffee, tea, cocoa and **spices**. About 455 companies ([Legalnest](https://www.legalnest.fr/code-ape-naf/4637z)).
- **10.84Z**: manufacture of condiments and seasonings, including spice processing. Spice *cultivation* is excluded. About 240 companies (unofficial count). McCormick, Beaun'Épices and Epice Passion sit here ([Legalnest](https://www.legalnest.fr/code-ape-naf/1084z); [Xerfi McCormick](https://www.xerfi.com/etudes-par-entreprise/mccormick-france_622980027)).
- **10.89Z**: "other food products n.c.a.". INSEE's NAF rév. 2 **explicitly includes food-supplement manufacture** ([INSEE 10.89Z](https://www.insee.fr/fr/metadonnees/nafr2/sousClasse/10.89Z)).
- **10.86Z**: homogenised and dietetic foods. **Some supplement companies register here**, so search both 10.89Z and 10.86Z ([Legalnest 1086Z](https://www.legalnest.fr/code-ape-naf/1086z)).
- **20.42Z**: manufacture of perfumes and toiletries, covering cosmetics, hair care and soap. Natural essential-oil extraction is **20.53Z** and pharma preparations are **21.20Z** ([Legalnest 2042Z](https://www.legalnest.fr/code-ape-naf/2042z)).
- **46.45Z**: wholesale of perfumery and beauty products ([Xerfi Star Parfums Cosmétiques](https://www.xerfi.com/etudes-par-entreprise/star-parfums-cosmetiques-spc_384027678)).
- **46.38B**: other specialised food wholesale, used by e.g. Sud Épices. **46.39B**: non-specialised food wholesale, used by e.g. Le Jardin des Épices ([Doctrine](https://www.doctrine.fr/e/ENT130BD6A5E3390D85BE7F)).
- **Cautions on NAF codes:**
  - A NAF code reflects only the main activity, so secondary activities are invisible ([Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/fr/blog/ou-trouver-le-code-naf)). Some firms are misclassified, e.g. Cailleau sits under a cultivation code ([SIRENE](https://api-avis-situation-sirene.insee.fr/identification/pdf/31389721700029)).
  - **NAF 2025 takes effect on 1 January 2027.** Every unit's APE code will change. During 2026, sirene.gouv.fr shows each firm its future code ([INSEE](https://www.insee.fr/fr/information/8181066); [Infogreffe](https://www.infogreffe.fr/NAF-2025-codes-APE-nouvelle-grille-lecture-entreprises-a-compter-de-2027); [Le Monde du Droit](https://www.lemondedudroit.fr/public/282-droit-public-general/100286-la-naf-2025-entre-en-vigueur-en-2027.html)). Lists built in 2026 should save both codes.

**Registry tools**
- **annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr**: free official directory. It runs on the Etalab API recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr, which combines INSEE SIRENE, RNE and INPI data and needs no API key ([Apify description](https://apify.com/seemuapps/pappers-sirene-scraper)). Its "administrative" search mode accepts a NAF code plus a département or postcode; this is a third-party description ([PolicyLayer](https://policylayer.com/tools/io-github-cturkieh-france-data-mcp/entreprises-in-radius)).
- **Pappers.fr**: advanced search exports to Excel/CSV in one click. **The first 10 rows are free**, the rest is paid. 68+ fields per row, including SIREN, address, establishments, turnover, NAF and headcount ([Pappers exports](https://www.pappers.fr/exports)). Freemium, best for manual searches and moderate batches ([Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/fr/blog/ou-trouver-le-code-naf)).
- **Sirene API**: free, for loading NAF-filtered lists into a sheet or CRM ([Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/fr/blog/ou-trouver-le-code-naf)).

## Q3. What outreach process should be used: sequence, samples, catalogue, conversion and pricing?

### Takeaway
B2B cold email to professionals is legal in France on an **opt-out** basis, if the message relates to the recipient's job and every email offers a free, easy unsubscribe. Average B2B cold-email reply rates are about **3–5%**, and only about 1 in 7 replies is positive. Plan on contacting roughly **100–150 targeted buyers to get 5–10 conversations and 1–3 sample trials** (an inference). Win with a short French sequence (LinkedIn, email, phone over 3–4 weeks) and a complete technical dossier: spec/TDS, CoA per lot, SDS for cosmetic ingredients, organic certificate and traceability. Send consistent, lot-matched samples. Quote in EUR on clear Incoterms 2020 terms: delivered (DAP/"franco") for French buyers, FCA for bulk import deals.

### Cited Findings
**Legal frame for French B2B prospecting**
- Commercial email to a professional is under **opt-out**; email to a consumer needs **opt-in** ([CNIL/BPI guide](https://WWW.CNIL.FR/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/bpi-cnil-rgpd_fiche_2_ameliorez-maitrisez-votre-relation-client_0.pdf)).
- Bercy accepts two cases: a generic address (contact@, info@), or a message whose subject relates to the recipient's profession ([Bercy Infos Entreprises](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/entreprises/obligations-entreprises-envoi-courriel-newsletters)).
- The person must be told at collection and must be able to object simply and free of charge, e.g. via an unsubscribe link. The lawful basis is legitimate interest. The CNIL updated its prospecting guidance (2026 article) ([Leto](https://www.leto.legal/news/cnil-communications-electroniques-prospection-regles-2026); [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/prospection-commerciale-rgpd)).

**Conversion benchmarks**
- Average B2B cold-email reply rates:
  - About 5% in 2025, down from 8.5% in 2019 ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)).
  - 5.8% in 2024 per Belkins (16.5M emails), as cited in compilations ([Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/cold-email-statistics)).
  - 3.43% platform-wide in Instantly's 2026 benchmark ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)).
  - 2.09% in a February 2026 sample of 2M emails, where about 14.1% of replies were positive, giving roughly a **0.64% interested-reply rate** ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/); [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)).
- Campaigns under 50 recipients average **5.8%** replies vs **2.1%** for large sends. Top-quartile senders reach 15–25% through tight targeting and sequencing ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)).
- Apollo calls a reply rate above 6% strong and above 8% exceptional. It separates total replies from positive replies ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach)).

**What buyers expect (CBI guides)**
- Buyers expect you to meet the legal minimum and go beyond it. They weigh quality, reliability, price, added value over their current supplier, and risk ([CBI cosmetic ingredients, 9 tips, updated May 2024](https://cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/doing-business)).
- Build a dossier: a **technical data sheet/specification, safety data sheet, certificates of analysis, and a product or sell sheet**. Keep it updated and adjustable. Do **not** copy SDS, TDS or CoA templates from the internet; have them prepared by a lab or chemist. Claims must be evidenced under Regulation (EU) 655/2013 ([CBI](https://cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/doing-business)).
- "Always send consistent quality samples to interested buyers." Buyers also expect traceability to the source and reliable quantities ([CBI](https://cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/doing-business)). The CBI spice guide covers similar points for spice buyers ([CBI spices doing business](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/doing-business/)).
- Curcumin specs differ (95% curcuminoids with ≥70% curcumin vs "95% curcumin"), so state the assay basis and method ([Atida example](https://www.atida.fr/vit-all-curcuma-extraits-standardises-avec-poivre-noir-120-gelules-vegetales.html)).
- For henna, retailers warn against PPD "black henna" and metallic-salt hennas. Les Couleurs de Jeanne claims no PPD, picramic acid or metallic salts ([Soin et Nature](https://www.soin-et-nature.com/fr/henne-128004); [Easypara LCDJ](https://www.easypara.fr/coloration-100-vegetale-2x50-g-blond-clair-les-couleurs-de-jeanne.html)).

**Pricing and Incoterms 2020**
- FOB, CFR and CIF are for traditional sea transport. For containerised goods FCA is usually more appropriate, and it works for all modes (LCL, FCL, air). Always write "Incoterms 2020" after the term and name the exact place ([BBVA](https://www.bbva.es/en/empresas/productos/negocio-internacional/noticias-comex/incoterms-2020-may26.html); [Banc Sabadell](https://www.bancsabadell.com/cs/Satellite/SabAtl/Incoterms/1191332202955/en); [iContainers FCA](https://www.icontainers.com/help/incoterms/fca/)).
- CBI notes that FOB (coastal) and FCA (landlocked) limit a beginner exporter's responsibilities ([CBI PDF](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1244.pdf)).
- **CIF** needs only minimum Institute Cargo Clause (C) insurance cover. **CIP** needs Clause (A), which is all-risk ([doesspice.com](https://doesspice.com/en/news/incoterms-2020?rCH=2)).
- **DAP**: the seller bears all costs and risks to the named place, excluding import duties and taxes, and usually prices higher ([Ship4wd](https://ship4wd.com/incoterms/dap-vs-fob)).
- Spices fall under HS chapter 09; check duty and VAT in TARIC ([search synthesis citing BBVA/Sabadell](https://www.bbva.es/en/empresas/productos/negocio-internacional/noticias-comex/incoterms-2020-may26.html)).

### Inferences
These are practical recommendations synthesised from the sources; they are not stated by any one source.
- **Sequence (French, 4 weeks, 6 touches):**
  - **Day 0:** LinkedIn connection with a short note: "Fournisseur basé à Paris d'épices et d'actifs végétaux d'origine Inde (curcuma, gingembre, cardamome, moringa, amla...), lots tracés, CoA par lot. Je serais ravi d'échanger."
  - **Day 1:** Email 1 to the named buyer or achats@. Subject naming their product, e.g. "Curcuma bio 5% curcumine pour vos mélanges ?". Three lines, 2–3 SKUs matching their range, an offer of free samples with CoA, and the unsubscribe line.
  - **Day 4:** LinkedIn message or comment.
  - **Day 7:** Email 2 with the spec sheet and a price indication, e.g. "à partir de X €/kg DAP, MOQ 25 kg".
  - **Day 10–12:** Phone call (French, short).
  - **Day 18:** Email 3 with a case or proof point (lab results, organic certificate).
  - **Day 28:** Break-up email.
  - Keep batches under 50 per product and segment, since small sends have reply rates about 2.8 times higher.
- **Volume math:** at a 3–6% reply rate and 15–30% of replies positive, 100 well-targeted contacts give about 3–6 replies and 1–2 sample requests. 300–500 contacts give 3–10 sample trials and 1–3 first orders over 3–6 months. This is an inference from the generic benchmarks; no spice- or ingredient-specific conversion data was found.
- **Sample kit:** 50–250 g (spices or powders) or 50–100 ml (oils) per SKU in neutral, labelled pouches showing product, botanical name, INCI where cosmetic, lot number, origin, packing date and organic code. Include the matching lot CoA (pesticides, aflatoxins and ochratoxin where relevant, heavy metals, microbiology, plus ethylene-oxide and Sudan-dye tests for spices), the spec/TDS, the SDS (oils and cosmetic powders), the organic certificate and allergen/GMO statements. Charge for samples only for large panels; for small brands, free samples are a normal cost of sale.
- **Catalogue and spec-sheet contents:** botanical name and part, INCI name (cosmetics), origin state/region, form and mesh, assay (curcumin %, withanolides %, piperine absent or present), moisture, ash, volatile oil, colour (ASTA for chilli), micro limits, pesticide compliance with EU MRLs, packaging, shelf life, MOQ, lead time, Incoterm, price band and certifications.
- **Price quotes for French customers:** quote in **EUR per kg, DAP buyer's site ("franco de port")** above a threshold, or EXW/FCA at your Île-de-France warehouse for collectors. For pallet or container deals direct from India, offer **FCA Indian port/ICD** plus **CIF/CIP Le Havre or Marseille-Fos** as options, and state the insurance cover. Give a price validity of 15–30 days, because spice prices are volatile.

### Gaps

- CBI's detailed sample guidance text was not retrievable.
- I found no source on typical French buyer payment terms for imported ingredients, or on French-specific sample customs.
- The CNIL's updated 2026 prospecting sheet could not be read directly; check cnil.fr.

### Inferences
- **Fastest action:** register now for Cosmetic 360 (6 days away) and SIAL Paris (9 days away). At SIAL, walk the spice, ethnic and organic halls and the India and Sri Lanka pavilions, and photograph stand lists. **Buy the Fi Europe pass before 19 October** to keep the €173 rate. Fi Europe is the key EU botanical-extract show for supplement buyers, but it is in Frankfurt.
- **Cheapest high-value 2027 options in Paris:** PharmagoraPlus (March, pharmacy-channel supplement brands) and MakeUp in Paris (June, probably free, beauty brands).
- **Worth a trip:** BIOFACH (February) for organic importers across the EU, and in-cosmetics and Vitafoods in Barcelona (April and May) for cosmetic and supplement ingredient buyers.
- **Visitor eligibility without a company.** No source says a SIRET is mandatory, but all of these shows are trade-only and SIAL reserves the right to ask for proof. In practice, register a micro-entreprise or SAS first so you have a SIRET, business cards, a one-page website and a LinkedIn company page. That is the safest professional proof and also makes you credible to buyers. This is an inference; registration cost and time were not researched here.
- **Working a show as a visitor:** pre-book 10–15 meetings by emailing exhibitors who buy, such as packers, brands and distributors, 1–2 weeks ahead. Carry 20–30 mini sample sachets and a one-page spec digest. Log every badge or business card into the tracker the same day. Exhibitor lists double as lead lists after the show.
