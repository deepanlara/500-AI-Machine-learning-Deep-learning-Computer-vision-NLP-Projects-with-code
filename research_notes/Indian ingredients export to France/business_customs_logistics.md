# Setting up in France to import Indian ingredients or act as sales agent: structure, tax, customs, logistics, pricing, payment risk (as of October 2026)

Scope: a person living near Paris working with their brother's Indian exporter "La Dolce India" (spices, botanical extracts, oils, henna/clay). Model A means importing in their own name and reselling. Model B means acting as a commercial agent or business introducer on commission, with India invoicing the EU buyer directly.

Method note: the research ran about 17 search and fetch calls. trade.ec.europa.eu (Access2Markets) and indiaseatradenews.com could not be reached from the research environment, so several tariff lines come from TARIC mirror sites, not the official database. Items marked "background knowledge, unverified" come from general knowledge and were not confirmed by a source in this session. The report writer should treat them as such.

## 1. Business structures in France (micro-entreprise, EI, EURL, SASU, agent commercial, apporteur d'affaires)

### Takeaway
For 2026-2028 the micro-entreprise limits are €203,100 turnover for selling goods and €83,600 for services. Social charges are 12.3% of turnover for goods sales and 21.2% for commercial services (BIC). The VAT franchise reform was dropped at end-2025, so the thresholds stay at €85,000 (goods) and €37,500 (services). A micro-entreprise suits Model B, commission agency, well. It suits Model A, importing, badly. Social charges are levied on gross turnover with no deduction of the cost of goods, and an importer needs a VAT number anyway to use import VAT reverse charge. A SASU or EURL (corporate tax 15% up to €42,500 of profit, then 25%) is the natural vehicle once real import volumes start. Creation costs are about €200-250 in mandatory fees.

### Cited Findings
**Micro-entreprise thresholds and rates**
- Micro-regime turnover ceilings for 2026-2028: €203,100 excl. tax for sale of goods and accommodation, €83,600 for services (BIC/BNC). Exceeding the ceiling in one year keeps the regime for the next year. After two consecutive years over the ceiling, the regime ends on 1 January of the following year. — [CCI Paris IDF](https://www.entreprises.cci-paris-idf.fr/actualites/micro-entrepreneur-revalorisation-des-seuils-pour-2026-2028); [Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/encyclopedie/micro-entreprise-regime-auto-entrepreneur/lessentiel-ce-regime/seuils-du-regime-micro); [Legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/actualites-fiscales/4436-nouveaux-seuils-micro-entreprises-annee-2026.html)
- VAT franchise (franchise en base) thresholds are unchanged in 2026: goods €85,000 base and €93,500 upper threshold, services €37,500 base and €41,250 upper threshold. The government abandoned the reform at end-2025. That reform, from the 2025 budget, would have cut the threshold to a single €25,000, which caused an outcry. Above the upper threshold, VAT is due from the date it is crossed, with no tolerance. — [Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/actualites/nouveaux-plafonds-chiffre-affaires-2026-micro-entreprise); [CCI Lyon (PLF 2026 relaunch of reform debate)](https://www.lyon-metropole.cci.fr/actualite/micro-entreprises-le-projet-de-loi-de-finances-2026-relance-la-reforme-du-seuil-de); [Socic](https://www.socic.fr/ressources-comptabilite/articles/micro-entreprise-et-tva-en-2026-franchise-depassement-de-seuils-regime-reel-et-option-volontaire/)
- The micro ceiling and the VAT threshold are separate limits. A micro-entrepreneur can exceed the VAT threshold, stay in the micro regime and charge VAT. For mixed activity, total turnover is tested against €85,000 and the services share against €37,500. — [Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/actualites/nouveaux-plafonds-chiffre-affaires-2026-micro-entreprise)
- 2026 social contribution rates on turnover collected: sale of goods 12.3% (+0.1% training contribution = 12.4%), commercial or craft BIC services 21.2% (+0.1-0.3% training), CIPAV liberal professions 23.2%, other BNC 25.6% per economie.gouv.fr and Bpifrance. Propulse by CA gives 26.1% for BNC, so the BNC rate is uncertain. — [economie.gouv.fr](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales); [Bpifrance Création](https://bpifrance-creation.fr/boiteaoutils/travailleurs-independants-cotisations-a-verser-debut-dactivite-regime-normal); [Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/micro-entrepreneur/cotisations-micro-entrepreneur)
- Optional flat income-tax withholding (versement libératoire): 1% of turnover for goods, 1.7% for BIC services, 2.2% for BNC. ACRE gives a 50% reduction in contributions for the first 4 quarters. Contributions are calculated on turnover with no deduction of costs. — [economie.gouv.fr / aggregated in search](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/micro-entreprises-quel-est-le-montant-de-vos-cotisations-sociales); [Advizexperts](https://advizexperts.fr/baremes-seuils/cotisations-micro-entrepreneur/)

**Companies (EURL / SASU) and corporate tax**
- Corporate tax (IS) in 2026: normal rate 25%. The reduced 15% rate applies to the first €42,500 of profit and was not changed by the 2026 finance law. Conditions: turnover under €10m, at least 75% of capital held by individuals, capital fully paid up. Example: €60,000 profit gives €10,750 IS (€6,375 + €4,375). A parliamentary proposal to raise the €42,500 bracket to €100,000 is not law. — [Hayot Expertise](https://hayot-expertise.fr/blog/is-2026-taux-normal-taux-reduit-15-seuils); [Propulse by CA](https://propulsebyca.fr/impots-et-taxes/impot-societes/taux); [Fiscaloo](https://www.fiscaloo.fr/12911-taux-reduit-is-2023/)
- Registration goes through the INPI guichet unique (formalities portal), mandatory since 1 January 2023. Mandatory costs: RCS registration about €33.83-37.45 (sources differ), beneficial-owner declaration about €19-21, legal notice at a flat rate since 1 January 2026 (€124-142 HT in mainland France, sources differ). The total is about €200-226 for a SASU and about €177 HT for an EURL. Online platforms charge about €400-850 all-in. — [Swim Legal SASU](https://www.swim.legal/blog/cout-creation-sasu); [Swim Legal EURL](https://www.swim.legal/blog/cout-creation-eurl); [LegalPlace](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/cout-creation-sasu-inpi/); [Captain Contrat](https://www.captaincontrat.com/creer-son-entreprise/sasu/cout-sasu-creation-gestion)

**Agent commercial (Model B)**
- The statute is in Code de commerce articles L134-1 to L134-17, with no 2026 reform found. Registration on the registre spécial des agents commerciaux (RSAC) at the commercial court registry is mandatory under art. R134-6, costing about €25. Without it, proof of status gets harder and the contract may be requalified, although case law says status depends on the actual activity, not registration alone. — [LegalPlace RSAC](https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/inscription-registre-special-agents-commerciaux/); [Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/agent-commercial-auto-entrepreneur)
- End-of-contract indemnity: no statute fixes the amount. Courts customarily award 2 years of gross commissions, usually averaged over the last 3 years, and may reduce it for short relationships. A 2026 Aix-en-Provence appeal ruling confirmed 2 years. The agent must notify the claim by registered letter within 1 year of termination or lose it. Serious misconduct (faute grave, strictly construed) or resignation without legitimate cause removes the right. — [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/fra/publication/indemnite-de-rupture-du-contrat-d-agence-commerciale); [Cour d'appel fiche 14](https://www.cours-appel.justice.fr/sites/default/files/2021-03/FICHE%2014-%20cessation%20contrat%20agent%20commercial.pdf); [Doctrine CA Aix 7 May 2026](https://www.doctrine.fr/d/CA/Aix-en-Provence/2026/CAPD96F3C66FA6731FA72CB); [Lebouard Avocats](https://www.lebouard-avocats.fr/post/indemnite-rupture-agent-commercial-regles-exceptions)
- Statutory notice (L134-11): 1 month in year 1, 2 months in year 2, 3 months from year 3 onwards. — [search summary of L134-11 via Justifit](https://www.justifit.fr/b/guides/droit-contrats/contrat-agent-commercial/)
- An agent commercial can operate as a micro-entrepreneur. — [Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/agent-commercial-auto-entrepreneur)

### Inferences
- **Model A on micro-entreprise:** €100k of goods sales costs about €12,400 in social charges whatever the margin. A 20% gross-margin trade (€20k margin) would lose about 62% of that margin to URSSAF before tax. Imported stock, freight and duty are not deductible. The micro regime is therefore poorly suited to trading with thin margins. A SASU (president as assimilé salarié, can take dividends instead of salary) or an EURL at IS is better once gross margin justifies about €1.5-3k a year of accounting fees (accounting fee range is background knowledge, unverified).
- **Import VAT in the franchise regime:** an importer must have a French VAT number for mandatory import VAT reverse charge (see section 3). A micro-entrepreneur in VAT franchise cannot recover import VAT, so the VAT becomes a pure cost. Opting for VAT is possible (background knowledge, unverified).
- **Model B on micro-entreprise:** commission income counts as commercial services (BIC), so the €83,600 ceiling and the 21.2% rate apply. Commissions invoiced to the Indian principal are B2B services to a non-EU business. Under the general B2B place-of-supply rule the service falls outside the scope of French VAT, so VAT franchise status matters less (background knowledge, unverified; confirm with an accountant).
- **Apporteur d'affaires (business introducer):** a looser alternative with no statute, no RSAC registration and no statutory end-of-contract indemnity. It suits occasional introductions rather than ongoing negotiation on the principal's behalf (background knowledge, unverified; no 2026 source fetched).
- **Family member as agent:** the 2-year indemnity is mandatory public order in favour of the agent. That works to the family's advantage if the brother's firm ever ends the relationship, but it also means the agency contract must be written carefully: exclusivity, territory, commission base, del credere if any, governing law. Tax authorities on both sides may scrutinise commission rates between related parties for transfer pricing. In India, commissions paid abroad by a related exporter may face scrutiny (background knowledge, unverified). Keeping commissions within the market range of 3-10% and documenting them is prudent.

### Gaps
- Typical commission rates for food-ingredient agents (the brief says 3-10%) could not be confirmed with a citable source in this session.
- No 2026 source fetched on apporteur d'affaires rules or URSSAF treatment.
- SASU/EURL social charge rates (about 75-82% of net salary for an assimilé salarié president, about 40-45% TNS for a gérant) are background knowledge, unverified.
- Kbis/RCS fees vary slightly between sources (€33.83 vs €37.45). Check the INPI tariff.

## 2. Customs formalities: EORI, customs representative, clearance costs

### Takeaway
An EORI number is free. It is obtained online via douane.gouv.fr (SOPRANO-Accès simplifié / EORI) and is active within about 24 hours. The customs service warns about scam sites that charge for it. Most small importers clear goods through a registered customs representative (RDE, often the freight forwarder). No reliable 2026 tariff for clearance fees was found.

### Cited Findings
- EORI is free and issued only via SOPRANO EORI on douane.gouv.fr, and the number is active 24 h after issue. Since April 2023 any operator can have an EORI based on SIREN rather than SIRET. Customs says the timetable for moving fully to EORI-SIREN will be announced during 2026. DSV states a 31/12/2025 end date for SIRET-based EORI, which conflicts with customs, and customs prevails. — [douane.gouv.fr EORI](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/demarche/enregistrer-votre-entreprise-aupres-de-la-douane-numero-eori); [DSV](https://www.dsv.com/fr-fr/a-propos-de-dsv/actualites/news-france/eori-siren-2696770)
- Customs warning (April 2025): fraudulent sponsored sites charge for EORI applications. — [douane.gouv.fr alert](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/actualites/arnaques-la-demande-de-numero-eori)
- Private individuals not carrying out customs-related activity do not need an EORI. — [douane.gouv.fr](https://www.douane.gouv.fr/demarche/enregistrer-votre-entreprise-aupres-de-la-douane-numero-eori)
- A common error is declaring with the carrier's EORI rather than the importer's own. That breaks the import VAT reverse charge (autoliquidation), which requires the importer's French VAT number and EORI on the declaration. — [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/autoliquidation-tva-importation-france/)

### Inferences
- **Model A:** the French entity is the importer of record. It needs an EORI, a French VAT number, a customs representation mandate given to the RDE/forwarder, and a duty deferment arrangement or payment of duty via the forwarder.
- **Model B:** the EU buyer is importer of record. The agent needs no EORI, which is a major simplification.
- Indicative clearance fees of about €60-150 per import declaration, plus disbursement fees of 2-5% on duties advanced by the broker, are background knowledge, unverified.

### Gaps
- No citable 2026 clearance fee schedule (Le Havre/Fos) found.
- No source fetched on additional food controls: BCP/SIVEP checks, official controls on certain Indian spices under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1793 (chilli/curry/spice mixes have been subject to increased checks for aflatoxins, pesticides and ethylene oxide). This is background knowledge, unverified, and important for the compliance researcher.

## 3. Import duties: TARIC rates, GSP for India, REX, India-EU FTA

### Takeaway
Most of the product range enters the EU at 0% MFN duty: whole turmeric, ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, plant extracts under 1302 and vegetable products under 1404 (the last three from background knowledge). The main exceptions are castor oil (1515 30 90: 5.1% MFN, 1.6% GSP) and some ground "other spices" (0910 99: up to 12.5%). For 2026-2028 the EU suspended GSP for India only in industrial sections (S-5, S-6a, S-7a/b, S-11a, S-13, S-14, S-15a/b). Agricultural lines, including Section III fats and oils, keep GSP, so castor and other oils should still get the GSP rate with a valid REX statement on origin. The EU-India FTA concluded on 26-27 January 2026 but is not yet in force, with entry into force unlikely before early 2027.

### Cited Findings
**Duty rates**
- Ginger 0910 11 00: 0%. Turmeric 0910 30 00: 0%. Curry 0910 91: 0%. "Other spices, crushed or ground" 0910 99/0910 91 90: 12.5%. Saffron crushed or ground: 8.5%. — [thetradehub.eu origin 0910](https://www.thetradehub.eu/en/customs/origin/0910); [zolltarifnummern 2026](https://www.zolltarifnummern.de/2026/09109999)
- Cardamom (0908): duty "from 0%". Pepper and Capsicum (0904): "0% to 4%", with no subheading detail found. — [celna24 0908](https://www.celna24.pl/customs-tariff/0908); [celna24 0904](https://celna24.pl/customs-tariff/0904)
- Castor oil 1515 30 90 (not for aminoundecanoic acid): MFN 5.1%, GSP 1.6%. — [hsciq EU 1515309000](https://hsciq.com/en/HSEU/Code/1515309000)

**GSP for India 2026-2028**
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1909 (September 2025) suspends GSP preferences for India from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2028 for sections S-5 mineral products, S-6a chemicals, S-7a plastics, S-7b rubber, S-11a textiles, S-13 stone/ceramics/glass, S-14 pearls/precious metals, S-15a iron and steel, and S-15b base metals. The retrieved annex did not show Section III (vegetable fats and oils). — [EUR-Lex 2025/1909](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1909/oj); [LawPlayer copy](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32025R1909)
- Agricultural lines are not graduated and keep GSP. Leather was reinstated. India's ministry says only 2.66% of exports to the EU are affected, while GTRI says 87% by value. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/european-union-suspends-gsp-export-benefits-india-says-no-impact-on-shipments); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/news?id=68430)

**EU-India FTA**
- Negotiations concluded in late January 2026 and the Commission called it the largest deal either side has concluded. The EU must complete legal scrubbing and translation, Council signature and European Parliament consent, and India must ratify. Squire Patton Boggs says it is unlikely in force before early 2027. India's factsheet lists spices, tea and coffee among sectors with EU tariff reductions or eliminations. — [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/eu-india-free-trade-agreement-concluded-major-global-trade-milestone-2026-01-29_en); [Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/media/dhqd4p2s/eu-india-free-trade-agreement-announced-conclusion-of-negotiations-and-practical-compliance-considerations.pdf); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/global-idt-insights-eu-india-free-trade-agreement-tariff-market-access-highlights/)

### Inferences
- Since most spice lines are already 0% MFN, the FTA and GSP matter mainly for castor and other oils (1515: 5.1% MFN vs 1.6% GSP), ground spice mixes, essential oils and oleoresins (3301) and any chemically defined extracts (Chapter 29 or 38). For chemically defined extracts, graduation of S-6a (chemicals) means full MFN duty for India in 2026-2028. Classification of curcumin is key: 1302 19 vegetable extract (0%) vs 2914/2932/3203 (background knowledge, unverified).
- **REX:** to claim GSP, the Indian exporter must be registered in the REX system and print a statement on origin on the invoice. Consignments of €6,000 or less can carry a statement without a REX number (background knowledge, unverified).
- Background knowledge, unverified, to confirm on TARIC: cinnamon 0906 0%; cardamom 0908 31/32 0%; chilli 0904 21 90 / 0904 22 00 0% (sweet peppers 0904 21 10 9.6%); 1302 19 vegetable extracts 0%; henna 1404 90 00 0%; black cumin and moringa seed oils 1515 90 99 about 5.1% MFN.

### Gaps
- Access2Markets could not be reached, so the official India-specific rates for each 10-digit code were not verified. The report should advise checking TARIC with origin "IN".
- FTA spice-specific tariff schedules have not been published or confirmed.

## 4. Import VAT and intra-EU VAT

### Takeaway
Since 1 January 2022 import VAT reverse charge (ATVAI) is mandatory in France for all VAT-registered businesses. Import VAT is declared and deducted on the same CA3 return, with no cash payment at the border. Edible spices should fall under the 5.5% reduced rate as "food for human consumption". Cosmetic uses (henna for hair, castor oil sold as a cosmetic) take the 20% standard rate. The rate follows the product's intended use and presentation.

### Cited Findings
- ATVAI is mandatory since 1 January 2022 for VAT-registered businesses under CGI art. 286 ter and 286 ter A, after the 2020 amending finance law (art. 181). Import VAT is no longer paid to customs and is reported on the CA3 return, pre-filled from customs data but to be reconciled with import declarations. Implementing details are in BOD n°7440 of 23 November 2021. — [douane.gouv.fr FAQ](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/uploads/files/FAQ%20op%C3%A9rateurs.pdf); [Dougs](https://www.dougs.fr/blog/tva-importation/); [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/autoliquidation-tva-importation-france/)
- CGI art. 278 bis applies the reduced rate to products for human consumption, except alcoholic drinks, caviar, margarines and vegetable fats, confectionery and certain chocolates. — [Propulse by CA food VAT](https://propulsebyca.fr/tva/alimentaire); [Assemblée nationale QE](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/11/questions/QANR5L11QE67155.pdf)

### Inferences
- Edible spices and food-grade ingredients: 5.5%. Henna, clay and cosmetic oils: 20%. Note the exclusion of "graisses végétales" in art. 278 bis, so food-grade vegetable oils for cooking may still be 5.5% (oils are generally reduced-rate). Castor oil is not food and so is 20%. Botanical extracts sold as food supplements: probably 5.5% if food (inference, unverified; seek a rescrit for edge cases).
- **Intra-EU B2B resale (Model A, selling to a buyer in Belgium or Germany, say):** exempt intra-Community supply with the buyer self-accounting, provided the buyer's valid VAT number is checked on VIES. A monthly recapitulative statement (état récapitulatif TVA) is filed, plus the EMEBI statistical survey if dispatches exceed €460,000 a year (EMEBI threshold is background knowledge, unverified).
- **Model B:** the Indian exporter invoices the EU buyer, and the buyer handles import VAT under its own ATVAI. The agent's commission to India is outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B general rule.

### Gaps
- No primary BOFiP source fetched confirming the rate for each product (henna, castor oil, moringa powder). Recommend a rescrit or accountant check.
- EMEBI/DEB thresholds for 2026 not verified.

## 5. Logistics: routes, transit times, freight rates (Red Sea), forwarders, warehousing

### Takeaway
India West Coast (Nhava Sheva/Mundra) to North Europe freight went through big swings in 2026. MSC's FAK to Antwerp was about $1,550 per container in February 2026 and $4,650 in August 2026. Hapag-Lloyd announced base-rate rises to about $5,253-6,753 per 20' box for North Europe from August-September 2026. In September 2026 carriers began a gradual, security-dependent return to Suez routing, and analysts expect overcapacity and falling rates in Q4 2026. LCL from India to North Europe runs about $45-95 per CBM ocean freight plus origin and destination charges.

### Cited Findings
- MSC FAK (16-28 February 2026) from Nhava Sheva: $1,550 to Antwerp and $1,650 to Valencia per 20' or 40', including CAC, piracy risk and ECA surcharges. — [MSC advisory Jan 2026](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/january/price-announcement-india-pakistan-to-europe)
- MSC FAK (1-31 August 2026): Nhava Sheva to Antwerp $4,650, to Valencia $4,750 (20' and 40'). — [MSC advisory June/July 2026](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/june/price-announcement-trade-from-india-and-pakistan-to-europe)
- Hapag-Lloyd: +$2,000 per container from Nhava Sheva/Mundra, taking North Europe to $5,253 per 20' and $4,556 per 40' (effective 1 August 2026 per Container News). Portcast reports +$1,000 to $6,253 per 20' (15 August) and $6,753 (1 September). The reports conflict and are base rates before surcharges. — [Container News](https://container-news.com/hapag-lloyd-raises-india-and-pakistan-europe-freight-rates/); [Portcast](https://www.portcast.io/carrier-advisories/hapag-lloyd-ocean-tariff-increase-india-to-europe-1aug2026)
- Older or undated Container News figure: West India to Rotterdam contract rates of $525 per 20' and $600 per 40', shown as a low-point reference. — [Container News](https://container-news.com/cn-analysis-freight-rates-on-major-indian-trades-see-downward-corrections-after-january-highs/)
- Red Sea/Suez: on 14 September 2026 the Gemini Cooperation (Maersk/Hapag) moved four more services back to Suez. On 16 September COSCO/OOCL made its first southbound transit since the crisis. Sea-Intelligence says Asia-Europe Suez routing has recovered to only about a quarter of pre-crisis levels. The Loadstar expects almost all carriers to make tentative Suez returns in October, and the trade heads into overcapacity in Q4 2026. — [Hellenic Shipping News](https://hellenicshippingnews.com/suez-transits-are-up-59-and-rates-are-about-to-fall); [Carraglobe](https://carraglobe.com/?p=10395); [gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/premier-alliance-prepares-for-asia-n-europe-services-return-to-suez/); [ShipUniverse](https://www.shipuniverse.com/?p=19273)
- LCL India to Germany in 2026: $45-95 per CBM, with Nhava Sheva to Hamburg at $50-90 per CBM, minimum 1 CBM, plus origin, destination and CFS charges. — [Suaid Global](https://suaidglobal.com/de/insights/lcl-shipping-rates/)

### Inferences
- **Transit times** (background knowledge, unverified): Nhava Sheva to Rotterdam or Antwerp about 20-25 days via Suez vs about 30-40 days via the Cape. Le Havre direct calls are fewer, so many shipments trans-ship or go via Antwerp or Rotterdam and then by truck to Paris (about 4-6 h from Antwerp). Fos-Marseille is closer via Suez (about 16-20 days) but about 8 h from Paris by truck. Chennai and Cochin (cardamom, spices, Kerala botanicals) usually trans-ship via Colombo or Singapore, adding about 5-10 days.
- **Container size:** a 20' FCL holds about 10-14 t of bagged spices. At $1,500-5,000 freight, that is about €0.10-0.45/kg, small relative to turmeric at about €2-4/kg FOB or cardamom at about €15-30/kg FOB (commodity price ranges are background knowledge, unverified). Freight therefore matters for low-value bulk (turmeric, chilli) and is negligible for cardamom and extracts.
- **Air freight** for samples and urgent extract orders: courier (DHL Express, FedEx) for under 50 kg, about $5-10/kg from India (background knowledge, unverified).
- **Forwarders** active India-France: DHL Global Forwarding, Kuehne+Nagel, DSV, CEVA (CMA CGM group, which absorbed Bolloré Logistics in 2024), Geodis, plus smaller specialists (background knowledge on market players, unverified).
- **Phytosanitary and packaging:** wooden pallets must be ISPM 15 heat-treated or stamped. Fumigation with ethylene oxide is banned for EU food. Indian spices had ethylene oxide recalls in 2020-2024, so the exporter's steam sterilisation and certificates of analysis matter (background knowledge, unverified).
- **Warehousing near Paris:** food-grade 3PLs in the Paris logistics belt (Rungis/Orly, Roissy, Marne-la-Vallée, Val-d'Oise) charge about €8-15 per pallet per month for storage plus handling (background knowledge, unverified).

- No direct Nhava Sheva to Le Havre or Fos rate quote found. No Drewry or Freightos India-Europe index value captured for September or October 2026.
- No citable 2026 3PL storage tariffs for Île-de-France.

## 6. Incoterms 2020 choice

### Takeaway (background knowledge, no source fetched in this session)
For a new small importer (Model A), the sensible start is buying CIF or CFR Le Havre/Antwerp. The brother books ocean freight, so the importer avoids freight management, but risk transfers at loading in India, so the importer needs cargo insurance or ICC(A) cover under CIF. Selling to French buyers would be DAP or FCA from the Paris-area warehouse. For Model B, the Indian company typically quotes FOB or CIF to the EU buyer. DDP is not advisable for an Indian exporter, who would then need to be importer of record and handle import VAT in France.

### Cited Findings
- No source fetched. ICC Incoterms 2020 is the reference, and the official ICC site was not consulted.

### Inferences

## 8. Payment terms and risk, credit insurance, Bpifrance, LME, currency

### Takeaway
French B2B payment terms are capped by law at 60 days net from invoice, or 45 days end-of-month if contractually agreed. The default is 30 days. Late payers owe a flat €40 recovery indemnity plus late-payment penalties, and the DGCCRF can fine abusive terms up to €2m for a legal entity. A Paris-based importer selling to French buyers must therefore finance up to about 60 days of receivables on top of about 30-60 days of ocean transit. That is the core working-capital issue in Model A. In Model B, the credit risk sits with the Indian exporter, while the agent's own risk is unpaid commission.

### Cited Findings
- LME caps: agreed terms can be at most 60 days net from invoice date or, by derogation, 45 days end of month. "60 days end of month" is not allowed. Periodic invoices: 45 days. Default when nothing is agreed: 30 days from receipt of goods. Sector derogations exist (e.g. transport 30 days end of month). — [Swim Legal](https://www.swim.legal/blog/delai-paiement-facture-entreprises-regles-lme-sanctions); [RubyPayeur](https://rubypayeur.com/blog/loi-lme/)
- Administrative fine up to €2m for a legal entity for illegal or abusive payment terms (L441-16). One source cites €75,000 for natural persons. Flat €40 recovery indemnity plus late penalties, at a minimum of 3x the legal interest rate (penalty rate is commonly ECB rate +10 points if the contract does not specify). — [RubyPayeur indemnité](https://rubypayeur.com/blog/indemnite-forfaitaire-frais-de-recouvrement/); [Le Coin des Entrepreneurs](https://www.lecoindesentrepreneurs.fr/indemnite-forfaitaire-de-recouvrement/); [Banque de France fiche](https://www.banque-france.fr/system/files/2024-04/823415%20Fiche-601.pdf)

### Inferences (background knowledge, unverified in this session)
- **Brother to importer (intra-family):** open account or CAD (documents against payment through banks) is cheap. Indian exporters must still realise export proceeds within the RBI/FEMA deadline (generally 9 months, an RBI rule), so long family credit lines are not unlimited.
- **Model B, India to unknown EU buyers:** advance payment of 30% with balance against copy B/L, or CAD/DP. Letters of credit cost about 0.5-2% all-in and are rarely used for small EU food B2B orders. Open account at 30-60 days with credit insurance once a relationship is established.
- **Trade credit insurance:** Allianz Trade (formerly Euler Hermes), Coface and Atradius dominate France, with SME policies typically about 0.1-0.5% of insured turnover. Bpifrance offers export guarantees and loans, mostly for French exporters, so they are relevant to import financing mainly via short-term working-capital loans.
- **EUR/INR risk:** invoicing in EUR shifts FX risk to the Indian exporter, who can hedge via Indian banks' forwards. INR has generally depreciated against EUR over the long run, which favours EUR invoicing for the French side.

- No current EUR/INR rate captured.

## 9. Insurance: product liability (RC produit) and recall

### Takeaway (background knowledge, unverified)
In Model A the French importer is legally treated as the "producer" for EU product liability and food law (General Food Law Reg. 178/2002 traceability and recall duties, Directive 85/374, recast by Directive (EU) 2024/2853 to apply from December 2026). RC professionnelle/RC produit cover is therefore essential, typically a few hundred to a few thousand euros a year for a small trader. Separate contaminated-product/recall insurance is advisable given recent EU alerts on Indian spices (ethylene oxide, pesticides, aflatoxins). In Model B the agent's exposure is much lower, but professional liability (RC pro) is still recommended.

### Cited Findings
- No sources fetched in this session.

### Inferences
- Model B moves most regulatory and liability burden (importer of record, food-business-operator duties, recall) onto the EU buyer and the Indian exporter. That is the main reason it is the lower-risk entry route.
