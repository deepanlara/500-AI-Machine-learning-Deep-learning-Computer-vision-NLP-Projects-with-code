# EU/French Regulatory Requirements and Market Access for Indian Cosmetic & Industrial Botanical Ingredients (castor, moringa, black cumin oils; henna; multani mitti; ashwagandha/moringa/amla extracts)

Scope note: research done October 2026 with ~16 search/fetch calls. Primary sites reachonline.eu and arkema.com could not be fetched (DNS errors), so some points rely on secondary sources and search-result excerpts. Statements marked "background knowledge, verify" are not source-backed and belong in the writer's caveats.

## 1. REACH (Reg. 1907/2006): registration duty, Annex V exemptions, Only Representative, clays, CLP

### Takeaway
Cold-pressed (or pressed and refined) vegetable oils that are not chemically modified and not classified as hazardous (beyond flammability or skin/eye irritation) are generally exempt from REACH registration under Annex V point 9. Naturally occurring minerals such as clays are exempt under Annex V point 7. The importer of record, who could be the Paris-based trader, still carries supply-chain duties such as SDS provision. Solvent-extracted oils and botanical extracts may fall outside the exemption. Above 1 t/yr per substance, the non-exempt items need registration by the EU importer or by an Only Representative appointed by the Indian exporter.

### Cited Findings
- Annex V point 9 covers vegetable fats, oils and waxes, animal fats, fatty acids and glycerol, but only if they are not chemically modified and do not meet hazard criteria. Substances classified only as flammable, skin irritant or eye irritant keep the exemption. PBT/vPvB substances and SVHCs listed for at least two years are excluded. The 2008 amendment (Reg. 987/2008) moved oils into Annex V on the grounds that they have no hazard beyond flammability and skin/eye irritancy. — [legislation.gov.uk Reg 987/2008](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2008/987/data.pdf); [Annex V text (UK mirror)](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2006/1907/annex/V/2015-06-01/data.xht)
- Cosmetics-industry guidance says pressed, or pressed and refined, fatty oils are exempt from REACH if they are not chemically modified or classified dangerous. Nonaqueous solvent-extracted fatty oils fall under REACH as UVCBs. — [Cosmetics & Toiletries, "REACH Update: Specifics for Naturals"](https://www.cosmeticsandtoiletries.com/regulatory/reach/17385739.html)
- Annex V point 8 exempts "substances occurring in nature, if not chemically modified", subject to the same hazard conditions. The search summary noted that the older Annex V wording refers to Directive 67/548. The current text refers to CLP (Reg. 1272/2008), so the consolidated version on EUR-Lex should be checked. — [Annex V (UK mirror)](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2006/1907/annex/V/2015-06-01/data.xht)
- Castor oil is EC 232-293-8, CAS 8001-79-4 (older or alternative CAS 68956-68-3 also appears). A Vertellus castor oil SDS states it is "exempt from REACH registration requirements (Annex V)". Other supplier SDSs (Univar) list a UK REACH registration-number column for it. — [ECHA document](https://echa.europa.eu/documents/10162/d08ff07a-0b5f-4800-321c-efdba76fdc0b); [Univar SDS](https://www.univarsolutions.co.uk/documents/file/view/code/sds_file/id/2057693/); [Palmer Holland/Vertellus SDS](https://palmerholland.com/getmedia/34ded157-7463-4715-aae4-f6e2b1a8ecb1/MITM09122_2)
- The registration threshold is 1 tonne or more per year per substance, per manufacturer or importer. For importers, tonnage is cumulative across all products. Below 1 t, there is no registration duty, but restrictions, authorisation and supply-chain communication duties, including SDS provision, still apply. — [Your Europe FAQ – Registering chemicals](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/chemicals/registering-chemicals-reach/faq/index_en.htm); [HSA Ireland – Roles and duties](https://hsa.ie/your_industry/chemicals/legislation_enforcement/reach/roles_and_duties_under_reach/)
- Only Representative (OR): a non-EU manufacturer cannot register directly. It can appoint an EU-based OR in writing, and the EU importers then count as downstream users and need not register. Importers should hold confirmation that their tonnage is covered. The OR also takes on the importers' other REACH duties. Without an OR, the EU importer must ensure registration. — [Ecomundo](https://en.ecomundo.eu/blog/each-2018-registration-guide); [CIRS – REACH roles](https://cirs-reach.com/REACH/REACH_Roles_Obligations.html)
- Clays: a fuller's earth SDS states the product is exempt under REACH Annex V point 7 (naturally occurring minerals, if not chemically modified). It describes bentonite as a naturally occurring UVCB mostly made of smectite/montmorillonite (CAS 1318-93-0) with quartz, feldspar, mica and calcite. Cosmetic-clay suppliers (Argile du Velay) state their montmorillonite clays are "not affected by REACH". — [Flints Fuller's Earth SDS](https://www.flints.co.uk/pdf/datasheets/PAT870%20Fuller's%20Earth%20F.pdf); [Argile du Velay Cosblue TDS](https://www.knowde.com/stores/argile-du-velay/products/cosblue)
- There is a caveat for clays. Accessory crystalline quartz (respirable crystalline silica) can drive CLP classification such as STOT RE, which can matter for the Annex V point 7 "not classified" condition. Identifiers also vary: bentonite is CAS 1302-78-9 / EC 215-108-5, and montmorillonite is CAS 1318-93-0. — [Lafaure bentonite](https://knowde.com/stores/lafaure/products/lafaure-calcium-bentonite-clay-superfine); [Flints SDS](https://www.flints.co.uk/pdf/datasheets/PAT870%20Fuller's%20Earth%20F.pdf)

### Inferences
- Castor, moringa and black cumin oils, if cold-pressed (refined is acceptable) and supplied neat without additives, are most likely Annex V exempt. In practice, the Paris trader, acting as EU importer, should hold a written Annex V justification per substance, not a registration. Hexane-extracted castor oil from Indian solvent-extraction plants (common for "commercial/FSG" grade) is a grey zone under the guidance cited above. The importer should ask the family company for the process route.
- Castor oil classification: castor oil is generally not classified under CLP (background knowledge, verify on the ECHA C&L inventory), so it stays inside the Annex V condition. Hydrogenated castor oil and other derivatives (12-HSA, sebacic acid) are chemically modified and would need registration if imported above 1 t/yr.
- Botanical extracts (ashwagandha, moringa leaf, amla) made with solvents are generally treated as UVCB substances. Whether Annex V point 8 applies depends on the process: only manual, mechanical or gravitational processes, dissolution in water, flotation, extraction with water, steam distillation or heating to remove water qualify (background knowledge of the Art. 3(39) definition, verify). Ethanol or hydro-glycolic extracts are likely not "natural substances" and would need registration above 1 t/yr. Volumes from a small trader are likely to stay below 1 t per extract.
- The Indian family company could appoint an OR (fees typically in the thousands of euros per substance per year; no source found). This only makes sense for non-exempt substances above 1 t.
- CLP: even exempt substances must be labelled and come with an SDS if classified. Unclassified neat oils supplied to professionals still usually get an SDS on buyer request, because buyers demand it routinely (see section 3).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the current consolidated Annex V text from EUR-Lex or reachonline.eu (DNS failure). Exact current wording should be verified.
- I could not confirm from ECHA's Registered Substances database whether castor oil (232-293-8), moringa oil or Nigella sativa oil have active registrations. Some companies register voluntarily despite the exemption.
- No source was found on OR fees, or on whether the Indian exporter (or Jayant/others) already has an OR.
- No source was found on the CLP classification of Indian fuller's earth (multani mitti, typically calcium bentonite/montmorillonite with variable quartz).

## 2. Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009: supplier vs Responsible Person, INCI names, henna/lawsone/PPD, heavy metals in clay

### Takeaway
The Cosmetics Regulation applies to finished cosmetic products. The legal duties sit with the brand's Responsible Person (RP), not the raw-material supplier. The supplier's role is to give the RP the data needed for the Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR), so documentation quality is what wins business. Henna as a hair dye has a positive SCCS opinion (2013) under defined use conditions. "Black henna" containing PPD is illegal for skin use and a major recall risk. Clay heavy metals are only tolerated as "technically unavoidable traces", so batch testing is expected.

### Cited Findings
- Manufacturers (the RP) must prepare a Cosmetic Product Safety Report before placing a product on the market. The report requires data on the safety and composition of each ingredient, which is why buyers demand detailed supplier dossiers. CBI notes that there are no direct legal obligations on the non-EU supplier itself. — [CBI ximenia/vegetable oil studies](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1382.pdf); [CBI avocado oil study](https://cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1390.pdf)
- The EU framework is "one of the world's most robust" and sets minimum requirements for ingredients such as moringa oil. — [CBI moringa oil market potential](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/moringa-oil/market-potential)
- Henna (Lawsonia inermis) as a hair dye: SCCS opinion SCCS/1511/13 (19 Sept 2013, ref C169) concluded that the information was sufficient to assess safe use. It found "100 g Henna powder mixed with 300 ml boiling water" safe for consumers. The opinion does not cover other henna extracts with different compositions or body-paint (skin) use. It recommended a reassessment of lawsone genotoxicity. France had reported positive in-vitro mouse lymphoma results and asked for in-vivo testing. An earlier SCCP/0943/05 opinion had found the data insufficient. — [EC SCCS opinion page](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/opinion-lawsonia-inermis-henna-c169_en); [EC publication page](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/lawsonia-inermis-henna-c169_en); [BfR presentation](https://www.BFR.bund.de/cm/343/anmerkungen-zur-risikobewertung-in-kosmetik-kommission-und-scc-x.pdf); [CosmeticOBS](https://client.cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/news-59/sccs-henna-ok-2-chloro-p-phenylenediamine-refused-1996)
- Lawsone (2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone, CI 75480, "Natural Orange 6") is present at 1–2% in dried henna leaves and is the colouring principle. — [EC SCCS opinion](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/opinion-lawsonia-inermis-henna-c169_en); [CosmileEurope – Lawsone](https://cosmileeurope.eu/inci/detail/8204/lawsone/)
- CosIng mirrors list LAWSONE and HENNA entries with "no linked annex records". This was not confirmed against the official CosIng or the consolidated Regulation. — [cosingchecker Lawsone](https://cosingchecker.com/ingredients/34993-lawsone/); [cosingchecker Henna](https://cosingchecker.com/ingredients/34247-henna)
- Black henna and PPD: PPD is permitted only in hair colorants, up to 2% as applied, and is not allowed in other cosmetics. Temporary tattoos count as cosmetics in the EU, so PPD "black henna" for skin is illegal. Samples have been measured at 0.4–29.5% PPD. A RAPEX/Safety Gate alert concerned an Indian "black henna" hair dye with PPD above 7.3% (more than 2% in the final solution). — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/health/How-safe-are-e28098black-hennae28099-tattoos.aspx); [CosmeticOBS recall 3 Jan 2020](https://cosmeticobs.com/en/articles/recalls-of-products-27/recall-of-products-january-3-2020-5095)
- Clay heavy metals: suppliers acknowledge that traces of heavy metals occur naturally in clays and say the EU tolerates them as technically unavoidable. The US Cosmetic Ingredient Review (2003) assessed bentonite, fuller's earth and montmorillonite as cosmetic ingredients. — [Argile du Velay TDS](https://www.knowde.com/stores/argile-du-velay/products/coswhite); [PubMed – CIR clay report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12851164/)
- Laboratory-grade fuller's earth SDSs state "not for food, drug... use", so the grade matters. — [LabAlley SDS](https://media.laballey.com/docbuilder/fuller-safety-data-sheet-671bb87634b24.pdf)

### Inferences
- INCI names (background knowledge, verify on official CosIng):
  - Ricinus Communis (Castor) Seed Oil (EU CosIng uses "Ricinus Communis Seed Oil")
  - Moringa Oleifera Seed Oil
  - Nigella Sativa Seed Oil
  - Lawsonia Inermis Leaf Powder / Lawsonia Inermis Leaf Extract (CosIng also has "Henna")
  - Multani mitti is typically sold as "Solum Fullonum" (the INCI for fuller's earth), or as Bentonite or Montmorillonite depending on mineralogy
  - Withania Somnifera Root Extract (cited above as present in inventory)
  - Moringa Oleifera Leaf Extract
  - Phyllanthus Emblica Fruit Extract (amla)
- Lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium and antimony are Annex II prohibited substances. Traces are only allowed if technically unavoidable and safe (Art. 17) (background knowledge, verify). In practice, French brands and safety assessors expect clay COAs with heavy metals (Pb, As, Hg, Cd, Ni, Sb), microbiology, and often asbestos/crystalline silica statements. Indicative limits often cited are the German BVL "technically avoidable" guidance values (e.g. Pb ≈ 20 ppm in powders), but these were not verified here.
- Henna: supply only pure Lawsonia inermis leaf powder (with lawsone % on COA). Never supply "black", "neutral" or "compound" henna blends that might contain PPD or metallic salts, and get a PPD-free analytical statement per lot. Henna for hair dyeing goes to hair-dye brands. Body-art henna is not covered by the SCCS opinion, which increases the RP's liability. Expect French buyers to be cautious.
- Hair-dye RPs have to show the henna matches the SCCS-assessed material. Supplying specifications comparable to the SCCS dossier (lawsone content, particle size, impurities) is a commercial advantage.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from the consolidated Regulation or official CosIng whether henna or lawsone has an Annex III or IV entry, or the exact Annex III entry number for PPD. Note that the Commission has, in background knowledge, banned lawsone as a standalone substance in some contexts. Verify on EUR-Lex before writing anything definitive.
- No official EU numeric heavy-metal limits for cosmetic clays were found. None exist in the Regulation itself (background knowledge). The German BVL values and member-state guidance were not retrieved.
- No information was found on any SCCS opinion after 2013 on lawsone genotoxicity, or whether the 2021 republication changed anything.

## 3. Documentation buyers will demand (COA, SDS, allergens, IFRA, Nagoya/ABS, COSMOS, CMR)

### Takeaway
A French/EU buyer will expect a full technical dossier per ingredient. It should cover the TDS/specification, COA per batch, SDS (CLP format, in French for France), allergen declaration, CMR/impurities statements, origin and traceability, animal-testing statement, and certificates (COSMOS/Ecocert, organic). For Indian botanicals there is also Nagoya/ABS evidence under India's Biological Diversity Act (as amended 2023) to support the buyer's due diligence under EU Reg. 511/2014. IFRA certificates matter only for the perfumery/Grasse channel.

### Cited Findings
- The CBI technical dossier list covers a Technical Data Sheet, Safety Data Sheet, Certificate of Analysis, allergen declaration, traceability information, sourcing policies and certifications. The allergen declaration is "one of the most important elements" for EU labelling compliance. — [CBI essential oils market entry](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/essential-oils/market-entry)
- Buyers also need physical, chemical, microbiological and toxicological characteristics and animal-testing status. CITES status may need to be shown for plant species. — [CBI study](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/1384.pdf); [CBI study](https://www.cbi.eu/sites/default/files/pdf/research/2297.pdf)
- CBI describes three tiers of buyer requirements: mandatory, additional (competitive) and niche. They include quality minima, sustainable and ethical practices, and packaging. The main entry channel is via European distributors. — [CBI](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/essential-oils/market-entry); [CBI moringa oil](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-cosmetics/moringa-oil/market-potential)
- EU Reg. 511/2014 (Nagoya compliance) requires users of genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge to exercise due diligence that they were accessed under the provider country's ABS law. Users must declare at the final product development stage, keep information for 20 years, and obtain a permit or mutually agreed terms (MAT), or stop utilisation, if uncertain. The Commission guidance was revised on 12 Jan 2021. — [legislation.gov.uk Reg 511/2014 Art 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2014/511/article/4/data.htm?view=plain); [WUR summary](https://subsites.wur.nl/en/EU-Regulation-5112014-on-the-Implementation-of-the-Nagoya-Protocol.htm)
- India's Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act 2023 took effect 1 April 2024. The new Biological Diversity Rules (G.S.R. 665(E)) were notified 22 Oct 2024 and took effect 21 Dec 2024. Foreign-controlled entities need prior NBA approval (Form 7). Indian entities follow the Section 7 route, with prior approval at commercialisation via Form 9 per one summary. Fees are reported at ₹5,000–10,000, with decisions within 180 days. — [Mondaq overview](https://www.mondaq.com/india/life-sciences-biotechnology-nanotechnology/1350780/the-biological-diversity-amendment-bill-2023-an-overview); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-navigating-change-unravelling-the-biological-diversity-amendment-act-2023); [BioSpectrum](https://www.biospectrumindia.com/views/17/23669/decoding-the-biological-diversity-amendment-act-2023.html)
- COSMOS: the "approved raw materials" database (non-organic) and the "certified raw materials" database (organic content) are separate. Approval relies on the manufacturer's documentary evidence and is valid for the current year. Entries list the percentage of Physically vs Chemically Processed Agro-Ingredients (PPAI/CPAI) and the certifier, e.g. Ecocert Greenlife. V3.1 approvals could only be used until 1 Dec 2025. — [COSMOS approved raw materials DB](https://www.cosmos-standard.org/en/databases/approved-raw-materials/?page=12); [COSMOS certified raw materials DB](https://www.cosmos-standard.org/en/databases/certified-raw-materials/?page=232); [Element – COSMOS overview](https://www.element.com/resources/articles/cosmos-standard-for-natural-and-organic-cosmetic-products)
- Aroma-Zone requires suppliers to sign a charter and has suppliers assessed by EcoVadis. Its selection criteria cover origin, quality, traceability, environmental impact and social/ethical commitments. — [Aroma-Zone – Achats responsables](https://www.aroma-zone.com/content/notre-demarche-achat-responsable)

### Inferences
- Practical dossier checklist per ingredient (synthesis):
  - Specification/TDS
  - Batch COA: acid value, peroxide value, fatty-acid profile, heavy metals, microbiology for powders and clays, pesticide residues for organic grades, lawsone % for henna, PPD-absent for henna
  - SDS in CLP/REACH Annex II format, in French
  - EU 26 allergens statement (relevant mainly for essential oils, but buyers ask anyway)
  - CMR, nanomaterial, GMO, irradiation, TSE/BSE-free, animal-testing and vegan statements
  - Residual-solvent statement (hexane) for oils
  - Country of origin and botanical identity (Latin binomial, plant part)
  - REACH status letter (Annex V justification)
  - Nagoya/ABS statement: NBA approval reference, or a reasoned explanation of why utilisation is out of scope. Pure commodity trade of oils without R&D on genetic properties is often out of EU 511/2014 scope, but extracts used in new product R&D can be in scope. Not verified.
  - Organic certificates: EU organic (Reg. 2018/848) plus COSMOS certified or approved
- IFRA: relevant only if the material is used in fragrance compounds. Castor-oil derivatives such as undecylenic aldehyde and sebacates, and Nigella or henna absolutes, may need IFRA conformity documents for Grasse buyers. Neat castor oil is mainly a solvent or fixative carrier there. Not source-verified.
- Indian-side ABS: as a domestic Indian exporter, the family company's compliance with the BD Act (Form 9/benefit-sharing for commercial utilisation, or claimed exemptions for normally traded commodities) should be documented. The amended Act exempts "codified traditional knowledge" users and cultivated medicinal plants in some cases (background knowledge, verify).

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether EU authorities accept NBA approval as sufficient due-diligence evidence.
- The scope of the Indian exemption for "normally traded commodities" (NTAC list) for castor, henna and moringa was not checked.
- No EcoVadis/RSPO-equivalent requirement was found for these specific ingredients. RSPO is not relevant (non-palm).
- No French-language SDS legal requirement source was retrieved (background knowledge: SDS must be in the official language of the member state).

## 4. Pharma-grade castor oil: Ph. Eur., GMP for excipients, CEP

### Takeaway
Ph. Eur. has monographs for virgin castor oil (Ricini oleum virginale, 0051), refined castor oil (Ricini oleum raffinatum, 2367) and hydrogenated castor oil. Fatty-acid limits were tightened in edition 10. Selling to pharma requires Ph. Eur. conformity, excipient GMP (EXCiPACT/IPEC-type), audits and change control. This is realistic only via established Indian pharma-grade producers or EU distributors, not as a trader's opening segment.

### Cited Findings
- EMA's herbal monograph requires castor oil to comply with Ph. Eur. Ricini oleum virginale (0051) or Ricini oleum raffinatum (2367). Ph. Eur. also covers hydrogenated castor oil. — [EMA draft EU herbal monograph](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/draft-european-union-herbal-monograph-ricinus-communis-l-oleum_en.pdf); [EMA Ricini oleum page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/ricini-oleum)
- Both monographs were updated in Ph. Eur. edition 10, adding fatty-acid limits for oleic acid and isomer (2.5–6.0%) and any other fatty acid (max 1.0%). — [EMA addendum assessment report](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/addendum-assessment-report-ricinus-communis-l-oleum_en.pdf)
- Commercial examples include a Vantage "Textron castor oil refined" grade (cold-pressed and refined, conforming to Ph. Eur.) and Glentham's specification referencing "Castor Oil, Virgin" Ph. Eur. — [Vantage](https://www.vantagegrp.com/Product/TEXTRON-CASTOR-OIL-REFINED); [Glentham spec](https://www.glentham.com/en/products/product/GL4383/specification/)

### Inferences
- A CEP is optional for excipients. Many pharma buyers accept Ph. Eur. conformity with a GMP/EXCiPACT certificate and an audit. The CEP search in EDQM's database returned nothing in this research.
- Likely buyers: EU pharma excipient distributors (e.g. Cooper (France), Fagron, Caesar & Loretz type players, not verified), laxative/herbal-medicinal manufacturers, and the cosmetics-pharma crossover (dermo-cosmetics). Volumes are small and margins higher, but supplier qualification takes 6–18 months (not verified).

### Gaps
- No CEP holders for castor oil were identified. The EDQM database was not queried directly.
- No source was found naming French pharma buyers of castor oil.

## 5. Castor oil derivatives in France: Arkema/Rilsan, lubricants, aroma chemicals; is it realistic for a small trader?

### Takeaway
The industrial castor chain is vertically integrated and India-centred. Arkema sources most of its castor oil from Gujarat through long-term partners, notably Jayant Agro-Organics, co-founder of the Pragati sustainable castor programme with BASF and Solidaridad. Arkema processes it into Rilsan PA11 and other derivatives. A small trader is unlikely to sell commodity castor oil to Arkema or BASF. The realistic castor opportunity is cosmetic/pharma grades (cold-pressed, organic, Ph. Eur.) in drum/IBC volumes to SMEs and distributors.

### Cited Findings
- Arkema sources most of its castor oil from India, mainly Gujarat. Rilsan PA11 is derived 100% from castor oil. Arkema's Singapore (Jurong Island) plant, opened in 2022, increased Rilsan capacity by 50%. — [American Chemistry Council blog](https://www.americanchemistry.com/chemistry-in-america/news-trends/blog-post/2021/arkema-s-sustainable-castor-oil-initiative-advances-sustainable-farming-to-produce-bio-based-polymer-materials); [Arkema – castor oil expertise (2025)](https://www.arkema.com/global/en/resources/post/2025/castor-oil-unique-expertise-worldwide/) (search excerpt; page could not be fetched)
- Pragati was launched May 2016 by Arkema, BASF, Jayant Agro-Organics and Solidaridad, under the SuCCESS code. The year-9 update (Feb 2026) reports more than 10,000 certified farmers, more than 12,000 ha, and more than 140,000 t of certified seeds cumulatively. Phase 3 runs 2023–2026. — [Arkema HPP Pragati year 9 news](https://hpp.arkema.com/en/media/newslist/news/global/csr/2026/20260210-pragati-year-9-sustainable-castor-bean-program/); [Arkema HPP Pragati page](https://hpp.arkema.com/en/sustainability/pragati-sustainable-castor-initiative); [Adhesives Mag year 8](https://adhesivesmag.com/articles/101571-arkema-and-partners-publish-year-eight-impacts-for-sustainable-castor-bean-program)
- Gujarat is described as growing the "majority", or about 80%, of world castor supply in older materials. — [ACC blog](https://www.americanchemistry.com/chemistry-in-america/news-trends/blog-post/2021/arkema-s-sustainable-castor-oil-initiative-advances-sustainable-farming-to-produce-bio-based-polymer-materials)
- The Pragati programme is also marketed to beauty buyers (SuCCESS-certified castor for cosmetics). — [Premium Beauty News](https://www.premiumbeautynews.com/en/founders-of-first-sustainable,21294)

### Inferences
- Arkema processes castor at Marseille (Saint-Menet: amino-11 monomer) and makes polymer at Serquigny (background knowledge, verify). This is an industrial chain buying thousands of tonnes on long-term contracts from integrated Indian crushers (Jayant Agro, possibly others). A trader cannot compete on price or certification (SuCCESS) there.
- Lubricants, biopolymer and paints (e.g. TotalEnergies lubricants, Condat, Oleon/Avril in the oleochemicals space; not verified) also buy at scale through oleochemical distributors. They are possible only if the family company is itself a large crusher or refiner with a SuCCESS or ISCC certificate.
- Grasse aroma chemicals: castor derivatives (undecylenic acid/aldehyde, heptanal, sebacic acid) come mainly from Arkema and the Indian/Chinese chemical majors. Grasse houses buy finished aroma chemicals, not crude castor oil. Henna and black cumin CO2 extracts or absolutes are niche perfumery possibilities.
- Realistic castor niche for a small trader: organic, cold-pressed, hexane-free "huile de ricin" for hair, lash and nail products. French consumer demand for castor oil as a DIY hair-growth product is strong (background knowledge). Sell in 20–200 kg units to indie brands, DIY retailers and façonniers.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Arkema's page to confirm named supplier relationships beyond the Pragati partnership. "Arkema sources directly from Jayant" is inferred from Pragati, not stated.
- No French or EU castor oil import volume or price data was retrieved.

## 6. French natural cosmetics market and realistic buyer segments

### Takeaway
France is the world's largest cosmetics exporter (€22.4 bn exported in 2025, about 14% world share). It has about 6,300 establishments, and Cosmetic Valley has 660 members, 80% of them SMEs. The realistic entry points for a small importer are:
- natural/DIY ingredient retailers (Aroma-Zone and peers)
- ingredient distributors that stock exotic oils and powders
- private-label façonniers in Cosmetic Valley and the South
- indie and "Ayurvedic-inspired" natural brands

Large groups (L'Oréal, LVMH, Clarins) buy through qualified distributors.

### Cited Findings
- Cosmetic Valley key figures: about 6,300 establishments in France, 226,300 jobs, €71 bn national turnover, €22.4 bn exported in 2025. France is world #1 exporter with 14% share (2023), ahead of the US (8%) and Germany (7%). ## 7. Ashwagandha / moringa / amla extracts in cosmetics: restrictions

### Takeaway
No EU cosmetic annex restriction was found for Withania somnifera root extract (listed in the CosIng inventory). The ashwagandha regulatory controversy is on the food-supplement side (RASFF rejections, Belgian ban, Dutch and Danish scrutiny), which affects reputation but not cosmetic legality. Moringa leaf and amla extracts appear unrestricted in cosmetics. The main issues for these extracts are REACH status (solvent extracts are not Annex V), Nagoya/ABS, pesticide residues and the solvent/carrier declaration.

### Cited Findings
- "Withania Somnifera Root Extract" and "Withania Somnifera Leaf/Root Extract" are in the CosIng inventory, with no linked annex records according to a third-party mirror. — [cosingchecker Withania root extract](https://cosingchecker.com/ingredients/60191-withania-somnifera-root-extract); [Leaf/root extract](https://cosingchecker.com/ingredients/86248-withania-somnifera-leafroot-extract)
- Food side: in the EU Novel Food catalogue all plant parts are "not novel" in food supplements. There were 26 RASFF border rejections of ashwagandha supplements in 2021–2024. Belgium treats it as medicinal and does not allow it in supplements, and the Netherlands has it under observation. The UK FSA considers it non-novel only for supplements. — [Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/ashwagandha-safety-questioned-in-europe-and-india/); [UK FSA consultation](https://www.food.gov.uk/news-alerts/consultations/ashwagandha); [VFI EuroTrends](https://eu-assets.contentstack.com/v3/assets/bltd5b5e8fe28aae49e/blt611e89b7bdb600c5/669faa1c38d6b47a960ed782/VFI-AA_20WP-EuroTrends-0620.pdf)

### Inferences
- In cosmetics, ashwagandha, moringa leaf and amla extracts can be sold if the RP's safety assessment is supported. Withania contains withanolides. Buyers may ask for marker content and pyrrolizidine/aflatoxin statements for botanical powders (not verified).
- Extracts are more often bought as finished cosmetic actives from EU extract makers that do the formulation (glycerin or propanediol carriers, preservation). Selling raw Indian extracts directly to brands requires the trader to supply full INCI composition (extract plus carrier plus preservative) and microbiology.

### Gaps
- Not verified on the official CosIng or the consolidated Regulation. No SCCS opinion on Withania, Moringa or Phyllanthus emblica extracts was found.
- No data was found on EU pesticide-residue or ethylene-oxide issues for Indian botanical powders in cosmetics. EtO has been a recurring issue in Indian food spices and herbs (background knowledge).
