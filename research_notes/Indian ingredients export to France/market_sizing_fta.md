# EU/France Import Market Sizing for Indian Spices, Botanical Oils & Extracts; Brochure Claim Verification; India-EU FTA Status (as of Oct 2026)

Method note: research was done 8 Oct 2026. WebFetch could not resolve most domains (ec.europa.eu, cbi.eu, wits.worldbank.org, etc.), so figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not full-page reads. Primary databases (Eurostat Comext, ITC Trade Map, UN Comtrade) were not queried directly. Treat every number as "verify in Comext/Trade Map before printing."

## 1. Brochure claim verification (verdicts)

### Takeaway
The brochure's spice figures are real Eurostat numbers from **2022** (published Dec 2023) and are now outdated by the 2023 release. The "fastest growing" turmeric claim is misleading. Most castor oil figures could not be traced to any source. They are close to, but higher than, published WITS/Comtrade figures, and two of the castor claims are overstated.

### Cited Findings

**Turmeric (HS 091030)**
- "EU imports 15,800 MT": **ACCURATE BUT OUTDATED (2022).** Eurostat: in 2022, turmeric (15,800 t) was the 5th most-imported spice into the EU, and 77% came from India — [Eurostat news, 27 Dec 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1)
- 2023 update: EU imported 16,000 t of turmeric (4th most-imported spice), **79% from India** — [Eurostat "Flavours of 2023", 2 Dec 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1); [Cyprus Mail coverage](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/12/02/ginger-most-imported-spice-in-eu-for-2023/)
- 2024: Mundus Agri reports 17,197 t of turmeric imported into the EU in 2024. The value figure in the snippet ("EUR 42,000 million") is clearly garbled and probably means ~€42 million. It also reports India's 2025 share as about 73% by value — [Mundus Agri](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/spices-eu-spends.n36669.html). The source behind these figures was not visible, so treat them as indicative.
- "€39.9M" and "18,136 MT": **UNVERIFIABLE.** I found no source with these numbers. 18,136 t could be a pan-European figure (EU+UK+others) or a later year, and €39.9M is consistent in size with Mundus Agri's ~€42M for 2024. The brochure mixes two different volumes for the same product without saying which year or which geography each covers.
- "India 77% share": **ACCURATE FOR 2022, OUTDATED.** The 2023 share was 79% ([Eurostat 2024](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1)). By value, India was about 73% in 2025 ([Mundus Agri](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/spices-eu-spends.n36669.html)).
- "+142% since 2012, fastest growing spice": **MISLEADING.** The +142% is real: Eurostat's 2022 release says EU imports of ginger and turmeric more than doubled between 2012 and 2022, with ginger up 149% and turmeric up 142%, the largest increases among the most-traded spices — [Eurostat 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1). But on Eurostat's own numbers **ginger, not turmeric, grew fastest**. The comparison base is 2012 to 2022, which is now old. CBI's 2017-2021 growth figure for turmeric was more modest: +8.0% a year by volume and +3.7% a year by value — [CBI turmeric market potential](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-health-products/turmeric/market-potential)

**Ginger (HS 091011/091012)**
- "EU imports 122,900 MT, most imported spice": **ACCURATE FOR 2022, OUTDATED.** 2022: ginger was the top spice at 122,900 t, with China as main origin at 54% — [Eurostat 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1). In 2023 ginger was still the most-imported spice, but volume fell to about 114,000 t and **China's share dropped to 43%** — [Cyprus Mail / Eurostat 2023 data](https://cyprus-mail.com/2024/12/02/ginger-most-imported-spice-in-eu-for-2023/)
- "54% from China": **OUTDATED** (43% in 2023, same source as above).
- "+149% since 2012": **ACCURATE (2012 to 2022 basis)** — [Eurostat 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1). Note that CBI says dried-ginger imports peaked in 2022 and fell in 2023 and 2024, so the growth story has reversed recently — [CBI dried ginger market potential](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/dried-ginger/market-potential)
- Relevance to India: Eurostat's ginger total is mostly **fresh** ginger, where China dominates. CBI estimates European imports of **dried** ginger from developing countries at about 18,000 t in 2024, of which about 12,000 t is powdered or crushed (+7.6% a year from about 9,000 t in 2020). China is about 50% of dried ginger (about 9,200 t). **India supplied about 1,810 t, roughly 10%** — [CBI dried ginger](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/dried-ginger/market-potential). An Indian exporter selling ginger powder or extract competes in this ~18,000 t dried segment, not the 114,000-123,000 t headline figure.

**Castor oil (HS 151530)**
- "EU imports $308.7M / 151,559 MT": **UNVERIFIABLE; the value looks high.** Published figures:
  - WITS/Comtrade: EU imports from all origins in 2023 were about **$260.5M / 152.3 million kg**, making the EU the second-largest importer after China — [WITS 2023 imports](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/ALL/year/2023/tradeflow/Imports/partner/WLD/product/151530)
  - Tradologie gives 2024 EU imports of **$264.42M / 162.87 million kg** — [Tradologie](https://www.tradologie.com/blogs/why-castor-oil-export-from-india-holds-global-importance/)
  - The brochure's volume (151,559 t) is close to the 2023 and 2024 tonnage. The $308.7M value may reflect 2022, when prices were higher, but I could not confirm this.
- "From India $300.4M / 148,291 MT": **UNVERIFIABLE.** WITS shows the EU importing **$251.8M / about 151.6 million kg** from India in 2024 — [WITS 2024 imports from India](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/All/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/IND/product/151530)
- "India share 97.3%": **PLAUSIBLE, ORDER OF MAGNITUDE CORRECT.** Taking the 2024 sources above together, $251.8M from India against about $264M total gives about 95%. That is an inference across two sources.
- "India >90% world production": **SLIGHTLY OVERSTATED.**
  - India had 89.8% of world castor seed output in 2020 — [AERC/DES Agri report](https://desagri.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2021-22-Castor-Crop-Cultivation-in-Gujarat.pdf)
  - About 88.5% in 2022 (FAOSTAT-based secondary source) — [Tridge/aggregators via search](https://www.tridge.com/intelligences/castor-bean-castor-seed/export)
  - "About 85-90%" is defensible; ">90%" is not.
- "France alone imports $90.3M": **UNVERIFIABLE FOR THE YEAR GIVEN; plausible for a recent year.**
  - France's HS 151530 imports were $136M in 2022 (third-largest importer worldwide after China at $504M and the Netherlands at $173M; Germany was $86.4M) — [OEC](https://oec.world/en/profile/hs/castor-oil-or-fractions-not-chemically-modified)
  - About $100M in 2023, down 25% — [TrendEconomy France 151530](https://trendeconomy.com/data/h2/France/151530)
  - $90.3M could be the 2024 figure, but I could not confirm this.
- "30-35% of Indian castor derivative exports go to EU": **UNVERIFIABLE / LIKELY OVERSTATED for castor oil.**
  - EU purchases of about $252M in 2024, set against India's roughly $1.0-1.04bn castor oil exports, give about 25% — [WITS](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/All/year/2024/tradeflow/Imports/partner/IND/product/151530); [Tradologie](https://www.tradologie.com/blogs/why-castor-oil-export-from-india-holds-global-importance/)
  - Derivatives such as hydrogenated castor oil, 12-HSA and sebacic acid fall under other HS codes and were not checked. Sources consistently name **China as the largest single buyer** of Indian castor oil — [Seair blog](https://www.seair.co.in/blog/castor-oil-export-from-india.aspx)
- "Top buyers France, Germany, Netherlands": **PARTLY ACCURATE; the order is wrong.** Within the EU, the **Netherlands** is the largest importer, ahead of France and Germany (2022: NL $173M, FR $136M, DE $86.4M) — [OEC](https://oec.world/en/profile/hs/castor-oil-or-fractions-not-chemically-modified)
- Re-export hubs: the Netherlands ($47.4M), France ($30.1M) and Germany ($26.4M) were themselves the next-largest castor oil exporters after India in 2023 — [WITS 2023 exports](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade/comtrade/en/country/ALL/year/2023/tradeflow/Exports/partner/WLD/product/151530). This points to re-export or processing.

### Inferences
- The brochure appears to be based on the Eurostat Dec 2023 release (2022 data) for spices. Replacing those numbers with the 2023 figures (turmeric 16,000 t at 79% India; ginger 114,000 t at 43% China), or better, 2024/2025 Comext data, would make it current.
- For French buyers, the castor numbers should be re-pulled from ITC Trade Map with year labels. As printed, unlabeled figures that differ by 15-20% from Comtrade could undermine credibility with professional buyers.

### Gaps
- Full Eurostat Comext 2024/2025 figures for each spice could not be pulled because the database was not reachable.
- No source found for €39.9M, 18,136 MT, $308.7M, $300.4M, 148,291 MT, $90.3M, or the 30-35% derivatives share.

## 2. Latest EU and France import figures for other products (cardamom, cinnamon, chilli, botanicals)

### Takeaway
For India, the EU's high-share spice is turmeric. In ginger, cardamom and cinnamon India is a minor supplier, behind China (ginger), Guatemala (cardamom) and Vietnam/Sri Lanka (cinnamon). In chilli/paprika China is dominant.

### Cited Findings
- EU total spice imports from outside the EU: **397,000 t (2022)** and **339,800 t (2023)**. China was the largest supplier, with 39% in 2023 — [Eurostat 2022](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20231227-1); [Efanews on Eurostat 2023](https://www.efanews.eu/item/46039-eu-eurostat-spice-imports-at-339-800-tons-in-2023.html)
- Cinnamon: 13,300 t in 2023 (5th most-imported spice). The main origin was **Vietnam at 35%**. No India share was given — [Eurostat 2023 release via search](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1)
- Paprika/capsicum (0904 21/22): **China 73%** of EU imports in 2023 — [Eurostat 2023](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20241202-1)
- Cardamom:
  - Europe imported 2,700 t from developing countries in 2023, with Guatemala dominant (86% of Germany's supply) — [CBI cardamom](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/cardamom-0/market-potential)
  - India's shipments to the EU rose 63% in 2024 against 2023, but were still only about **6% of EU cardamom volume** in 2024 — [Mundus Agri](https://www.mundus-agri.eu/news/cardamom-slightly-bumpy-ride-europe.n34678.html)
- Turmeric demand drivers and hubs (CBI):
  - Most attractive markets are the UK, the Netherlands, Germany, **France**, Spain and Poland. The Netherlands is a trade hub with large re-exports — [CBI turmeric](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/natural-ingredients-health-products/turmeric/market-potential)
  - Most turmeric goes to the food industry. Supplements and herbal medicinal products each have separate access requirements (same source).
- Ginger hubs:
  - CBI names Spain, France and Belgium among the biggest European dried-ginger importers.
  - The UK imports about 7,000 t (China 77%, India about 10%).
  - The Netherlands is a hub; Germany increasingly imports direct rather than via NL — [CBI dried ginger](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/dried-ginger/market-potential)

### Inferences
- Positioning: turmeric and curcumin are where India is the default origin. For ginger, cardamom and chilli, a French buyer would need a reason to switch from China or Guatemala, such as organic certification, specific varieties (for example Kerala ginger or Byadgi/Kashmiri chilli), or residue compliance.

### Gaps
- No France-specific 2023-2025 import figures by spice were found. Comext extraction is needed for codes 0910 30, 0910 11/12, 0908, 0906, 0904 with partner India and reporter FR.
- No reliable EU import figures found for moringa, ashwagandha or other extracts (HS 1302 19), henna (HS 1404 90) or fuller's earth (HS 2508 20). HS 1302 is too aggregated to isolate these.
- No 2024/2025 India share found for EU chilli or cinnamon.

## 3. Market trends in France (supplements, organic, cosmetics, ethnic food, diaspora)

### Takeaway
France's food-supplement market passed €3bn in 2025 and is dominated by pharmacy sales, with stress/sleep/vitality the core segments. This favours ashwagandha, turmeric/curcumin and moringa, but under tightening safety scrutiny. The Indian diaspora is small, so ethnic-food demand alone is a niche.

### Cited Findings
- French food-supplement market:
  - **>€3bn in 2025**, +2.6% on 2024 (Synadiet-IQVIA observatory), up from €2.9bn in 2024 (about 400 companies, 16,000 jobs) — [Le Moniteur des Pharmacies](https://www.lemoniteurdespharmacies.fr/business/retail/officine/complements-alimentaires-un-marche-a-3-milliards-deuros); [Synadiet](https://www.synadiet.org/les-complements-alimentaires/leur-consommation/)
  - Channels: pharmacy about 55% of sales, organic stores about 8%, distance selling >10%. About 70% of the market is vitality/immunity, stress/sleep and respiratory. Deloitte forecast €4bn by 2030 (same sources).
- Ashwagandha in France:
  - Suspended 2014-2015, then allowed again in supplements via EU mutual recognition.
  - **ANSES (April 2024)** advised caution for pregnant women, minors and people with thyroid or liver conditions, without banning it.
  - **Denmark banned ashwagandha in 2024.**
  - Claims that only the root is permitted were not verified against an official source — [summary of French sources via search](https://fr.news.yahoo.com/ashwagandha-danger-interdiction-que-sait-on-sur-ce-complement-alimentaire-star-de-la-medecine-ayurvedique-095616963.html)
- Indian diaspora in France:
  - India's MEA estimates about **119,000 overseas Indians in metropolitan France**, plus about 367,000 in overseas departments (Réunion, Guadeloupe, etc.) — [Wikipedia: Indians in France (citing MEA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_France)
  - An earlier MEA 2017 figure for metropolitan France was 48,791 (same source).
- Indian restaurants (commercial data vendor; methodology unclear):
  - About **1,123 in France** as of April 2026, +1.94% since 2023.
  - **506 in Île-de-France** as of Oct 2025, then Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes (144) and PACA (103) — [Rentech Digital](https://rentechdigital.com/smartscraper/business-report-details/list-of-indian-restaurants-in-france)

### Inferences
- For a Paris-based agent, the more scalable channels are likely B2B ingredient distributors serving pharmacy-led supplement brands, organic/natural cosmetics makers (castor oil, moringa oil, henna, amla, multani mitti) and food manufacturers (turmeric oleoresin, curcumin), rather than the ethnic-retail channel.
- Ashwagandha carries regulatory reputational risk in the EU (ANSES caution, Danish ban). Shilajit and noni face novel-food or safety questions in the EU, which were not verified here and should be checked against the EU Novel Food Catalogue.

### Gaps
- No quantified figures found for the French organic-spice market or natural-cosmetics ingredient demand.
- No reliable France-level data found for the moringa or ayurvedic-extract market.

## 4. India-EU FTA status (Oct 2026), MFN duties and GSP

### Takeaway
Negotiations were **concluded on 27 Jan 2026**. Legal scrubbing was reported complete in August 2026, and **signing is targeted for December 2026 in Brussels**. The agreement is **not yet in force**: it still needs European Parliament consent and Council approval, plus national ratifications if it is a mixed agreement. Indian spices are in the "immediate duty elimination" basket once it applies. Until then, MFN rates or GSP apply, and India's GSP coverage for agricultural chapters has not been graduated.

### Cited Findings
- Conclusion:
  - EU and India concluded FTA negotiations at the 16th India-EU Summit in New Delhi on 27 Jan 2026 — [EC press release IP/26/184](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_184/IP_26_184_EN.pdf); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/27/india-eu-trade-deal-tariffs-exports.html); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/01/eu-india-conclude-negotiations-fta.html)
  - Liberalisation coverage: 96.6% for India and 99.3% for the EU — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3a4b2c71-5df9-4cb5-bb1d-16a709a4bc26)
- Indian factsheet: **70.4% of EU tariff lines, covering 90.7% of India's exports by value**, get immediate duty elimination at entry into force, explicitly including **spices**, tea, coffee, textiles and leather — [PIB factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2219146&reg=3&lang=2); [Embassy of India Paris factsheet PDF](https://www.eoiparis.gov.in/content/Paris_DOCPR_020326.pdf)
- Borderlex (14 Sep 2026): the EU will fully remove tariffs on most Indian seafood and on some fruits, vegetables and spices. There are 26 different staging formulae of 1-10 years for other agri-food lines — [Borderlex](https://borderlex.net/2026/09/14/eu-india-series-reduced-ambition-on-agri-food/)
- Status and timeline:
  - June 2026: the Austrian envoy said formal signing was expected in Brussels in December — [ANI, 30 Jun 2026](https://aninews.in/news/world/asia/everybodys-optimistic-india-eu-fta-in-legal-scrubbing-phase-signing-slated-for-december-says-austrian-envoy-robert-zischg20260630163826/)
  - August 2026: legal scrubbing completed, with EU internal approvals and translations underway and signing expected by year-end — [ANI, 20 Aug 2026](https://aninews.in/news/business/india-eu-fta-legal-scrubbing-completed-signing-expected-by-year-end-govt-sources20260820171517/)
  - Minister Goyal has said the deal is on track for December signing — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/piyush-goyal-says-india-eu-free-trade-agreement-on-track-for-december-signing-after-legal-scrub)
- Ratification: after signing, the deal needs European Parliament consent and Council qualified-majority approval, plus national ratification if it is classed as mixed — [Wikipedia: India-EU FTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93European_Union_Free_Trade_Agreement)
- Tariff schedules: product-level schedules and staging should be checked once the legal text is published — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/che/legal-updates/eu-india-fta-the-next-big-trade-shift)
- Castor oil: no source found that states castor oil's FTA staging.
- GSP:
  - Regulation (EU) 2025/1909 sets graduated sections for 2026-2028. For India, the excluded sections are S-5, S-6a, S-7a, S-7b, S-11a, S-13, S-14, S-15a, S-15b, S-16, S-17a and S-17b. The new 2026 additions are minerals (S-5), rubber (S-7b) and vehicles (S-17b) — [EC Access2Markets, 1 Dec 2025](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/gsp-system-updates-period-2026-2028)
  - The 2023-2025 India exclusions were S-6a (chemicals), S-7a (plastics), S-8b (leather articles) and S-11a (textiles) — [EC Access2Markets 2023 notice](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/suspension-year-2023-certain-tariff-preferences-granted-certain-gsp-beneficiary-countries)
  - **Section II (vegetable products, incl. spices Ch. 09, Ch. 13 extracts and Ch. 14 henna) and Section III (fats and oils, incl. castor oil) are NOT graduated for India.**
  - India's Commerce Ministry says the 2026 GSP change affects only about 2.66% of India's exports to the EU — [BusinessToday, 24 Jan 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/india-eu-summit-ahead-commerce-ministry-says-gsp-impact-limited-to-266-of-exports-not-87-512766-2026-01-24)
  - **Caveat:** HS 2508 (fuller's earth / multani mitti) falls in S-5 (mineral products), which is graduated for India from 2026. Since the MFN rate on most raw clays is believed to be 0% (unverified), the practical impact is probably nil.
- MFN duties: the tariff lookup was **not verified**, because search did not return TARIC/Access2Markets pages.
  - A tariff listing confirms CN 1515 30 10 (for aminoundecanoic acid production) and 1515 30 90 (other) as separate lines, and shows a GSP preference entry for 1515 30 90 — [tariffnumber.com](https://www.tariffnumber.com/2025/15153090)
  - From background knowledge (unverified): most single spices in Ch. 09 (turmeric, ginger, cinnamon, cardamom, dried chilli) carry 0% EU MFN duty. Castor oil 1515 30 90 is believed to carry about 5.1% MFN, reduced under standard GSP. Plant extracts 1302 19 and henna 1404 90 are believed to be 0%. Confirm every rate in [Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/home) for origin India.
  - A spice-exporter blog claims Indian spices faced 3-9% EU tariffs before the FTA. This is a non-official source and conflicts with the 0% MFN understanding for whole spices; it may refer to spice mixtures or processed lines — [Sadbhaav Spices blog](https://sadbhaavspices.com/how-india-eu-fta-benefits-spice-exporters/)

### Inferences
- In Oct 2026 the FTA gives **no tariff benefit yet**. Even if signed in Dec 2026, entry into force is unlikely before late 2027, given EP consent and possible national ratification. Marketing should say "FTA concluded Jan 2026, signing expected Dec 2026," not "duty-free under FTA."
- Because most raw spices are already at or near 0% MFN or GSP, the FTA's main value for this product line is likely on castor oil, spice mixtures (garam masala, curry powder), processed or prepared items such as tamarind paste, and possibly SPS cooperation, rather than single whole spices. This inference depends on the unverified MFN rates above.
- Non-tariff barriers matter more than tariffs for these products: pesticide MRLs, ethylene oxide, aflatoxin and Sudan-dye checks on Indian spices, which led to EU increased-control regimes in 2020-2024. These were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation of EU MFN or GSP rates for each CN line.
- No product-level FTA tariff schedule found, especially for castor oil and spice mixtures.
- Nothing found dated September or October 2026 confirming that the December signing target still holds. The latest status source is ANI on 20 Aug 2026; Borderlex on 14 Sep 2026 describes the deal content but not new timing.
