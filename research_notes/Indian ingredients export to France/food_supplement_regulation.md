# EU and French Regulatory Requirements for Importing Indian Spices and Botanical Food/Supplement Ingredients into France (as of October 2026)

Method note: research used web search only. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every domain tried (eur-lex.europa.eu, cbi.eu, indianspices.com, spicemodo.com), so primary texts could not be opened in full. Figures below come from search-result extracts of official documents (EUR-Lex, ANSES, Légifrance, data.gouv.fr) where possible, and from trade press otherwise. Items marked "background knowledge, not verified this session" are the researcher's prior knowledge and should be checked before use.

## 1. Importer responsibilities under EU General Food Law (Reg 178/2002), French registration, traceability, HACCP

### Takeaway
The person who imports into France and places the goods on the EU market is the food business operator (FBO) responsible for compliance. In France, food safety controls were consolidated under the Ministry of Agriculture (DGAL) through the "police unique" reform. Food supplement declarations moved from DGCCRF (Téléicare) to DGAL (Compl'Alim) in September 2025. No source found in this session describes a dedicated "importer registration" procedure, so registration requirements must be confirmed with the local DDPP.

### Cited Findings
- DDPPs (departmental directorates for population protection) carry out food safety policy under the prefect. DGAL agents inside DDPPs inspect food establishments, both approved and non-approved. Some DDPPs have a unit dedicated to port import activity for food, plants and feed. — [DDPP Hérault activity report 2023](https://www.herault.gouv.fr/contenu/telechargement/49421/368950/file/Bilan%20activit%C3%A9%20DDPP%202023.pdf)
- Customs acts on all food products at clearance. A 2021 customs document assigned import health controls on non-animal-origin food to DGCCRF, with files notified to the DDPP. This is likely superseded by the 2023 transfer to DGAL. — [Douane "transfert CCRF: ce qui change"](https://form.douane.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2021-10/26/transfert-ccrf-ce-qui-change-v2.pdf); [Assemblée nationale committee record](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/comptes-rendus/cesouvalim/l16cesouvalim2324024_compte-rendu.pdf)
- Food supplement declarations were handled by DGCCRF on Téléicare from 2016 to September 2025. Since September 2025 DGAL handles them on Compl'Alim. — [data.gouv.fr dataset "Déclarations de compléments alimentaires"](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/declarations-de-complements-alimentaires)
- The Téléicare procedure was to be decommissioned on 16 May 2025 and replaced by compl-alim.beta.gouv.fr. — [Actifs-Connect](https://actifs-connect.com/les-erreurs-a-eviter-lors-de-la-teledeclaration-dun-complement-alimentaire/). This date conflicts with the September 2025 date given by data.gouv.fr; the official dataset is more reliable.
- An establishment declaration (CERFA 13984) to the DDPP is required for establishments handling or storing animal-origin food. Spices and botanicals are not of animal origin, so the scope of this declaration for a spice importer needs confirming. — [DDCSPP Ardèche notice](https://www.ardeche.gouv.fr/contenu/telechargement/20588/172390/file/ddcspp_notice_declaration.pdf)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session: Reg 178/2002 Art. 17 makes the FBO responsible for compliance at every stage it controls. Art. 18 requires one-step-back/one-step-forward traceability. Art. 19 requires withdrawal/recall and notification to authorities when an unsafe food is identified. Reg 852/2004 Art. 5 requires HACCP-based procedures, and Art. 6 requires registration of every food establishment with the competent authority. In France this registration is usually done with the DDPP, now through the online declaration on mesdemarches.agriculture.gouv.fr.
- Background knowledge: the French importer of record, not the Indian family exporter, is the EU FBO who answers to DDPP/DGAL and DGCCRF. If the importer only trades (B2B, no storage), a lighter HACCP/food safety plan still applies, along with traceability records by lot.
- Practical point: EU buyers (manufacturers, supplement brands) will usually require IFS/BRC/FSSC 22000 or similar certification from the Indian site, plus a supplier questionnaire. This is commercial practice, not law.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for the exact French registration route for a non-animal food importer or trader in 2026. Confirm with the DDPP of the department (e.g., Hauts-de-Seine, Val-de-Marne, Seine-Saint-Denis).
- Full division of tasks after the "police unique" transfer (DGAL vs DGCCRF on import of non-animal food) was not confirmed with a primary source.

## 2. Official border controls: Reg (EU) 2019/1793 Indian entries, frequency, CHED-D/TRACES, certificates

### Takeaway
India has several entries in Reg 2019/1793, which is amended roughly every six months. The most recent confirmed amendment is Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/194 of 28 January 2026. It ended ethylene oxide (EtO) increased controls on Indian okra, vanilla and cloves, and moved the Indian Piper/Capsicum/Pimenta heading 0904 entry from Annex II (special conditions with certificate) to Annex I (increased checks at 20%). Cumin and okra from India were at 30% for pesticides under Reg 2024/3153. A trade blog reports a June 2026 amendment (2026/1206) that could not be verified. The current consolidated text must be checked before each shipment.

### Cited Findings
- Reg 2026/194 (28 January 2026) removed increased EtO controls on okra from India because "official controls ... indicate an overall satisfactory degree of compliance". It also ended increased EtO controls on vanilla and on cloves (whole fruit, cloves and stems) from India, which had applied since January 2022. — [EUR-Lex, Reg (EU) 2026/194](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32026R0194); [Spices Board copy](https://indianspices.com/indianspices/sites/default/files/Regulation_EU_2026_194.pdf)
- Reg 2026/194 states that the entry on "peppers of the genus Piper, dried or crushed or ground fruit of the genus Capsicum or of the genus Pimenta from India in point 1 of Annex II ... should be deleted and transferred to Annex I", with checks at 20%. — [EUR-Lex HTML OJ L 2026/194](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202600194). Because only search extracts were available, the hazard on the new Annex I entry (EtO, pesticides or both) was not confirmed. A trade blog describes it as a "general 0904 pesticide check at 20%".
- Reg 2024/3153 (18 December 2024) found a high rate of pesticide residue non-compliance for cumin and okra from India and raised identity and physical checks to 30%. — [EUR-Lex, Reg (EU) 2024/3153](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202403153); CBI says Indian cumin moved to 30% in January 2025 — [CBI buyer requirements, spices](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/spices-herbs/buyer-requirements)
- 2022 change: no EtO was detected in Indian spices such as turmeric root, so those were excluded from increased controls. Only some dried spices from India stayed at 20%, and the official certificate for every consignment of Indian nutmeg was no longer needed. — [Food Safety News, July 2022](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2022/07/eu-import-control-changes-mention-ethylene-oxide-and-aflatoxins/)
- The original 2019 annex listed curry leaves (Bergera/Murraya koenigii, ex 1211 90 86) from India for pesticide residues at a 10% check rate. — [Reg 2019/1793 original text (copy)](https://www.shefexil.org/upload/EU-Commission-Regulation-1793%20dtd.pdf). The 50% rate for Indian curry leaves found in searches is a UK rule (Welsh SI 2024/1214), not EU. — [Senedd, Welsh SI 2024 No. 1214](https://senedd.wales/media/cakk013r/sub-ld16820-e.pdf); [Spices Board circular 17/2024](https://www.indianspices.com/indianspices/sites/default/files/Circular%2017_2024_03_16.pdf)
- The original 2019 annex listed dried, roasted, crushed or ground Capsicum from India (0904 21 10) for aflatoxins at 20%. — [Reg 2019/1793 original text (copy)](https://www.shefexil.org/upload/EU-Commission-Regulation-1793%20dtd.pdf)
- Unverified: trade blog spicemodo.com describes an "Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1206 of 9 June 2026". It says this deleted the aflatoxin entry for Indian Capsicum (leaving the 20% 0904 pesticide check), moved cinnamon to Annex I, and kept turmeric in Annex II at 20%. It also says the UK has not followed. Searches did not find 2026/1206 on EUR-Lex. — [Spicemodo, "EU Spice Import Rules 2026: What Changed in June"](https://spicemodo.com/blog/eu-spice-import-rules-2026.html)
- A Spices Board advisory mentioning nutmeg, mace and cardamom at 50% concerns Great Britain's rules, not the EU's. — [Spices Board trade advisory](https://indianspices.com/indianspices/sites/default/files/Trade%20advisory_compressed.pdf)
- An AGRINFO analysis of the impacts of Reg 2019/1793 exists and may help with frequency trends. — [AGRINFO, Impacts of Reg 2019/1793](https://agrinfo.eu/documents/136/Impacts-Reg-2019-1793-quantitative-PTalks-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session, on procedure. For goods in Annex I or II, the importer (or customs broker) must pre-notify each consignment in IMSOC/TRACES NT with a CHED-D (Common Health Entry Document for food of non-animal origin), at least one working day before arrival. Goods must enter through a designated border control post (BCP), such as Roissy-CDG, Le Havre or Marseille-Fos. Customs will not release goods without a validated CHED-D. Annex II goods also need an official certificate from the Indian authority (Export Inspection Council/Spices Board) and laboratory results from an accredited lab for each consignment. Checks: documentary on 100%, identity and physical (sampling) at the listed frequency. The importer pays for controls and lab analyses, and the goods are held until results are available when sampled.
- Product mapping (needs confirming against the current consolidated text): chilli (0904 21/22) is likely affected by the Piper/Capsicum/Pimenta entry (20%). Curry leaves may still be listed for pesticides. Turmeric's status conflicts between sources (excluded from EtO controls in 2022 per Food Safety News, but listed in Annex II at 20% per an unverified blog). Ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, tamarind, moringa, ashwagandha, amla, triphala, shilajit, noni and black cumin seed oil were not identified in any India entry in this research. Mixed spices such as garam masala may fall under the 0910 91 "mixtures" heading if listed. That is not confirmed.
- Products not listed in 2019/1793 still face random official controls under Reg 2017/625 and customs risk profiling.

### Gaps
- The current (October 2026) consolidated Annex I/II text could not be opened. Exact India entries, hazards and percentages for chilli, curry leaves, turmeric, garam masala/mixtures and cinnamon need confirming on EUR-Lex (search "02019R1793").
- The existence and content of "2026/1206" are unverified.

## 3. Ethylene oxide crisis, current rules, RASFF counts for Indian spices 2023–2025, MDH/Everest

### Takeaway
EtO alerts on Indian products peaked in 2021 and have since fallen sharply. India nonetheless remains the largest single origin of EtO notifications in RASFF. The 2024 MDH/Everest affair started in Singapore and Hong Kong. No EU RASFF notification naming those brands was found.

### Cited Findings
- EU RASFF EtO notifications fell from 345 in 2021 to 25 in the "last 12 months". India was the largest origin: 9 of 25 in the last year and 85 of 144 since 2023. Recent notifications include garam masala, biryani masala, chilli powder, black pepper and cumin from India. — [RASFF tracker (third-party analysis), "Ethylene oxide five years on"](https://rasff.fruit-risk.com/findings/ethylene-oxide-five-years-on.html). This is a third-party analysis whose exact time window was unclear. Verify on the RASFF Window.
- An EFSA monitoring figure cited in commentary says that out of 47 samples, the MRL was exceeded in 2.3%, including six samples of Curcuma from India and five of chilli peppers from India and Uganda. — [Public Policy Substack (secondary)](https://publicpolicy.substack.com/p/253-a-puzzle-called-regulation?open=false). This is a secondary source and context is unclear.
- In April 2024 Singapore's SFA and Hong Kong's CFS withdrew MDH and Everest curry powders for EtO. Maldives and Nepal followed. — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDH_and_Everest_global_safety_concerns); [Indian Diaspora news](https://www.indiandiaspora.org/news/indian-spice-brands-mdh-and-everest-face-international-bans-and-investigations)
- FSSAI tested about 3,245 samples and reportedly found no EtO in MDH/Everest samples. This was reported from unnamed sources while some lab reports were pending. — [Business Today, 22 May 2024](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/fssai-found-no-trace-of-ethylene-oxide-in-sample-spices-of-mdh-and-everest-says-official-430462-2024-05-22); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/fssai-finds-no-trace-of-ethylene-oxide-in-samples-of-mdh-everest-spices-in-28-lab-reports-3033032)
- Indian officials reported a "drastic decline" in EtO alerts on Indian exports to the EU. — [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/ensure-compliance-with-norms-govt-tells-everest-mdh-inspects-facilities-623215)
- Many Indian organic consignments contaminated with EtO were the basis for the EU's delisting of Indian organic control bodies (see section 6). — [EUR-Lex Reg 2021/2325 (ELI)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/2325/oj)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session: the EU MRL for EtO is the sum of EtO and 2-chloroethanol expressed as EtO. For spices it is set at the limit of quantification (about 0.1 mg/kg for spices, 0.02–0.05 mg/kg for many other foods) under Reg 396/2005 as amended by Reg 2015/868. EtO is not approved as a fumigant in the EU. The 2021 Member State position was "zero tolerance": products with any detectable EtO in an ingredient must be withdrawn and recalled, even when the final product is below the limit. This was codified for food additives by Reg 2022/1396. French authorities (DGCCRF/DGAL) ran large recall campaigns in 2021. Steam sterilisation (rather than EtO fumigation) is the accepted alternative, and buyers typically demand EtO/2-CE test reports per lot.
- Common RASFF hazards for Indian spices, background knowledge consistent with the sources above: pesticide residues (chlorpyrifos, profenofos, etc.), EtO, aflatoxins (chilli, nutmeg), Salmonella (sesame, black pepper, coriander), and less often Sudan dyes or lead.

### Gaps
- Official RASFF counts for Indian spices in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (all hazards) were not obtained because the RASFF Window could not be fetched. Run a RASFF Window search with product category "herbs and spices", origin India, by year.
- No EU-specific RASFF notification naming MDH/Everest was found.

## 4. Contaminant limits: Reg 2023/915 (aflatoxins, OTA, PAH, lead), Sudan dyes/lead chromate, MRLs (Reg 396/2005)

### Takeaway
Reg 2023/915, which replaced Reg 1881/2006 from 25 May 2023, sets the maximum levels for mycotoxins and metals in spices. OTA is 15 µg/kg for most dried spices. Aflatoxin limits for spices were seen in the consolidated text with "10.0" (total) and specific exceptions, but the exact rows could not be confirmed. Use the consolidated version (latest seen: 01.01.2025, amended e.g. by 2024/1756).

### Cited Findings
- OTA: 15 µg/kg in dried spices and 10 µg/kg in dried herbs. — [Care-Europe blog](https://care-europe.com/blog/mycotoxins-herbal-ingredients-eu-regulation-2023-915-export/); [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/eu-regulations-on-food-contaminants/). These are secondary sources. Primary: [Reg 2023/915 consolidated, 01.01.2025](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02023R0915-20250101)
- The consolidated Reg 2023/915 contains a dried-spices row with values 10.0 and 50.0 and the exception "except cardamom and smoked Capsicum spp.". Column context was not visible. — [EUR-Lex Reg 2023/915](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/915/oj/eng)
- Reg 2023/915 has been amended since adoption (e.g., Reg 2024/1756 amended or added certain maximum levels). — [Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/8573-new-eu-maximum-levels-for-contaminants-in-foods-aflatoxins-metals-and-more); [Eurofins](https://www.eurofins.com/en-de/food-testing/news/contaminants-regulation-eu-2023-915/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session; check against the consolidated Reg 2023/915:
  - Aflatoxins in spices (Capsicum, Piper, Myristica, Zingiber, Curcuma and mixtures): AFB1 5 µg/kg, sum B1+B2+G1+G2 10 µg/kg.
  - OTA: Capsicum spp. 20 µg/kg; other spices (including Piper, Myristica, Zingiber, Curcuma) 15 µg/kg. Liquorice also has specific limits.
  - Lead: a limit for spices was introduced by Reg 2021/1317 (applied from 2022), with different values for fruit spices, root/rhizome spices (e.g., turmeric, ginger: about 1.5 mg/kg), bark spices, seed spices and dried herbs. Food supplements have their own limits: lead 3.0 mg/kg, cadmium 1.0 mg/kg, mercury 0.10 mg/kg. These matter for shilajit, triphala and other Ayurvedic powders, which are prone to heavy metals.
  - PAH (benzo[a]pyrene and the sum of 4 PAH) limits apply to food supplements containing botanicals and propolis, and to smoked products. They matter for extracts and oils.
- Background knowledge: Sudan I–IV and Para Red are unauthorised colours, so any detection is non-compliant. The EU imposed emergency measures on Indian chilli and turmeric (Decision 2005/402/EC), later lifted. Lead chromate adulteration of turmeric (documented in South Asia, mainly Bangladesh) is a known risk. Buyers commonly require Sudan dye and lead testing on turmeric and chilli.
- Background knowledge: pesticide MRLs come from Reg 396/2005. Spices sit in Annex I part B (code 0820000 onward), and default MRLs of 0.01 mg/kg apply where no specific MRL exists. Processing factors may apply to extracts. Black cumin (Nigella) seed oil and botanical extracts are measured against the raw agricultural commodity with processing factors.
- Microbiology: there is no EU legal Salmonella criterion for spices under Reg 2073/2005. Salmonella in spices is nevertheless treated as unsafe under Art. 14 of Reg 178/2002 and is a frequent RASFF hazard. The ESA Quality Minima Document (industry standard) sets buyer expectations on Salmonella absence, moisture, ash and extraneous matter.
- Pyrrolizidine alkaloid limits in Reg 2023/915 (herbal infusions, cumin seeds, dried herbs, food supplements) may affect cumin, tea blends and botanical supplements.

### Gaps
- Exact current Reg 2023/915 values (aflatoxin rows for spices, lead in spices by category, PAH, PA in cumin) could not be confirmed from the primary text because the fetch failed.
- The ESA Quality Minima Document could not be retrieved.

## 5. Novel Food status and French/EU national positions: ashwagandha, shilajit, noni, moringa, curcumin, amla, triphala; turmeric hepatotoxicity; Denmark ban; Arrêté 24 June 2014; Compl'Alim declaration

### Takeaway
Most of the botanicals are traditional, but status depends on the plant part and form. Shilajit (mumijo) is "not novel" only in food supplements. Ashwagandha root is the accepted form while leaf is contested and risky. High-purity curcumin extracts have been treated as novel by Italy. France allows ashwagandha but ANSES (2024) advises against it for several groups. ANSES (2022) set a curcumin limit of 153 mg/day for supplements. Denmark banned ashwagandha in 2023. By July 2026 an EU-level Article 8 review of ashwagandha was still under consideration, not launched.

### Cited Findings
**Novel Food catalogue (general)**
- The Commission's Novel Food status Catalogue is non-binding and non-exhaustive. Its revised version has applied since 16 November 2023. The FBO must prove significant consumption in the EU before 15 May 1997. — [European Commission Novel Food status Catalogue](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/novel-food-status-catalogue_en)

**Shilajit (mumijo)**
- A Commission consultation on novel food status concluded that mumijo is not novel in food supplements, but other food uses may be novel. — [EC consultation document, mumijo](https://food.ec.europa.eu/document/download/f551e7aa-8128-49bc-be3e-36e35b82eae0_en?filename=novel-food_consult-status_mumijo.pdf); [Rosapharma, March 2025 NF updates](https://rosapharma.pt/novel-food-europe-updates-summary-from-march-2025/)

**Moringa**
- Moringa leaf is reported as "not novel" in the catalogue. — [Manubolu (German importer guide, secondary)](https://manubolu.de/blog/novel-food-regulation-eu.html). Not confirmed against the catalogue.

**Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)**
- A secondary source says the root has the accepted status and that the leaf "is not authorised and cannot be sold as food in the EU". — [Manubolu](https://manubolu.de/blog/novel-food-regulation-eu.html). Unverified.
- Industry criticises the Danish assessment for not distinguishing root and leaf. — [Vitafoods Insights](https://www.vitafoodsinsights.com/botanicals-herbs/-incorrect-and-misleading-industry-defends-ashwagandha-root-extract-safety-amid-danish-assessment)
- ANSES opinion "relatif aux risques liés à l'utilisation des préparations de Withania somnifera (L.) Dunal dans les compléments alimentaires" (referral 2021-SA-0077), reportedly dated 19 April 2024 and published June 2024. It recommends against use by pregnant or breastfeeding women, under-18s, people with thyroid, liver or heart conditions, and people on sedatives or CNS-acting drugs. It followed 8 adverse-effect cases. It did not ban the plant or set a maximum daily dose. — [La Compagnie des Sens (secondary)](https://www.compagnie-des-sens.fr/ashwagandha-interdit-france/); [Nutriradio](https://nutriradio.fr/news/prudence-recommandee-par-l-anses-sur-l-utilisation-de-l-ashwagandha-538). Verify on anses.fr.
- Denmark: Fødevarestyrelsen banned ashwagandha (root and extracts) from April 2023, based on a 2020 DTU risk assessment that could not set a safe intake level. — [McGill OSS](https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/critical-thinking-health-and-nutrition/why-did-denmark-ban-ashwagandha); [Food Law Consult, July 2023](https://foodlawconsult.com/2023/07/18/ashwaganda-prohibited/). A 2025 proposal to codify the ban in Danish legislation is reported but not confirmed as enacted. — [Phytotherapy Research regulatory review, 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.70151?af=R)
- Poland (2020) allows only root, up to 3 g and up to 10 mg withanolides per day, and not for children or pregnant/breastfeeding women. Sweden lets municipalities decide case by case. The Netherlands and France advise against use by certain groups. — [Sagentia](https://sagentia.com/blog/navigating-ashwagandha-balancing-safety-consumer-demand-and-regulatory-concerns/); [Phytotherapy Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ptr.70151?af=R)
- The Heads of Agencies (HoA) Food Supplements working group (June 2024) recommended Article 8 (Reg 1925/2006) procedures and placing the root in Annex III List C (community scrutiny). — [BVL, first report of HoA WG Food Supplements, 6 June 2024](https://www.bvl.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/01_Lebensmittel/Internationales/report_HoA_WG_FS-en.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=6)
- In August 2024 EFSA had received no mandate on W. somnifera. — [Nutrition Insight](https://www.nutritioninsight.com/news/regulatory-update-spotlight-on-international-authorities-response-to-ashwagandha-safety-concerns.html)
- July 2026: at a 14 July meeting in Brussels, regulators were "still considering" an Article 8 review of ashwagandha. Industry is preparing safety data. — [NutraIngredients, 16 July 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/16/ashwagandha-armed-with-critical-safety-data-as-industry-prepares-for-europe-wide-assessment/)
- The UK FSA published a summary of responses to its ashwagandha call for evidence on 25 June 2026, for the Committee on Toxicity. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/summary-of-responses-to-call-for-evidence-on-ashwagandha)

**Turmeric / curcumin**
- ANSES nutrivigilance: 15 hepatic adverse-effect cases possibly linked to turmeric/curcumin supplements since 2009, one life-threatening. Italy recorded about 20 hepatitis cases between November 2018 and June 2019. The French and Italian systems together recorded more than 40 hepatitis cases in ten years. — [Vigil'Anses n°17, June 2022](https://vigilanses.anses.fr/sites/default/files/VigilAnsesN17_Juin2022_Curcuma_0.pdf); [HAL record](https://anses.hal.science/VIGIL_ANSES/hal-03753488v1)
- ANSES recommends that curcumin intake from supplements stay below 153 mg/day for a 60 kg adult. EFSA's ADI for curcumin (E100) is 3 mg/kg bw, about 180 mg/day at 60 kg. ANSES advises against use for people with biliary disease and those on anticoagulants, anticancer drugs or immunosuppressants. It flags bioavailability-enhanced formulations (reportedly 4–185 times more bioavailable). — [Hépatites Info Service](https://www.hepatites-info-service.org/curcuma-et-hepatites-vigilance/); [Naturenergie (secondary, cites NUT2019SA0111)](https://naturenergie.fr/posts/curcuma-anses-effets-indesirables-hepatite-complement). The opinion date is given as 27 June 2022 by one source and May 2022 by another.
- The Italian Ministry of Health is reported to consider turmeric extracts with up to 95% curcumin as Novel Food. "Curcumin in Curcuma preparations" is on the HoA list of 13 substances of concern. — [NutraIngredients, June 2024](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/06/13/European-food-safety-report-taking-pot-shots-at-popular-botanicals/); [Label-bank newsletter](https://label-bank.com/blog/foodlabel/202408wgfs)
- UK COT has issued a statement on turmeric and curcumin supplements. — [COT](https://cot.food.gov.uk/print/pdf/node/13066)

**France: plant list and declaration**
- The Arrêté of 24 June 2014 lists the plants (other than fungi) authorised in food supplements and their conditions of use (Annex I). It reproduces plants accepted via mutual recognition from 2006 to 2010. — [Légifrance, Arrêté du 24 juin 2014](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000029254516); [DGCCRF reading guide](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/files/files/directions_services/dgccrf/securite/produits_alimentaires/Complement_alimentaire/CA-Plantes-ArrPP-Guide-consultation.pdf)
- Declaration under Decree 2006-352: Article 15 applies to products whose ingredients are authorised in France. Article 16 (mutual recognition) applies when the product contains at least one plant or substance not authorised in France. DGAL has 2 months to examine a declaration, after which an attestation is issued. DGAL confirmed that the Article 15 lists remain in force. — [Synadiet](https://www.synadiet.org/les-complements-alimentaires/reglementation/); [Actifs-Connect](https://actifs-connect.com/les-erreurs-a-eviter-lors-de-la-teledeclaration-dun-complement-alimentaire/); [entreprises.gouv.fr](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/espace-entreprises/s-informer-sur-la-reglementation/complements-alimentaires); [agriculture.gouv.fr](https://agriculture.gouv.fr/quest-ce-quun-complement-alimentaire)
- Since September 2025 declarations are made on Compl'Alim (DGAL). — [data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/declarations-de-complements-alimentaires)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session:
  - Noni (Morinda citrifolia): fruit juice was authorised as a novel food (Decision 2003/426/EC), and later fruit puree/concentrate, leaves (dried, for infusion) and fruit powder received authorisations. These now appear in the Union list (Reg 2017/2470) with specifications. Noni forms outside the list (e.g., some extracts or seed products) need a Novel Food check.
  - Moringa oleifera leaf is commonly treated as not novel. Moringa seed or other parts may differ.
  - Amla (Phyllanthus emblica fruit) and the triphala components (Terminalia chebula, T. bellirica) are believed to appear on the French 2014 plant list and/or the BELFRIT list. Check Annex I of the Arrêté for each species and part, plus any restrictions.
  - Withania somnifera root is on the French plant list (with warnings). Nigella sativa seed is a traditional food, and its cold-pressed oil is generally not novel.
  - The status of high-concentration curcumin (95%) in foods other than supplements, and of novel formulations (e.g., micellar or phospholipid complexes), is uncertain. Some formulations have been assessed as novel.
  - France's DGCCRF/DGAL has issued restrictions or warnings on plants via the plant list conditions (e.g., required warnings). For ashwagandha, French practice after the ANSES opinion is to expect warning statements on labels for the at-risk groups.
- Ashwagandha extracts standardised to 2.5–5% withanolides are root extracts in most commercial cases. Leaf or "whole plant" extracts carry the highest regulatory risk (Novel Food status, Danish and HoA concerns). Sell root-only with documentation (botanical identity, plant part, extraction solvent, withanolide profile including withaferin A).
- Selling to Denmark is not possible for ashwagandha. In other Member States, national lists such as Belgium's and Italy's apply. An EU-wide Article 8 restriction could still come and should be monitored.

### Gaps

- The full text of the ANSES 2024 ashwagandha opinion (2021-SA-0077) and the 2022 curcuma opinion were not retrieved. Dates and recommendations come from secondary sources.
- Whether the Danish ban was codified in 2025, and current Swedish, Finnish and Dutch status, were not confirmed.
- Whether each product appears on the Arrêté 2014 Annex I list (with conditions) was not checked.

## 6. Organic: Reg (EU) 2018/848, Indian control bodies, COI in TRACES, fraud

### Takeaway
India was an "equivalent" third country under the old regime (unprocessed plant products). The EU repeatedly delisted Indian control bodies over EtO-contaminated organic consignments. In May 2025 it recorded APEDA's suspension of two bodies and added new ones. Organic imports still need an electronic Certificate of Inspection (COI) in TRACES. The importer must check which bodies are recognised on the shipment date.

### Cited Findings
- Reg 2021/2325 removed CU Inspections India, Ecocert India, Indocert, Lacon Quality Certifications and OneCert International from the list of control bodies recognised by India. The EU cited many EtO-contaminated organic consignments from India certified by them. — [EUR-Lex Reg 2021/2325](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2021/2325/oj)
- Reg 2022/2049 removed TQ Cert Services Private Limited after India suspended it. — [EUR-Lex Reg 2022/2049](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2022/2049/oj/eng)
- Reg 2025/883 (14 May 2025) recorded APEDA's suspension of the Sikkim State Organic Certification Agency (IN-ORG-028) and Reliable Organic Certification Organisation (IN-ORG-033). It listed ISCOP (IN-ORG-009), CGOCERT (IN-ORG-035), GSCI Service (IN-ORG-036), MS Agroland (IN-ORG-037), Krushi Certification (IN-ORG-038) and CERT ID India (IN-ORG-039). — [EUR-Lex Reg 2025/883](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/883/oj/eng); [IOAS copy](https://ioas.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/COMMISSION-IMPLEMENTING-REGULATIONEU-2025883-of-14-May-2025.pdf)
- A Commission draft (C(2025)2051) said the old Annex II list of control bodies under Reg 2021/2325 is obsolete because Article 2 applied until 31 December 2024. — [EUR-Lex PI_COM C(2025)2051](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=PI_COM:C(2025)2051)
- Earlier press reported the EC's suspension of five Indian agencies for processed products. — [Krishi Jagran](https://krishijagran.com/agriculture-world/european-commission-to-suspend-five-organic-certification-agencies-from-clearing-exports-of-processed-products-from-india/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session: under Reg 2018/848, recognitions of third countries under old equivalence agreements expire by 31 December 2026 unless trade agreements are concluded. India's equivalence (Reg 2021/2325 Annex I) covered unprocessed plant products and processed plant products for food, and was limited to products produced in India. An EU–India agreement status is unknown. If India's recognition lapses at end-2026 without an agreement, Indian organic exports would need certification by EU-recognised control bodies (Annex II of Reg 2021/1378 under "compliance" rules), such as Ecocert, Control Union or Kiwa, which certify Indian operators directly. This is a major 2026–2027 change to verify.
- Every organic consignment needs an electronic COI issued in TRACES by the exporter's control body and endorsed by the Member State authority before release for free circulation. Since Reg 2021/2306, the first consignee also checks it. Products may face EtO and pesticide residue testing, and residue findings trigger OFIS investigations. In France, the importer must be certified by a French organic control body (e.g., Ecocert France, Bureau Veritas, Certipaq) and notified to Agence BIO.

### Gaps
- The status after December 2026 of India's equivalence recognition, and the current list of recognised Indian control bodies (late 2025–2026 amendments), were not confirmed.
- Recent organic fraud case numbers involving Indian products (OFIS statistics) were not found.

## 7. Labelling and claims: Reg 1169/2011, French language, allergens, lot, origin; Reg 1924/2006 and botanical "on hold" claims

### Takeaway
Nothing new was retrieved this session on labelling. The rules below are background knowledge and should be validated. The main pitfalls are French-language labelling, undeclared mustard or celery in spice mixes, missing lot numbers, unauthorised health claims on botanicals, and supplement-specific labelling under Directive 2002/46/EC and Decree 2006-352.

### Cited Findings
- France requires supplements to be declared and to comply with the Decree 2006-352 framework (labelling includes the daily dose, warnings, and the statement that supplements do not replace a varied diet). — [entreprises.gouv.fr](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/espace-entreprises/s-informer-sur-la-reglementation/complements-alimentaires); [Synadiet](https://www.synadiet.org/les-complements-alimentaires/reglementation/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not verified this session:
  - Reg 1169/2011 Art. 9 mandatory particulars: name, ingredients, allergens emphasised (Annex II includes mustard and celery, both common in garam masala and curry powder, and sesame and nuts as possible cross-contaminants), net quantity, date of minimum durability, storage conditions, name and address of the EU FBO, origin where its omission could mislead (and Art. 26(3)/Reg 2018/775 for the primary ingredient), and a nutrition declaration (spices and herbs are exempt under Annex V). Directive 2011/91/EU requires a lot number.
  - French language: the Code de la consommation (Art. L.112-8 and Loi Toubon 94-665) requires French on labels of products sold in France. B2B bulk deliveries may carry the particulars in commercial documents (Art. 8(7) of Reg 1169/2011).
  - Claims: Reg 1924/2006 allows only authorised claims (Reg 432/2012 list). About 2,000 botanical health claims have been "on hold" since 2010. Under transitional Art. 28(5)–(6) they may be used, under national rules and at the operator's risk, if they comply with the Regulation. French DGCCRF accepts on-hold claims in practice, provided they are substantiated. Claims such as "reduces stress" (ashwagandha) or "anti-inflammatory" (turmeric) are risky. Medicinal claims (treat or cure) are prohibited and can lead to reclassification as an unauthorised medicine by ANSM.
  - Supplements (Directive 2002/46/EC, Decree 2006-352): the label needs the term "complément alimentaire", the names of the nutrient or substance categories, the recommended daily dose, the warning not to exceed it, the keep-out-of-reach-of-children warning, and the statement that supplements should not replace a varied diet. Plant-specific warnings required by the Arrêté 2014 must also appear (e.g., for curcuma and ashwagandha, warnings for pregnant women, people with biliary disease or on anticoagulants).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 DGCCRF enforcement statistics (e.g., food supplement or spice survey results, non-compliance rates) were retrieved.
- Whether DGAL or DGCCRF published updated botanical warning requirements (e.g., an amended Arrêté 2014 after the ANSES 2024 ashwagandha opinion) was not found.
